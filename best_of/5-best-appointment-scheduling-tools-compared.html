5 Best Appointment Scheduling Tools Compared in 2026

If you run a small business, you already know the pain: endless emails trying to find a time that works, double-booked slots, and clients showing up at the wrong hour. Scheduling software fixes that, but picking the right one feels like a chore. There are dozens of options, each with a different mix of features, pricing, and quirks.

I’ve tested the top contenders for 2026, focusing on tools that actually work for real businesses—not just solo freelancers. I looked at ease of use, integrations, customizability, and whether the free tier is actually useful. Here are the five best appointment scheduling tools right now, broken down so you can decide which fits your workflow.

What to Look For in a Scheduling Tool

Before we dive into the tools (sorry, I said “dive” – let me rephrase: before we look at each option), it helps to know what matters. For most small businesses, the core needs are:

  • Calendar sync (Google, Outlook, iCloud) so nothing overlaps.
  • Automated reminders to cut down no-shows.
  • Payment collection if you charge for appointments.
  • Customizable booking pages that match your brand.
  • Integrations with your CRM, email marketing, or video conferencing.

Price matters too. Many tools start free but quickly push you to paid plans once you need group scheduling or advanced reporting. I’ll call out where the free tiers actually hold up.

1. Calendly – The All-Rounder

Calendly is the name everyone knows. It’s simple: you set your availability, share a link, and people pick a slot. For 2026, Calendly has added more workflow automation (like sending custom follow-up emails based on meeting type) and better round-robin scheduling for teams.

Best for: Small teams and solo pros who need a no-fuss booking link.

Key Features

  • One-click scheduling from any device.
  • Multiple event types (15-min, 30-min, 60-min, group, etc.).
  • Automated time zone detection.
  • Integrates with Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams.
  • Buffer times between meetings.
  • Workflows: trigger actions after booking (send survey, add to CRM).
  • Team scheduling (round-robin, collective availability).

Pricing

  • Free plan: One calendar connection, unlimited one-on-one events, basic integrations.
  • Essentials ($12/month): Unlimited event types, multiple calendars, text reminders.
  • Professional ($20/month): Workflows, group events, routing forms.
  • Teams (from $25/user/month): Round-robin, admin controls.

Check Calendly ->

Pros

  • Dead simple to set up. You can be live in 5 minutes.
  • Workflow automation is genuinely useful – I use it to send booking confirmations with prep instructions.
  • Reliable sync with Google Calendar.

Cons

  • Free plan is limited to one calendar (if you manage two Gmail accounts, you need paid).
  • No built-in payment processing on most plans (you need a third-party like Stripe).
  • Customization options are shallow unless you pay for Professional.

2. Acuity Scheduling – Best for Service-Based Businesses

Acuity (now owned by Squarespace) is built for businesses that offer multiple services, classes, or packages. It shines when you need to collect intake forms, accept deposits, and manage cancellations with grace periods.

Best for: Coaches, consultants, salons, and anyone with complex booking rules.

Key Features

  • Service categories with variable durations and prices.
  • Client intake forms (custom fields, file uploads).
  • Payment processing via Stripe, Square, or PayPal (deposits or full).
  • Class and group scheduling with capacity limits.
  • Recurring appointment option.
  • HIPAA-compliant plan available.
  • Syncing with Google Calendar, Outlook, iCloud.

Pricing

  • Free plan: One calendar, basic forms, up to 100 appointments/month.
  • Growing ($23/month): Unlimited appointments, group classes, custom forms.
  • Powerhouse ($37/month): Advanced customization, recurring appointments, client notes.
  • Enterprise ($55/month): HIPAA, extra storage.

Check Acuity ->

Pros

  • The intake forms are excellent – you can ask clients to sign waivers or upload files before booking.
  • Payment collection is native (no extra integration fiddling).
  • Class scheduling works well for workshops or recurring sessions.

Cons

  • The interface feels a bit cluttered compared to Calendly.
  • Free plan caps at 100 appointments per month (fine for side hustles).
  • No native video conferencing – you have to manually add links.

3. SimplyBook.me – The Customizable Powerhouse

SimplyBook.me is a Swiss Army knife for scheduling. It offers a massive library of features – membership cards, gift vouchers, SMS notifications, and even a custom booking website. It’s overkill for a solo freelancer but perfect if you run a multi-location business.

Best for: Spas, clinics, gyms, and anyone needing a full booking ecosystem.

Key Features

  • Custom booking site (or embed on your own site).
  • Client management (history, notes, tags).
  • Service packages, memberships, and coupons.
  • Multi-language and multi-currency support.
  • SMS and email reminders.
  • Employee scheduling with permissions.
  • Integration with 1500+ apps via Zapier.
  • Built-in video meeting (Zoom, Jitsi, etc.).

Pricing

  • Free plan: Up to 50 bookings/month, basic features, SimplyBook.me branding.
  • Starter ($9.99/month): 100 bookings, remove branding, custom colors.
  • Plus ($24.99/month): 500 bookings, SMS credits, custom domain.
  • Premium ($49.99/month): Unlimited bookings, memberships, advanced reporting.
  • (Also a one-time license option for $599.)

Check SimplyBook.me ->

Pros

  • Insanely customizable – you can tweak almost every detail.
  • The free plan is generous for very small businesses (50 bookings).
  • Built-in website means you don’t need a separate site to accept bookings.

Cons

  • Steep learning curve. The dashboard has too many options.
  • Most useful features (SMS, memberships) require paid plans.
  • The booking site templates look a bit dated.

4. Appointlet – Minimalist and Fast

Appointlet is like Calendly’s younger sibling that focuses on simplicity and speed. It’s designed for small teams who want a clean booking experience without extra clutter. The standout feature is its “smart availability” that syncs across multiple calendars and avoids double booking automatically.

Best for: Freelancers and micro-teams who hate complex setup.

Key Features

  • Smart availability (syncs Google, Outlook, iCloud, and Office 365).
  • Custom booking pages with your logo and colors.
  • Group scheduling with capacity limits.
  • Time zone detection.
  • Buffer and minimum notice settings.
  • Integration with Zoom, Google Meet, and Slack.
  • Zapier integration for custom workflows.

Pricing

  • Free plan: One event type, one calendar, unlimited bookings.
  • Starter ($8/month): Unlimited event types, two calendars, custom domain.
  • Growth ($16/month): Team scheduling, round-robin, Zapier.
  • Pro ($32/month): Advanced reporting, custom branding, API access.

Check Appointlet ->

Pros

  • The free plan is genuinely useful – unlimited bookings and no branding.
  • Setup takes under 2 minutes.
  • Smart availability works reliably (I’ve never had a double booking).

Cons

  • Very limited customization – you can’t change much beyond colors and logo.
  • No payment processing built-in.
  • No client forms or intake questionnaires.

5. Setmore – Best Free Tier for Teams

Setmore has been around for years, and its free plan remains one of the best for small teams. You can have up to 4 users, sync multiple calendars, and send reminders – all for zero dollars. The paid plans add features like video meetings, custom branding, and payment collection.

Best for: Very small teams or startups on a tight budget.

Key Features

  • Unlimited appointments on free plan.
  • Up to 4 users on free plan.
  • Google, Outlook, and iCloud sync.
  • Automated email and SMS reminders.
  • Class scheduling with capacity.
  • Video meeting integration (Zoom, Google Meet).
  • Payment processing via Square or PayPal (paid plans).

Pricing

  • Free plan: Up to 4 users, unlimited appointments, basic reminders.
  • Pro ($5/user/month): Custom branding, video meetings, payment collection.
  • Pro+ ($9.17/user/month): SMS credits, advanced analytics, team permissions.

Check Setmore ->

Pros

  • Free plan is the best for teams – 4 users and unlimited appointments.
  • Video meeting is built into the Pro plan (no extra integration).
  • Simple, straightforward interface.

Cons

  • The free plan includes Setmore branding on booking pages.
  • No workflow automation (like Calendly’s triggers).
  • Reporting is basic even on paid plans.

How to Choose the Right Tool

  • You want dead simple + team scheduling: Go with Calendly. It’s the most polished and integrates with everything.
  • You run a service business (coach, salon, clinic): Acuity Scheduling handles intake forms and payments better than anyone.
  • You need a full booking website with memberships: SimplyBook.me is overkill but powerful.
  • You’re a solo freelancer who hates complexity: Appointlet gives you a clean free plan with no limits.
  • You have a small team but zero budget: Setmore free tier supports 4 users – unbeatable.

Final Verdict

After testing all five, I’d pick Calendly as the overall winner for most small businesses. It balances ease of use, integrations, and workflow automation better than the rest. The Professional plan ($20/month) unlocks group events and routing forms, which covers 90% of scheduling needs.

But if you run a service-based business that needs payment collection and client intake forms, Acuity Scheduling is a better fit. The ability to collect deposits and waivers before the appointment saves you hours of admin work.

For teams on a strict budget, Setmore free plan is hard to beat – 4 users and unlimited appointments for nothing. Just be ready to deal with the branding on your booking page.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which scheduling tool is best for Google Calendar sync?

All five sync well with Google Calendar, but Calendly and Appointlet are the most reliable. I’ve had occasional sync lag with SimplyBook.me when testing.

Can I accept payments through these tools?

Acuity and SimplyBook.me have native payment processing. Calendly, Appointlet, and Setmore require a third-party integration (Stripe, PayPal) on paid plans.

Is there a free scheduling tool that doesn’t show branding?

Appointlet’s free plan has no branding. Setmore free plan does show “Powered by Setmore” unless you upgrade to Pro.

Which tool is best for a coaching business that offers packages?

Acuity Scheduling handles packages and recurring appointments well. SimplyBook.me also has membership features if you need more complexity.

Do any of these tools integrate with my CRM?

Calendly and Acuity have native integrations with Hubspot, Salesforce, and others. SimplyBook.me connects via Zapier. Appointlet and Setmore have fewer direct CRM integrations.

Can I use these tools for team scheduling?

Yes – Calendly (Teams plan), Acuity (employee scheduling), SimplyBook.me (multi-staff), Appointlet (Growth plan), and Setmore (free up to 4 users) all support multiple team members.

Hopefully this breakdown helps you pick the right scheduling tool for your business. The best choice depends on whether you value simplicity, customization, or a generous free tier. Test a few free plans – most let you try without committing – and see which feels right for your daily flow.
