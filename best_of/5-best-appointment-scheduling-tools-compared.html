If you’re still emailing back and forth to book a single meeting, you’re wasting time you could be spending on actual work. Appointment scheduling tools have been around for years, but the options in 2026 are smarter, more flexible, and way more affordable than you might think.

I’ve tested over a dozen scheduling apps in the last few months, working with real small businesses — a freelance designer, a dental clinic, a yoga studio, and a consulting firm. I looked at how each tool handles booking flows, calendar sync, payment collection, team management, and integrations with other business apps.

Here are the five tools that stood out, along with the honest upsides and downsides of each. By the end, you’ll know exactly which one belongs in your stack.

Calendly – The Best All-Rounder for Most Teams

Calendly is the name everyone knows, and for good reason. It’s simple to set up, works with nearly every calendar (Google, Outlook, iCloud, Exchange), and handles the core booking tasks without fuss.

What I liked: The one-click booking link is still the smoothest experience for clients. You set your availability, share a link, and they pick a time that works — no back-and-forth. The newer features in 2026 include “collective availability” for group meetings and a revamped round‑robin for teams. I also appreciate the ability to add custom questions and payment collection via Stripe.

Where it falls short: If you need complex service-based bookings (like a salon with multiple staff and service durations), Calendly starts to feel limited. You can’t really handle multi-step booking flows (e.g., choose a service, then a staff member, then a time) without a workaround. Also, the free plan only lets you have one event type, which is a bit stingy.

Pricing: Free (1 event type, 1 calendar connection), Essentials $10/month, Teams $16/month, Enterprise custom.

Acuity Scheduling – Best for Service-Based Businesses

Acuity, now owned by Squarespace, is built for businesses that sell appointments by service, class, or package. Think hair salons, massage therapists, personal trainers, and consultants who charge different rates for different offerings.

What I liked: The service-based booking flow is second to none. You can set up multiple service categories, each with its own duration, price, and staff assignment. Clients can see real-time availability per staff member and add add-ons (like a hair treatment or a 15-minute consult). Acuity also handles cancellation policies, waitlists, and gift certificates really well. The integration with Square for payments is solid.

Where it falls short: The interface feels a bit dated compared to newer tools. Setting up advanced rules (like “if client books service A, block service B on the same day”) can get confusing. And if you only need simple meeting scheduling, Acuity is overkill — you’ll pay for features you’ll never use.

Pricing: Free (1 calendar, 1 location, limited features), Growing $16/month, Professional $27/month.

SimplyBook.me – Most Customizable for Unique Workflows

SimplyBook.me is the wild card. It started as a booking system for clinics and gyms, but it has evolved into a highly customizable platform that can handle almost any scheduling scenario you can dream up.

What I liked: The customization engine is powerful. You can build custom booking forms with conditional logic, set up complex availability rules (e.g., “allow booking only on first Monday of the month”), and integrate directly with WordPress, Wix, and Shopify. It also has a built-in SMS and email reminder system that works well. For a solo operator, the free plan is generous: unlimited services and staff.

Where it falls short: All that flexibility comes with a learning curve. The admin interface is cluttered, and you’ll probably need to watch a few tutorials to get things set up correctly. Some features (like the mobile app) feel half-baked. And if you’re a simple freelancer who just needs a calendar link, this is way too much.

Pricing: Free (up to 50 bookings per month), Basic $9.90/month, Premium $29.90/month, Enterprise custom.

Square Appointments – Best for In-Person Retail & Services

If you already use Square for payments or POS, Square Appointments is a no-brainer. It’s designed for businesses that run a physical location — retail shops, salons, dental offices — where clients book services and pay at the same time.

What I liked: The tight integration with Square’s payment ecosystem is fantastic. You can collect deposits, sell packages, and issue refunds all from the same dashboard. The booking flow is straightforward for both client and staff, and you can manage multiple locations easily. The free plan actually includes unlimited staff and locations, which is rare.

Where it falls short: Square Appointments is not great for online-only businesses or teams that work remotely. The calendar sync is limited to Google and iCloud (no Outlook natively), and the reporting features are basic unless you pay extra. Also, the client booking page looks a bit generic — you can’t customize it much.

Pricing: Free (unlimited staff & locations, core features), Plus $29/month, Premium custom.

Setmore – Best Free Tier for Solo Operators

Setmore has been around for ages and remains a solid choice for freelancers and solopreneurs who want a fully functional free tool without feeling nickel-and-dimed.

What I liked: The free plan is genuinely usable. You can have unlimited appointments, unlimited calendar connections, and basic features like email reminders and a public booking page. The interface is clean and modern, and the mobile app works well. Setmore also offers a video meeting integration (through its own Setmore Video) so clients can join a call directly from the booking confirmation.

Where it falls short: The paid plans are a bit expensive compared to competitors. For $15/month (Pro) you get SMS reminders and payment collection, but Acuity’s $16 plan offers more. Also, Setmore doesn’t handle complex service combos or staff scheduling as well as Acuity or SimplyBook.me. It’s best for simple, one-person scheduling.

Pricing: Free (unlimited appointments), Pro $15/month, Team $35/month (up to 6 users).

How to Choose the Right Tool

The best scheduling tool depends on what kind of business you run. Here’s a quick cheat sheet:

  • Freelancer or consultant: Calendly is the easiest to start with. The free plan is limited, but the $10 Essentials plan is worth it if you have more than one meeting type.
  • Salon, spa, or clinic: Acuity Scheduling or SimplyBook.me. Acuity is more polished; SimplyBook.me is more flexible.
  • Retail shop with appointments: Square Appointments, especially if you already use Square for payments.
  • Solo operator who wants everything free: Setmore. No catch — just a solid free tier.
  • Business with unique rules (like class passes, waitlists, or multi-step booking): SimplyBook.me. It’s the most configurable, but be ready to invest time in setup.

Pros and Cons Summary

Calendly

  • Pros: Extremely easy to set up, clean client experience, great for teams, reliable sync.
  • Cons: Limited for service-based workflows, free plan too restrictive, no native payment collection (only via Stripe).

Acuity Scheduling

  • Pros: Excellent service-based booking, strong payment integration, good for classes and packages.
  • Cons: Dated interface, complex rule setup, overkill for simple scheduling.

SimplyBook.me

  • Pros: Highly customizable, generous free features, strong WordPress integration, built-in reminders.
  • Cons: Steep learning curve, cluttered admin, mobile app needs improvement.

Square Appointments

  • Pros: Free unlimited staff/locations, deep Square payments integration, good for physical locations.
  • Cons: Limited calendar sync, generic booking page, weak reporting on free plan.

Setmore

  • Pros: Truly free unlimited appointments, clean interface, good mobile app, video meeting integration.
  • Cons: Paid plans are pricey for what you get, weak service management, no team scheduling on free plan.

Verdict: Which One Should You Pick?

If I had to recommend one tool for the widest range of small businesses, it’s Calendly. It’s not the cheapest, and it won’t handle a hair salon’s complex service tree out of the box, but for 80% of use cases — meetings, demos, consultations, team scheduling — it just works. The $10 Essentials plan is a fair trade for the time you save.

But if your business is a salon, clinic, or fitness studio, go with Acuity Scheduling. The extra setup time pays off every time a client books a service without confusion.

And if you’re on a tight budget and need a solid free tool, Setmore is your friend. No hidden gotchas.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Can I use these tools to collect payments at booking?

Yes — Calendly (via Stripe), Acuity (Square/PayPal), SimplyBook.me (multiple gateways), Square Appointments (native Square payments), and Setmore (paid plans with Stripe) all support payment collection at the time of booking.

2. Do these tools sync with Google Calendar?

All five sync with Google Calendar and most also sync with Outlook, iCloud, and Exchange. Square Appointments does not natively sync with Outlook — only Google and iCloud.

3. Which tool is best for a team with multiple staff members?

Calendly has the cleanest team scheduling (round-robin, collective availability). SimplyBook.me and Acuity also handle staff assignment well, especially for service-based businesses.

4. Can I embed the booking page on my website?

Yes. All tools provide embed codes, widgets, and direct links. SimplyBook.me and Acuity offer deeper integration with WordPress and Wix.

5. Is there a free plan that’s actually usable?

Setmore’s free plan is the most generous for solo users. Square Appointments is also free for unlimited staff and locations if you don’t need advanced features. SimplyBook.me’s free plan is good for low-volume bookings.

6. Which tool has the best mobile app for clients?

Calendly and Setmore both have clean, responsive mobile booking pages. SimplyBook.me’s app is functional but a bit clunky.

7. Can I set custom cancellation or rescheduling policies?

Acuity and SimplyBook.me offer the most granular control over cancellation policies, including time windows and automatic refunds. Calendly has basic rules, while Setmore and Square Appointments are more limited.

8. Do these tools support recurring appointments?

Yes, but with varying ease. Acuity and SimplyBook.me handle recurring services (like weekly classes) best. Calendly can do recurring meetings but not recurring services.

9. What if I need to handle multiple locations?

Square Appointments and SimplyBook.me are best for multi-location setups. Calendly and Acuity can manage multiple calendars but don’t have a true “location” concept.

10. Which tool is the most secure for healthcare or HIPAA?

None of these tools are HIPAA-compliant out of the box. You’d need a specialized solution like TheraNest or SimplePractice. However, Calendly offers a HIPAA plan for enterprise customers. Always check with your compliance officer.

Choosing the right scheduling tool doesn’t have to be a headache. Start with your actual workflow — are you booking simple 30-minute calls, or are you running a full-service business with staff, services, and payments? Match that to the tool that does it best, and you’ll wonder why you waited so long to automate.
