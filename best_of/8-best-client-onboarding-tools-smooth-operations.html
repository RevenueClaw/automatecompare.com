You’ve closed the deal. Now the real work begins: getting that new client set up, happy, and paying on time. A messy onboarding process can kill momentum, confuse clients, and create extra admin work for you. That’s why the right client onboarding software matters.

I’ve tested dozens of tools over the past year, from simple form builders to full-blown project management suites. Some are great for freelancers on a budget; others work better for agencies juggling multiple clients. Below, I’ve narrowed it to eight tools that actually deliver on their promises.

Why Client Onboarding Software Matters

Manual onboarding—sending individual emails, chasing signatures, creating separate invoices—eats hours every week. Automating those steps means:

  • Clients get a consistent experience every time.
  • You stop forgetting key steps (like sending that welcome packet).
  • Payments and contracts get handled faster.
  • Your team spends less time on repetitive tasks.

The best tools handle at least three of these: proposals/contracts, payment collection, project kickoff, and automated communication. Some do all four.

The 8 Best Client Onboarding Tools

1. HoneyBook

Best for: Freelancers and small service businesses who want an all-in-one platform.

HoneyBook combines proposals, contracts, invoices, and a client portal. You build a workflow that triggers emails, reminders, and next steps automatically. The client portal lets clients upload files, sign documents, and pay without leaving the platform.

Key features

  • Customizable templates for proposals, contracts, and invoices.
  • Automated email sequences (e.g., send a welcome note immediately after signing).
  • Integrated payment processing (credit cards, ACH).
  • Project timeline with task checklists.

Pricing: Starts at $16/month (annual) for the Starter plan; Unlimited plan at $40/month. Free trial available.

Pros: Very easy to set up; beautiful templates; strong mobile app.

Cons: Limited integrations with third-party apps; reporting could be deeper.

2. Dubsado

Best for: Creative agencies and consultants who need heavy workflow automation.

Dubsado lets you create complex workflows with triggers, delays, and conditional logic. You can send a proposal → client signs → auto-create a project → send a welcome email → schedule a kickoff call → send an invoice—all without manual intervention.

Key features

  • Workflow builder with if/then logic.
  • Client portal with file sharing and scheduling.
  • Built-in form builder (questionnaires, intake forms).
  • Automated invoicing and payment reminders.

Pricing: $35/month (billed annually) or $45/month monthly. No free plan, but 14-day trial.

Pros: Extremely flexible workflows; good Zapier integration.

Cons: Steeper learning curve; interface feels dated.

3. 17hats

Best for: Solo entrepreneurs who want a simple, affordable option.

17hats bundles proposals, contracts, invoicing, and a simple CRM. Its onboarding workflows are basic but effective: send a proposal → get signature → send invoice → create project. You can also set up email templates and reminders.

Key features

  • Basic workflow automation (triggered by events).
  • Contact management with tags and notes.
  • Expense tracking and bookkeeping integration.
  • Mobile app for iOS and Android.

Pricing: $15/month (annual) or $25/month monthly. Free trial.

Pros: Low cost; easy to learn; includes bookkeeping.

Cons: Limited automation depth; no client portal.

4. Bonsai

Best for: Freelancers who need contract templates and proposal creation.

Bonsai started as a contract and proposal tool, then added invoicing and basic workflow automation. You can create a proposal → client signs → auto-generate a contract → send an invoice. It also offers a freelance CRM with task management.

Key features

  • Legal contract templates (US-specific).
  • Proposal editor with pricing tables.
  • Time tracking and expense logging.
  • Automated invoice reminders.

Pricing: $25/month (annual) for the Workflow plan; $39/month for Workflow Plus (includes tax assistance). Free trial.

Pros: Excellent contract library; good time tracking.

Cons: Workflow automation is limited; no client portal beyond document sharing.

5. Client Portal (by Workiz)

Best for: Service businesses (cleaning, plumbing, repair) that need scheduling and client communication.

Workiz’s Client Portal is more niche—it’s built for field service businesses. But its onboarding features are solid: you can send a welcome portal link, collect customer info, schedule appointments, and take payments. The portal also lets clients track job status.

Key features

  • Customizable client portal with branding.
  • Online booking and scheduling.
  • Payment collection (credit card, invoicing).
  • Job tracking and notifications.

Pricing: Starts at $45/month per user. Free trial.

Pros: Great for service businesses; integrated scheduling.

Cons: Not suited for digital agencies or consultants; more expensive per user.

6. Monday.com (with Onboarding Template)

Best for: Teams that already use Monday.com and want a flexible workflow.

Monday.com isn’t purpose-built for client onboarding, but its onboarding template and automation features can replicate most dedicated tools. You can create a board with columns for each step (welcome email sent, contract signed, payment received, kickoff call), then set up automations to move items along.

Key features

  • Visual project boards with custom columns.
  • Automations (trigger: status change → send email, create task, etc.).
  • Integrations with Slack, Gmail, Zoom, and hundreds of apps.
  • Client-facing boards (guest access).

Pricing: $8/seat/month (annual) for Basic; $12 for Standard (includes automations). Free for up to 2 seats.

Pros: Extremely customizable; powerful integrations; scales with your business.

Cons: Requires setup time; no built-in contracts or invoicing (need integrations).

7. PracticePanther

Best for: Law firms and legal professionals who need strict compliance.

PracticePanther is legal-specific, but its client intake and onboarding features are top-notch. You can create intake forms, send engagement letters, collect retainers, and manage conflicts checks—all automated. The client portal stores documents and communication securely.

Key features

  • Conflict checking and intake workflows.
  • E-signatures (integrated with DocuSign).
  • Payment processing (trust accounting compliant).
  • Calendar and task management.

Pricing: Starts at $59/month (billed annually). Free trial.

Pros: Built for legal compliance; strong trust accounting.

Cons: Overkill for non-legal businesses; expensive.

8. Tally

Best for: Minimalists who just need a simple form-to-welcome email flow.

Tally is a form builder that can double as a lightweight onboarding tool. You create an intake form, collect payment via Stripe, then send a confirmation email with next steps. No contracts, no project management—but if you only need to collect info and accept payments, it’s free (with limits) and dead simple.

Key features

  • Unlimited forms on free plan (limited submissions).
  • Stripe payment collection.
  • Email notifications and custom confirmations.
  • Logic jumps and conditional fields.

Pricing: Free for up to 100 submissions/month; Pro at $29/month for 10,000 submissions.

Pros: Free tier; extremely easy to use; no learning curve.

Cons: No contracts, no project management, no client portal.

How to Choose the Right Tool

Ask yourself these questions before picking one:

  • Do I need contracts and payments inside the tool? If yes, HoneyBook, Dubsado, or Bonsai are your best bets. If you already use a separate payment processor (Stripe, PayPal) and a contract tool (PandaDoc, DocuSign), Monday.com or Tally might work.
  • How complex are my onboarding workflows? If you have many conditional steps (e.g., "if client selects package A, send this; if package B, send that"), Dubsado or Monday.com give you the most control. For simple linear flows, HoneyBook or 17hats are fine.
  • Do I need a client portal where clients can see their project status? HoneyBook, Dubsado, and Workiz offer client portals. Monday.com has guest access (clients can see specific boards). 17hats and Bonsai don’t have dedicated portals.
  • What’s my budget? Tally is free for very basic needs. 17hats and HoneyBook (Starter) are under $20/month. Dubsado and Bonsai are in the $25–$45 range. PracticePanther is for legal pros with deeper pockets.

Final Verdict

If you’re a freelancer or small service business owner who wants an all-in-one solution with minimal setup, go with HoneyBook. It balances ease of use with enough automation to save you hours per week. The client portal and beautiful templates give your clients a professional experience from day one.

If you’re an agency or consultant who needs complex, multi-step workflows, Dubsado is the better choice—despite its learning curve. The conditional logic and deep integrations let you automate almost everything.

For those on a tight budget, 17hats or Tally (if you only need forms and payments) are solid options. And if you’re already deep in the Monday.com ecosystem, use its onboarding template—it’s free and highly customizable.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is client onboarding software?

It’s a tool that automates the process of getting a new client set up: sending proposals, collecting signatures, taking payments, creating projects, and sending welcome communications. It replaces manual email chains and spreadsheets.

Do I need a separate CRM?

Most client onboarding tools include basic CRM features (contact management, notes, tags). If you need a full-fledged CRM with sales pipeline tracking and advanced reporting, you might want to use a dedicated CRM (like Hubspot) and integrate it with your onboarding tool via Zapier.

Can I use these tools for recurring clients?

Yes. Most allow you to create templates and workflows that you can reuse. Some, like Dubsado and HoneyBook, let you duplicate existing projects or apply workflows to returning clients.

Is client onboarding software secure?

Reputable tools use encryption (SSL/TLS) for data in transit and at rest. For legal or financial data, choose a tool with SOC 2 compliance or specific certifications (PracticePanther has legal-grade security). Always check their security page.

How long does it take to set up?

Simple tools like Tally or 17hats can be set up in 30 minutes. HoneyBook and Bonsai take a few hours to customize templates. Dubsado and Monday.com may require a day or two to build out workflows properly.
