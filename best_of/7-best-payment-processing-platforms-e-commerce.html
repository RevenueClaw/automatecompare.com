If you're running an online store, payment processing can feel like dealing with twenty different puzzle pieces. You want something that doesn't charge you an arm and a leg, works with your shopping cart system, and keeps customers feeling secure. After testing and comparing dozens of platforms, here are the seven that Make sense for small businesses in 2026.

Stripe – Best for Custom Checkouts and Global Reach

Stripe has been a go-to for developers and store owners who want complete control over the checkout experience. It's not the simplest tool to set up from scratch, but once you have it running, it handles a ton of complexity behind the scenes.

Key features:

  • Supports over 135 currencies and payment methods like Apple Pay, Google Pay, and local wallets.
  • Stripe Radar includes machine-learning fraud detection out of the box.
  • Subscriptions and invoicing are native, no extra plugin needed.
  • Connect product allows you to build marketplace-style payment flows.

Pricing: 2.9% + 30¢ per successful transaction for standard cards. International cards add 1.5% and a small currency conversion fee. No monthly fee for the basic account.

Pros:

  • Highly customizable – you can tweak almost every part of the payment flow.
  • Excellent developer documentation and API design.
  • Global support for markets outside the US, including strong European and Asian coverage.

Cons:

  • The default dashboard can be overwhelming if you're not used to technical tools.
  • Support is mostly chat-based, and response times vary.
  • Some premium features (like advanced fraud rules) cost extra.

If you're comfortable with a bit of setup work, Stripe gives you the most flexibility. Check Stripe ->

PayPal – Best for Trust and Ease of Use

PayPal has been around long enough that many customers feel safer checking out with it than entering credit card details. For a solo seller just starting, it's the lowest-friction option.

Key features:

  • PayPal Checkout buttons are one-click integrations for most platforms.
  • Seller protection policies cover certain unauthorized transactions and item-not-received claims.
  • Venmo integration lets you accept payments from Venmo users.
  • Instant payouts available for a small fee.

Pricing: 2.99% + 49¢ per transaction for standard checkout. PayPal Payments Pro (custom checkout) costs $30/month plus 2.59% + 49¢. Microtransactions (under $10) have a lower rate: 4.99% + 9¢.

Pros:

  • Customers trust the brand, which can boost conversion on smaller stores.
  • Setting up a button on a simple site takes minutes.
  • Works with virtually every e-commerce platform on the market.

Cons:

  • Fees are higher than most competitors, especially for international transactions.
  • Account freezes and holds happen more often than you'd like.
  • The reporting dashboard hasn't been updated in years and feels clunky.

If you need something up and running fast and don't want to think about it, PayPal works. Just be ready for the occasional hold. Check PayPal ->

Square – Best for Offline and Online Combo

Square started with card readers for in-person sales, but their online payment tools have caught up nicely. If you sell both at markets and through your website, Square handles both sides cleanly.

Key features:

  • Free online checkout links and payment pages (no need for a full site).
  • Inventory syncs between your physical sales and web store.
  • Built-in appointment booking and invoicing tools.
  • Square Terminal for in-person chip/contactless payments.

Pricing: 2.6% + 10¢ per online transaction. No monthly fee for the basic plan. In-person transactions are 2.5% + 10¢. Premium features like advanced reporting cost $20/month.

Pros:

  • The free payment links mean you can start selling on social media right now.
  • Hardware is affordable and looks clean on a counter.
  • Payouts go to your bank account by next business day.

Cons:

  • You can't easily switch to another processor without rebuilding your setup.
  • Limited support for high-risk industries or international sellers.
  • The inventory system works well until you have more than 1000 SKUs, then it gets slow.

Square is ideal for small shops that do a mix of online and offline sales. Check Square ->

Shopify Payments – Best for Shopify Stores

If you're using Shopify for your online store, this is the obvious choice. Shopify Payments comes built in and eliminates the extra transaction fees Shopify charges when you use a third-party gateway.

Key features:

  • Zero additional transaction fees (compared to 0.5%–2% extra for external gateways).
  • Supports local payment methods in more than 20 countries.
  • Subscriptions and multi-currency display work natively.
  • Apple Pay, Shop Pay, and Google Pay are all included.

Pricing: 2.9% + 30¢ for the Basic Shopify plan. The Standard plan drops to 2.6% + 30¢, and Advanced goes to 2.4% + 30¢. No separate monthly fee beyond your Shopify subscription.

Pros:

  • No extra fees means you save 1–2% per transaction compared to using Stripe or PayPal with Shopify.
  • Shop Pay checkout is faster and increases conversion.
  • Chargeback handling is simple inside the Shopify dashboard.

Cons:

  • You must use Shopify for your entire store.
  • If your store is high risk or you sell prohibited items, they can cancel your account suddenly.
  • Reporting is decent but not as deep as Stripe's analytics.

If you're on Shopify, going with Shopify Payments is a no-brainer. The fee savings alone cover the subscription. Check Shopify Payments ->

Adyen – Best for High-Volume Merchants

Adyen is less known among small businesses, but it powers a lot of larger online brands. If you're processing over $10,000 a month, Adyen's pricing structure can save you money.

Key features:

  • Single platform handles online, in-app, and in-person payments.
  • Revenue optimization tools (like automatic retries on declined transactions).
  • Detailed fraud management with machine learning scoring.
  • Global acquiring in many regions reduces cross-border costs.

Pricing: Transaction fees vary by payment method and region, usually 2.5%–3.5% + small per-transaction fee. No monthly fee, but setup and integration require some effort. Volume discounts are available at higher tiers.

Pros:

  • Lower effective rates for high monthly sales due to interchange-plus pricing.
  • Direct acquiring in many countries removes multiple middlemen.
  • The retry logic alone can recover 3–5% of failed transactions.

Cons:

  • Setup is more involved and typically needs a developer.
  • Not ideal for stores under $5,000/month – fees can actually be higher.
  • Support can feel enterprise-focused, not small-business friendly.

Adyen makes sense when you outgrow Stripe's flat pricing or need better international coverage. Check Adyen ->

Authorize.Net – Best for Established Businesses That Need Stability

Authorize.Net has been around for a long time. It's not flashy, but it's proven and works with most merchant accounts. If your bank requires a traditional gateway, Authorize.Net is the standard.

Key features:

  • Works with any merchant account you choose (you're not locked into one provider).
  • Payment forms can be hosted (simple) or embedded (custom).
  • Recurring billing and customer information management built in.
  • Advanced fraud detection filters you can configure yourself.

Pricing: $25/month gateway fee plus 2.9% + 30¢ per transaction. Some merchant accounts charge extra on top. There's also a $49 setup fee unless you use a promotion.

Pros:

  • Rock-solid reliability – minimal downtime historically.
  • You can shop for a merchant account separately to get better rates.
  • Supports a wide range of older payment methods like eCheck.

Cons:

  • The monthly fee is high if you're just starting and have few sales.
  • The user interface looks like it's from 2015, and not in a charming way.
  • Documentation is thorough but sometimes hard to navigate.

Authorize.Net is a safe choice if you want a separate merchant account and don't want to change providers often. Check Authorize.Net ->

Braintree – Best for Mobile Apps and Multi-Platform Stores

Braintree is owned by PayPal but operates independently. It gives you more control than PayPal's standard checkout while keeping that backend connection.

Key features:

  • Native support for mobile app payment flows on iOS and Android.
  • Drops-in UI components that look modern without custom coding.
  • PayPal payments are included at lower rates than PayPal's own service.
  • Subscription management and credit card vaulting.

Pricing: 2.59% + 49¢ per transaction. No monthly fee. PayPal transactions inside Braintree are 2.29% + 49¢. Micro-transactions have a separate rate.

Pros:

  • Excellent mobile SDKs – fastest way to get payments in an app.
  • Fraud protection from both Braintree's toolset and PayPal's backend.
  • Supports over 40 local payment methods across various countries.

Cons:

  • Underwriting can be strict – some small businesses get denied.
  • The dashboard is less intuitive than Stripe's.
  • Customer support is hit-or-miss, especially outside the US.

For app-based stores or stores that want PayPal's reach without the checkout friction, Braintree is a strong pick. Check Braintree ->
