Top 6 Free Email Marketing Tools for Startups in 2026

Email is still the highest-ROI channel most small businesses have. It's also the one where startups waste the most money, usually by paying for a plan they don't need yet.

Here's the thing nobody tells you: the free tiers available in 2026 are not the crippled demos they were five years ago. Several of them will carry a startup from zero to 1,000 subscribers without you entering a credit card. The trick is knowing which limits actually matter and which ones are just marketing copy.

So I signed up for eleven free plans, sent test campaigns through each one, and tracked what broke. Six of them are worth your time. Here's the honest breakdown — including the parts the vendors would rather you didn't read.

What actually matters in a free email marketing plan

Before the list, a quick filter. Most "free email marketing" comparisons just list contact limits. That's the least interesting number. What matters:

  • Contact limit vs. send limit. Some tools give you 2,000 contacts but only 6,000 sends a month. If you email weekly, that's a math problem waiting to happen.
  • Is automation gated? A welcome email is the single highest-performing message you'll ever send. If the free tier won't let you build one, the plan is nearly useless for a real business.
  • Deliverability. Free plans usually share IP pools with everyone else on the platform. That's fine for most, but it means your reputation is partly out of your hands.
  • Branding and watermarks. Some free tiers stamp "sent via" footers on your emails. For a startup trying to look established, that stings.
  • What happens at the limit. Does the account pause sends? Do you get locked out? Does it auto-upgrade you into a bill?

Keep those five in mind as you read. Now the tools.

1. MailerLite — best overall free tier for most startups

Check MailerLite ->

If you only read one section of this article, read this one. MailerLite's free plan gives you 1,000 subscribers and 12,000 emails per month — that's 12 sends per subscriber, which is more headroom than almost anyone else offers at zero cost.

The editor is genuinely pleasant to work with. Drag-and-drop blocks, saved templates, and a decent image library. You get landing pages and a basic website builder on the free plan too, which matters more than people realize. A startup without a site can launch a waitlist page and start collecting emails the same afternoon, all inside one tool.

What surprised me: signup forms and pop-ups are included free, and they're not the ugly ones. You can set triggers, exit intent, the works.

The honest downside is automation. Free accounts get access to the automation builder, but the workflow options are limited compared to the paid tiers — you're mostly working with simple welcome and birthday sequences. If your growth plan involves a five-branch nurture sequence based on behavior, you'll hit the ceiling fast.

Also worth knowing: MailerLite is EU-based (Lithuania), so GDPR compliance is baked in rather than bolted on. For startups selling into Europe, that's a real advantage.

Pros

  • 1,000 subscribers and 12,000 monthly sends on the free plan
  • Free landing pages and website builder
  • Clean, fast editor that doesn't fight you
  • GDPR-friendly by default
  • Paid plans start around $10/month when you outgrow it

Cons

  • Automation depth is limited on the free tier
  • No phone support on free (24/7 email and chat only)
  • Some advanced segmentation sits behind paid plans

Best for: SaaS startups, agencies, and solo founders who want the most subscribers for $0.

2. Brevo — best free plan if your contact list is already big

Check Brevo ->

Brevo (the company formerly known as Sendinblue) does something none of the others on this list do: unlimited contacts on the free plan. Your limit is on sends, not storage — 300 emails per day, roughly 9,000 a month.

That's a strange but genuinely useful structure. If you've been collecting emails for two years at trade shows and have 8,000 names sitting in a spreadsheet, Brevo will hold all of them for free while you figure out what to do. Most competitors would charge you real money just to import that list.

Brevo is also more than email. The free plan includes a CRM, SMS marketing credits (limited), and basic marketing automation. For a startup that hasn't picked a CRM yet, this can replace two subscriptions.

The catch: 300 emails a day is a hard ceiling. Send a 5,000-person campaign and it'll take you over two weeks to deliver. Brevo will queue it, but your "Tuesday launch" becomes a two-week trickle. For a startup doing weekly newsletters to a growing list, that's the wrong shape of limit.

Also, the free plan stamps Brevo branding on your emails unless you're on a paid tier. And the interface, while improved, is denser than MailerLite's — there's a lot of menu.

Pros

  • Unlimited contacts on the free plan
  • 300 emails/day (about 9,000/month)
  • Includes CRM and SMS tools at no cost
  • Transactional email API available free
  • Paid plans start around $9/month

Cons

  • Daily send cap makes big campaigns impractical
  • Brevo branding on free-plan emails
  • Interface takes getting used to
  • Automation features are more limited than the marketing suggests

Best for: Startups with a large existing contact list and modest send volume, or anyone who wants email plus a lightweight CRM in one login.
