Whether you run a service business, coach clients, or schedule sales calls, the right appointment booking tool can save you hours every week. But with dozens of options out there, it's easy to overpay for features you don’t need or get stuck with a clunky interface.

I’ve spent the last month testing the five most popular scheduling tools side-by-side. I set up real booking flows, synced real calendars, and even booked fake appointments to test notifications, reminders, and payment integrations. Here’s what I found.

What to Look for in a Scheduling Tool (Before You Pick)

Before we jump into the reviews, here’s a quick primer on the features that actually matter for small businesses:

  • Calendar sync (two-way). The tool must write events back to Google Calendar, Outlook, or iCloud. One-way sync is useless if someone books a time you already blocked.
  • Booking forms & custom questions. If you need to collect client info before the appointment (e.g., reason for visit, package selection), Make sure the tool supports custom fields.
  • Payment integration. For service businesses (hair salons, consultants, tutors), taking deposits or full payment at booking reduces no-shows.
  • Group & round-robin scheduling. If you have a team, you might want clients to book with a specific person or the next available.
  • Automated reminders & follow-ups. Email and text reminders are table stakes; post-appointment follow-ups can save you manual work.
  • Pricing. Some tools charge per user or per event. Free tiers exist, but they often limit the number of events or customizations.

Now let’s see how each tool stacks up.

1. Calendly – The All-Around Favorite

Check Tool ->

Calendly is the name everyone knows. It’s built for one-on-one and small group meetings where you just need to share a link and let someone pick a slot. No frills, but what it does, it does well.

What I liked:

  • Simple setup. I created my first event type in under 2 minutes. Connect your calendar, set availability hours, and share the link. That’s it.
  • Multiple event types. You can have separate links for “30 min sales call,” “1 hour coaching,” and “15 min check-in.” Each can have different buffer times, advance notice, and availability.
  • Workflows (paid plans). Automatic follow-up emails, reminders, and even SMS messages. You can attach a calendar event invite that adds a Zoom link automatically.
  • Routing (paid). Perfect for teams: let leads book with the right sales rep based on location, product interest, or round-robin.
  • Integrations. Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Salesforce, Hubspot, and hundreds more via Zapier.

What I didn’t:

  • Expensive per user. The Team plan is $16/month/user and still limits you to one calendar connection per user. If you have a receptionist managing a team, you pay for them too.
  • No built-in payment processing. You need to use a third-party integration (Stripe or PayPal via Zapier) to take payments. That adds complexity and extra cost.
  • Limited custom branding on the lower tier. The free plan shows “Powered by Calendly” and the basic interface is pretty generic.

Best for: Freelancers, sales teams, and anyone who needs a dead-simple link-based scheduler. If you don’t need payments or fancy forms, Calendly is the easiest choice.

Pricing: Free (1 event type), Essentials $12/mo, Team $16/mo/user, Enterprise custom.

2. Acuity Scheduling – Best for Service Businesses

Check Tool ->

Acuity has been around forever (since 2009, now owned by Squarespace). It’s designed for appointment-based businesses like salons, clinics, consultants, and tutors. If you need to manage multiple locations, staff, and services, Acuity is a strong contender.

What I liked:

  • Deep customization. You can create detailed intake forms, require clients to sign waivers, and set automatic cancellation policies. For example, I set up a haircut booking that asked about hair type, previous color history, and allergies.
  • Built-in payments. Accept deposits or full payment via Stripe or PayPal without any extra integration. You can even charge cancellation fees automatically.
  • Staff management. Set individual calendars for each team member, with different services and availability. Clients can pick their preferred stylist or the next available.
  • Client management. Acuity keeps a history of every client’s appointments and intake forms. You can send batch emails or add notes visible to staff.
  • Two-way calendar sync. Works with Google, iCloud, Outlook, and Office 365. I tested it with Google – bookings showed up instantly.

What I didn’t:

  • Steep learning curve. The settings menu is overwhelming. It took me 30 minutes just to find where to change my time zone. The layout feels outdated.
  • No free plan. There’s a 14-day free trial, but then you’re paying at least $16/month. And features like client reminders are locked behind higher tiers.
  • Design limitations. Customization of the booking page is clunky. You can’t easily embed it without looking a bit old-school.
  • Reporting is basic. You get appointment and payment summaries, but no advanced analytics like conversion rates or source tracking.

Best for: Independent service providers and small multi-staff businesses that need intake forms and payments. It’s less useful for internal meeting scheduling.

Pricing: Pay as you go (no free plan), $16/mo for Emerging ($20/mo billed monthly), $27/mo for Growing, $49/mo for Power.

3. SimplyBook.me – The Feature Powerhouse (But Ugly)

Check Tool ->

SimplyBook.me is crammed with features. It’s like the Swiss Army knife of scheduling – and like many Swiss Army knives, it works, but it’s not pretty. This tool is ideal for businesses that need a full booking website, online payments, and advanced customizations without learning to code.

What I liked:

  • Built-in website booking page. You get a fully branded booking site (yourcompany.simplybook.me) that includes services, staff bios, and a blog. It can double as a basic website.
  • Massive feature list. Over 50 “custom features” (their term for add-ons) including coupon codes, membership cards, class schedules, recurring appointments, waiting list, and SMS marketing.
  • Payment gateways. Stripe, PayPal, Square, Authorize.net, even bank transfers. You can require a credit card to hold the appointment without charging.
  • Multi-language and multi-currency. Perfect if you serve international clients.
  • Customizable booking flow. You can embed the booking button on any site, or use the iframe. I liked the “Book Now” button that opens a quick popup.

What I didn’t:

  • User interface is dated and crowded. The dashboard looks like it was designed in 2012. Finding settings is a maze. I had to watch a YouTube tutorial just to set up automatic reminders.
  • Free plan is extremely limited. Only 50 bookings per month and no payment integration. The paid plans also restrict features based on tier – you need the $49.90/mo Premium plan to get SMS reminders and custom domain.
  • Mobile app is weak. The apps (iOS/Android) work for checking bookings but you can’t manage most settings from them.
  • Support can be slow. I sent a ticket and got a reply 28 hours later.

Best for: Businesses that need tons of features on a budget (lots of functionality per dollar) and don’t mind a clunky interface. Great for gyms, yoga studios, and wellness centers.

Pricing: Free (50 bookings/mo), Subscription $8.50/mo, Premium $49.90/mo, Enterprise custom.

4. YouCanBook.me – Simple & Reliable

Check Tool ->

YCBM (YouCanBook.me) positions itself as the middle ground between ultra-simple Calendly and feature-heavy Acuity. It’s particularly strong at custom forms and integrates beautifully with Google Calendar.

What I liked:

  • Excellent Google Calendar integration. If you live in Google Calendar, this is your tool. It syncs perfectly, respects your existing events, and lets you set “availability blocks” that override other calendar entries.
  • Detailed booking forms. You can ask clients all sorts of questions (text, dropdown, checkboxes, file uploads). Responses appear in the event description or in a separate tab. I set up a form that asked “What’s the main topic for our call?” – super useful for prep.
  • Custom questions with logic. You can show/hide questions based on previous answers. Example: “Do you have a referral code?” If yes, show a field for the code. That’s rare at this price point.
  • Reminders and follow-ups. Automated email and text reminders. You can also send a post-meeting feedback request.
  • Embedded calendar view. The “book now” button opens a slick calendar widget that looks great on any website.
  • Affordable. The solo plan is $10/month and includes everything a freelancer needs.

What I didn’t:

  • No built-in payments. Like Calendly, you need a third party (Stripe via Zapier) to take payments. That’s an extra monthly cost and setup.
  • Limited team features. You can set up multiple calendars but it’s not designed for round-robin or group booking. The team plan is $16/mo but still feels basic.
  • No dedicated mobile app. The responsive web interface works fine on phone, but if you want an app, there isn’t one.
  • Calendar sync for Outlook/iCloud is less reliable. I tested with Outlook and experienced a 10-minute delay. Google sync was instant.

Best for: Freelancers and small teams that use Google Calendar and need powerful custom forms without the complexity of Acuity. If you don’t need payments, YCBM is a top pick.

Pricing: Free (1 calendar, 1 event type), Solo $10/mo, Team $16/mo, Pro $24/mo.

5. Setmore – Best Free Plan for Small Teams

Check Tool ->

Setmore has been a favorite for budget-conscious businesses for years. Its free plan is one of the most generous: unlimited staff, unlimited appointments, and basic booking features. If you have a small team and can’t justify paying for scheduling, Setmore is worth a look.

What I liked:

  • Generous free tier. You get unlimited appointments, up to 4 staff members, and basic booking forms. No time limit on meetings. Calendar sync (Google, iCloud) is included.
  • Mobile app. The free mobile app lets your team see their schedule, send reminders, and check in clients. That’s rare for free software.
  • Class scheduling. You can set up recurring classes (yoga, bootcamp) with capacity limits. Clients can book multiple slots. Free plan supports up to 4 classes.
  • Payment integrations. Accept deposits or full payment via Square or Stripe. The free plan allows payments (with a 2% transaction fee on the free plan – but that’s waived in paid plans).
  • Video chat integration. You can automatically generate Google Meet, Zoom, or Microsoft Teams links. Another feature that’s free.

What I didn’t:

  • Outdated design. The booking page looks a little dated, though you can customize colors and add a logo. It’s not terrible, but it doesn’t feel modern.
  • Limited custom fields on free plan. You get only a few basic fields (name, email, phone). For more questions, you need the paid Pro plan.
  • No two-way sync on free plan. Actually, Setmore syncs one-way from your calendar to Setmore, but doesn’t block available times from your calendar. If you have an existing event in Google, Setmore won’t know about it – a big drawback. Two-way sync is a paid feature.
  • Support. Live chat is available only on paid plans. Free users rely on email support that can take up to 48 hours.

Best for: Very small teams or startups that want a free scheduling tool for basic needs. If you’re willing to pay, better options exist.

Pricing: Free (up to 4 staff), Pro $5/mo (per user? no, it’s per account), Team $20/mo. Two-way sync is $2/mo extra.

Which One Should You Pick?

After using all five tools for real tasks, here’s my honest recommendation based on different needs:

  • If you just want a link to share so people can pick a time (no payments, no custom forms): Go with Calendly. It’s the fastest to set up and the most reliable. The free plan is fine for one meeting type.
  • If you run a service business (salon, clinic, coaching) and need intake forms, payments, and staff management: Get Acuity Scheduling. Yes, it’s more expensive and has a learning curve, but it handles the whole client lifecycle.
  • If you need a ton of features and want to keep costs low (even if it’s clunky): SimplyBook.me is your pick. The free plan is tiny, but the $8.50 plan gives you more features than most competitors at that price.
  • If you live in Google Calendar and need powerful custom forms without paying extra for payments: YouCanBook.me is the sweet spot. The Solo plan at $10 is excellent value.
  • If you have a small team and want a truly free solution for basic scheduling: Setmore works. Just be aware of the one-way sync limitation on the free plan – it will double-book you if you’re not careful.

My overall winner for most small businesses: Acuity Scheduling. It’s not the cheapest or the prettiest, but once you climb the learning curve, it saves the most time per week. The combination of intake forms, payments, and staff scheduling is unmatched. Pair it with a Squarespace website and you have a complete booking system.

Frequently Asked Questions

What’s the best free scheduling tool?

Setmore’s free plan is the most generous if you need multiple staff members (up to 4) and unlimited appointments. For a solo freelancer, Calendly’s free plan covers one event type, which is fine for basic use. Just remember that Setmore’s free plan has no two-way calendar sync.

Can I take payments with any of these?

Yes, but only some have it built-in. Acuity, SimplyBook.me, and Setmore (with transaction fee on free) accept payments directly. Calendly and YouCanBook.me require Zapier or third-party integrations.
