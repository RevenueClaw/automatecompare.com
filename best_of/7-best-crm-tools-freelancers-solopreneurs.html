You’ve got a handful of clients, a growing pipeline, and a to-do list that’s longer than your arm. You’re a freelancer, a solopreneur, a one-person show. And somewhere between chasing leads, sending invoices, and actually doing the work, you realize: you need a CRM.

But here’s the thing – most CRMs are built for teams of five, fifty, or five hundred. They’re bloated, expensive, and assume you have a sales ops person. You don’t. You need something that works for you, not against you.

I’ve spent the last month testing over 50 CRM tools, filtering for solopreneurs. I looked at pricing, ease of use, essential features (contact management, pipeline tracking, email integration, automation), and whether the tool actually scales with you. No fluff. No enterprise features you’ll never touch.

Here are the seven best CRM tools for freelancers and solopreneurs in 2026.

How We Picked the Best CRM for Freelancers

To narrow down the list, I used the following criteria:

  • Price for a solo user – Can you get real value for under $30/month? Ideally, there’s a generous free tier.
  • Ease of setup and daily use – If it takes more than an hour to set up, it’s out. Freelancers don’t have time for onboarding marathons.
  • Essential features – Contact management, pipeline stages, email sync (Gmail/Outlook), basic automation (e.g., follow-up reminders), and mobile app.
  • Integrations – Does it connect with tools you already use: Zapier, Stripe, Calendly, invoicing apps?
  • Scalability – Can it grow with you if you later add a virtual assistant or part-time help?

I also considered real user reviews (G2, Capterra) and my own hands‑on testing with free trials. The result: seven tools that cover the spectrum from dead‑simple to feature‑packed, all viable for a solo operator.

1. Hubspot CRM – Best Free CRM for Freelancers

HubSpot’s free CRM is the default starting point for many freelancers – and for good reason. It gives you contact management, deal pipeline, and email tracking at no cost. No credit card required.

What I loved:

  • The free tier is genuinely useful. You can manage up to 1,000 contacts, log emails, and create a simple sales pipeline.
  • Email tracking and meeting scheduling are built in.
  • The mobile app is solid – I could log a call or update a deal on the go.

What I didn’t love:

  • Once you outgrow the free plan, pricing jumps fast. The Starter plan ($20/month) adds automation, but the next tier (Professional at $90/month) is overkill for most solopreneurs.
  • Customization is limited on free. You can’t rename deal stages or add custom fields without paying.
  • The UI can feel cluttered – HubSpot wants you to upgrade, so it shows you greyed‑out features everywhere.

Best for: Freelancers who are new to CRM and want a low‑risk, zero‑cost way to organize contacts and track deals. If you’re just starting out, HubSpot’s free plan is a no‑brainer.

2. Pipedrive – Best for Sales‑Focused Freelancers

Pipedrive is built around a visual sales pipeline. It’s designed for people who think in stages: lead → contacted → proposal sent → closed. If you’re a freelancer who wants to move deals through a funnel, this is your tool.

What I loved:

  • The drag‑and‑drop pipeline is intuitive. I could see exactly where each deal stood.
  • Activity reminders and email templates save time.
  • The “Goals” feature helps you track monthly revenue targets – motivating for solopreneurs.

What I didn’t love:

  • No native email client. It integrates with Gmail/Outlook, but you can’t send/receive emails from within Pipedrive without a paid add‑on (Pipedrive Campaigns).
  • The free trial is only 14 days, and the cheapest paid plan is $14.90/month (Essential) – reasonable, but the features you really want (automation, custom fields) require the Advanced plan at $27.90/month.
  • Reporting is basic on lower tiers.

Best for: Freelancers who live by their sales pipeline – consultants, coaches, or anyone who tracks multiple leads through a structured process. Pipedrive keeps you focused on closing.

3. Freshsales (by Freshworks) – Best All‑Around Value

Freshsales is the sweet spot between affordability and features. It’s part of the Freshworks suite, and its AI‑powered lead scoring (Freddy AI) is genuinely useful for solopreneurs who want to prioritize hot leads.

What I loved:

  • Built‑in phone and email. You get a local phone number and email integration without extra apps.
  • AI lead scoring works well – it learns from your past closed deals to rank new leads.
  • The free plan includes 500 contacts, pipeline management, and mobile app. Paid plans start at $9/user/month (Growth) – the best value for solo users.

What I didn’t love:

  • The interface can feel busy. There are many tabs and settings – it took me a couple of days to find everything.
  • Automation (workflows) is limited on the free plan. You need the Growth plan for multi‑step workflows.
  • Some integrations (like Zapier) require a paid plan.

Best for: Freelancers who want an all‑in‑one tool with AI assistance, phone, and email – without paying enterprise prices. Freshsales is my top pick for most solopreneurs.

4. Zoho CRM – Best for Customization on a Budget

Zoho CRM is the Swiss Army knife of CRMs. It’s packed with features – custom modules, blueprints, workflows, and even a built‑in AI assistant (Zia). And it’s affordable, starting at $14/user/month (Standard) with a free tier for up to 3 users.

What I loved:

  • Unmatched customization. You can create custom fields, modules, and even set up approval processes.
  • Zia (the AI) can predict deal closure probability and suggest next steps.
  • Integrates with the entire Zoho ecosystem (Books, Invoice, Projects) – great if you already use Zoho apps.

What I didn’t love:

  • Steep learning curve. The interface is dense, and setting up automation can be confusing.
  • The free tier is limited to 20,000 records (but only 3 users – fine for solopreneurs).
  • Mobile app is less polished than competitors.

Best for: Freelancers who need a CRM that can bend to their exact workflow – especially if you’re technical or willing to invest time in setup. Zoho scales beautifully as you add team members later.

5. Streak – Best CRM for Gmail Users

Streak turns your Gmail inbox into a CRM. No new interface, no extra tabs – it lives inside Gmail. If you spend all day in email, this is the most lightweight option.

What I loved:

  • Zero learning curve. You tag emails as deals, create pipelines, and track everything from your inbox.
  • The free plan includes basic CRM features (up to 500 emails/day, 10 pipelines).
  • Shared pipelines (for when you later hire a VA) are easy to set up.

What I didn’t love:

  • It’s Gmail‑only. No Outlook support.
  • Limited reporting and no native invoicing or phone.
  • The free plan has a limit of 10 pipelines – fine for most freelancers, but you might hit it quickly if you manage multiple projects.

Best for: Freelancers who live in Gmail and don’t want to learn a new tool. Streak is perfect for solo consultants, writers, or designers who just need simple deal tracking.

6. Copper – Best for Google Workspace Power Users

Copper (formerly ProsperWorks) is designed to integrate deeply with Google Workspace. It syncs contacts, emails, calendar events, and files automatically. If you’re all‑in on Google, Copper feels like a native extension.

What I loved:

  • smooth Google integration. Contacts sync both ways, and you can log emails without manual effort.
  • Beautiful, clean interface – one of the best‑looking CRMs I tested.
  • Workflow automation is available on the Basic plan ($23/user/month) – you can automate follow‑ups and task creation.

What I didn’t love:

  • No free plan (only 14‑day trial). The cheapest plan is $23/month – pricey for a solo freelancer.
  • Reporting is basic. You’ll need the Professional plan ($69/month) for advanced analytics.
  • Limited customization compared to Zoho or HubSpot.

Best for: Freelancers who rely heavily on Google Workspace (Gmail, Calendar, Drive) and want a CRM that feels like a natural part of their workflow. Ideal if you have a bit more budget for a polished experience.

7. Monday.com CRM – Best for Visual Project + CRM Hybrid

Monday.com started as a project management tool, but its CRM module is surprisingly robust. It’s highly visual – think colourful boards, columns, and automations. For freelancers who manage both projects and clients in one place, this is a strong contender.

What I loved:

  • Extremely flexible. You can create boards for leads, deals, projects, tasks – all in one workspace.
  • Automation is easy to set up (e.g., “when status changes to ‘won’, create an invoice”).
  • Beautiful interface and mobile app.

What I didn’t love:

  • Pricing per user adds up. The CRM plan starts at $12/user/month, but the real value (automations, integrations) requires the Pro plan at $19/user/month.
  • Overkill for simple contact management. If you only need a basic pipeline, Monday.com might be too much.
  • Learning curve for building your own boards – it’s not as plug‑and‑play as Streak or HubSpot.

Best for: Freelancers who want an all‑in‑one workspace for client projects and CRM. Great for creative agencies, web developers, or anyone who needs to track deliverables alongside deals.

Buying Guide: How to Choose the Right CRM for Your Freelance Business

You’ve seen the options. Now, how do you pick? Here are the key factors to consider:

1. Budget

  • $0/month – HubSpot (free tier) or Streak (free tier) work well for under 500 contacts.
  • Under $15/month – Freshsales Growth ($9/mo) or Zoho Standard ($14/mo) give you the most features.
  • $15–$25/month – Pipedrive Advanced ($27.90/mo) or Copper Basic ($23/mo) offer more automation.
  • Over $25/month – Only consider if you need advanced reporting or multiple users.

2. Number of Contacts

Most free plans cap at 500–1,000 contacts. If you have a large database, you’ll need a paid plan. Zoho’s free tier allows 20,000 records, which is generous.

3. Required Integrations

  • Email – Do you need native email (Freshsales, Copper) or are you fine with sync (Pipedrive, HubSpot)?
  • Calendar – Most integrate with Google Calendar. Streak and Copper are tightest with Gmail.
  • Invoicing – If you need invoicing, look at Freshsales (integrates with FreshBooks) or Zoho (native Zoho Books).
