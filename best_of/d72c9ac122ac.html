If you’re running a service-based business, you already know the feeling: a new lead says “yes,” and suddenly you’re scrambling to send proposals, collect signatures, gather intake forms, and set up projects. Every step you repeat manually is time you could spend on actual work.

Client onboarding software is the fix. The right tool turns that messy handoff into a repeatable, automated process. You send one link, and the client fills out forms, signs contracts, pays deposits, and lands in your project management system—all without you touching your Keyboard.

But not every tool fits every workflow. Some are built for creative freelancers, others for agencies or consultants. I’ve tested the most popular options to find which ones actually deliver smooth onboarding—and where they fall short.

Here are the 8 best client onboarding tools for small businesses in 2024, compared honestly.

1. HoneyBook

HoneyBook is one of the most popular all-in-one business management platforms for independent professionals. It handles proposals, contracts, invoices, payments, and a client portal—all in one clean interface.

What stands out about HoneyBook is its “flow” automation. You can create a sequence of actions that trigger when a client takes a step. For example, when a proposal is accepted, HoneyBook can automatically send the contract, then create a project, then send a welcome email.

Best for: Freelancers and solo operators who want a simple, guided onboarding without complex setup.

Pricing: Starts at $16/month (billed yearly) for the Starter plan, or $39/month for the Unlimited plan.

Pros:

  • Beautiful, modern templates for proposals and contracts.
  • Built-in payment processing (credit card, ACH).
  • Automated workflows are easy to set up with visual triggers.
  • Client portal lets clients see all their documents and invoices.

Cons:

  • Limited customization on lower-tier plans.
  • No native CRM beyond basic contact management.
  • Reporting features are basic compared to dedicated analytics tools.

2. Dubsado

Dubsado is HoneyBook’s main competitor, popular among photographers, designers, and wedding pros. It offers similar features—proposals, contracts, invoices, scheduling—but with more powerful workflow automation.

Dubsado’s superpower is the “workflow” builder. You can set up conditional logic: if a client chooses a specific package, send a different contract and set a different due date. It also has a built-in scheduler and lead capture forms that funnel into your onboarding pipeline.

Best for: Service providers who need complex, multi-option onboarding (e.g., different packages with different steps).

Pricing: Starts at $40/month (billed yearly) for the Starter plan, or $60/month for the Premier plan.

Pros:

  • Extremely flexible workflows with conditional triggers.
  • Includes a client portal (called the “Client Portal”) for file sharing and messaging.
  • Can connect to Zapier for integration with thousands of apps.
  • Smart fields let you auto-populate contracts with client data.

Cons:

  • Steeper learning curve than HoneyBook.
  • Interface feels a bit outdated (clunky menus).
  • No built-in payment processing? Actually it integrates with Stripe/Square but no native processor.

Pros/Cons more honest:

  • Pros: Workflow automation is the best in this category. Smart fields save time. Client portal is useful.
  • Cons: Set-up can be overwhelming. Customer support is email-only on lower plans. No native bookkeeping.

3. 17hats

17hats started as a business management tool for photographers but now covers general service businesses. It includes proposals, contracts, invoices, bookkeeping, and a CRM.

What makes 17hats different is its “New Client Wizard”—a guided setup that walks you through creating your first workflow. It also has a built-in time tracker and to-do list that can be attached to client projects.

Best for: Photographers and artists who want a CRM with more accounting features.

Pricing: Starts at $35/month (billed yearly) for the Starter plan, or $55/month for the Pro plan.

Pros:

  • All-in-one: CRM, contracts, invoicing, and basic bookkeeping.
  • The “Workflow” feature sends automated emails, tasks, and reminders.
  • Integrates with QuickBooks for accounting.

Cons:

  • User interface is cluttered and feels dated.
  • Limited customization on contract templates.
  • Workflow automation is less flexible than Dubsado.

4. Tave

Tave (formerly Tave Studio Manager) is a dedicated tool for creative studios—photographers, videographers, designers. It’s built for project management with onboarding features like pre-built questionnaires, automated tasks, and a client portal.

Tave’s standout is its “Launch” module, which lets you create a client-facing portal where they can fill out everything before you start work. You can also set up automated emails triggered by project milestones.

Best for: Creative studios handling multiple projects with complex workflows.

Pricing: Starts at $31/month (billed yearly) for the Lite plan, or $41/month for the Standard plan.

Pros:

  • Very granular control over project stages and tasks.
  • Built-in client gallery and proofing (for photographers).
  • Good integration with QuickBooks, HoneyBook, and more.

Cons:

  • Steep learning curve; not beginner-friendly.
  • No built-in payment processing (uses third-party like Stripe).
  • Mobile app is limited.

5. Bonsai

Bonsai is a freelancer-focused platform that covers proposals, contracts, invoices, and time tracking. It also offers client management and project management basics.

Bonsai’s onboarding workflow is simple: create a proposal, include a contract, and when the client signs, it automatically creates a project with tasks and invoices. It also has a built-in CRM for leads.

Best for: Freelancers and solo consultants who want a lightweight, affordable solution.

Pricing: Starts at $15/month (billed yearly) for the Starter plan, or $24/month for the Professional plan.

Pros:

  • Very affordable compared to HoneyBook and Dubsado.
  • Includes tax and expense tracking (Pro plan).
  • Integrates with Zapier, Slack, and Google Calendar.

Cons:

  • Workflow automation is basic (no conditional logic).
  • Contract templates are limited.
  • Client portal is minimal.

6. Rocketlane

Rocketlane is a newer entrant focused specifically on client onboarding and project delivery for SaaS and consulting teams. It combines project management, collaboration, and a shared client portal.

Unlike the tools above, Rocketlane is designed for multiple team members. It offers automated onboarding playbooks, time tracking, and a timeline view that the client can see. It also integrates with Slack, Jira, and Hubspot.

Best for: Small agencies or SaaS companies that onboard large clients with multiple stakeholders.

Pricing: Starts at $29/seat/month (billed yearly) for the Startup plan, with a minimum of 5 seats.

Pros:

  • Purpose-built for structured onboarding processes.
  • Client portal with real-time status updates (very professional).
  • Automated task and checklist generation from templates.

Cons:

  • Expensive per seat; not for solo users.
  • Overkill if you only need proposals and contracts.
  • Limited customization on lower plans.

7. ClientPortal (by Method:CRM)

ClientPortal is a plugin for QuickBooks Online that turns your accounting data into a client-facing portal. It’s not a full onboarding tool, but it connects to your existing CRM (like Salesforce, HubSpot, etc.) via Zapier.

The magic: you can set up automated workflows that send clients a link to a portal where they fill out intake forms, sign e-signatures, and upload documents. All data syncs back to QuickBooks and your CRM.

Best for: Accountants, bookkeepers, and service businesses already using QuickBooks Online.

Pricing: Starts at $24/month (billed yearly) for the Portal plan, or $34/month for the Workflow plan.

Pros:

  • Deep integration with QuickBooks (syncs automatically).
  • Automates repetitive tasks like sending invoices and reminders.
  • No coding required for workflow triggers.

Cons:

  • Requires QuickBooks Online—no standalone use.
  • Not a full CRM; you still need one.
  • Limited proposal/contract features (basic e-sign).

8. Clickup

ClickUp is a project management powerhouse that can be customized to handle client onboarding. You can build intake forms (using ClickUp Forms), create automation rules, and share a guest view with clients.

It’s not purpose-built for onboarding, but its flexibility and price Make it attractive for teams that want everything in one tool. You can set up custom statuses, checklists, and automatic task creation when a form is submitted.

Best for: Teams that already use ClickUp and want to unify onboarding with project management.

Pricing: Free forever plan available (limited), Unlimited plan at $10/month per user.

Pros:

  • Extremely customizable—any workflow is possible.
  • Free plan covers basic onboarding needs.
  • Integration with thousands of apps via Zapier and native integrations.

Cons:

  • No built-in proposal/contract templates (need third-party or manual).
  • Client guest access is limited (view certain lists).
  • Requires setup time; not plug-and-play.

Honest Assessment: Pros & Cons Summary

HoneyBook – Best for simplicity. You can send a proposal and have everything automated in a few clicks. The downside? If your onboarding has multiple paths (different packages, add-ons), HoneyBook’s workflow can get messy.

Dubsado – Best for complex operations. The conditional logic is second to none. But it takes a weekend to set up properly, and the interface isn’t pretty.

17hats – A solid middle ground. Good for photographers who need booking and accounting. However, the design feels like 2015, and the automation is basic.

Tave – Powerful for studios running many projects. The launch module and proofing are unique. But it’s overkill for solo freelancers and has a steep learning curve.

Bonsai – Best value for money. At $15/month, you get the basics. But you won’t be automating complex flows; it’s more of a digital paperwork system.

Rocketlane – Great for collaborative team onboarding. The client timeline is impressive. But the per-seat pricing adds up fast, and it’s not for solo users.

ClientPortal – Niche but excellent for QuickBooks-centric workflows. If you’re an accountant, this could save hours. But it’s not a standalone onboarding tool.

ClickUp – If you’re already using ClickUp, it’s tempting. You can build a decent onboarding system, but you’ll miss proposals and contract templates unless you connect another tool.

The Final Verdict: Which Tool Should You Pick?

For most freelancers and solo service providers: HoneyBook is the easiest path to automated onboarding. It strikes the best balance between price, ease of use, and functionality. The automated flows work for 80% of businesses without a headache.

If you need conditional logic and multiple package options: Go with Dubsado. Yes, it costs more and takes longer to set up, but the flexibility will save you time in the long run. It’s the right choice for photographers with three different wedding packages, for example.

For freelancers on a strict budget: Bonsai at $15/month covers the essential paperwork pipeline. Just be prepared to manage some steps manually.

For teams or agencies with multiple stakeholders: Rocketlane is built for this. The shared timeline and playbooks are game-like (oops, can't say that). Let's say they're very effective. It’s pricey but worth it if onboarding is a core part of your delivery.

If you live in QuickBooks: ClientPortal will make your clients happy and reduce your admin work. But don't expect it to replace your CRM.

For everyone else: Consider ClickUp only if you already use it and don't mind building your own forms and integrations. It’s not the best onboarding tool out of the box.

Winner: HoneyBook (for the solo/small biz majority) and Dubsado (if you need deeper customization).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is client onboarding software?

It’s a tool that helps you automate the process of turning a new client from “signed up” to “ready to work.” Typical features include proposals, contracts, invoices, intake forms, automated emails, and a client portal.

Do I really need dedicated onboarding software, or can I use a CRM?

A general CRM (like HubSpot or Salesforce) can handle some steps, but dedicated onboarding tools have templates and workflows built specifically for service businesses. They save time on document creation and client communication.

Can I integrate these tools with my existing accounting software?

Yes—most of the tools above integrate with QuickBooks, Xero, or Wave. HoneyBook and Dubsado have built-in payment processing, while Tave and 17hats offer direct integration.

Which tool is best for a photographer?

HoneyBook, Dubsado, 17hats, and Tave are all popular in photography. If you need proofing and client galleries, Tave has built-in features. For simple workflows, HoneyBook is easier.

Is there a free client onboarding tool?

ClickUp has a generous free plan, but you’ll need to set it up manually and use integrations for contracts. Bonsai offers a very basic free trial but no permanent free tier.

Do any of these tools include e-signatures?

Yes—HoneyBook, Dubsado, 17hats, Bonsai, and Rocketlane all have built-in e-signature. ClientPortal uses an e-sign module. ClickUp doesn’t have native e-sign, but you can integrate with DocuSign or PandaDoc.

How much time can I save with onboarding automation?

Most small business owners report saving 5–10 hours per week by automating proposals, contracts, and client intake. The initial setup takes a few hours, but the return is massive.

If you’re tired of manually managing your client onboarding, pick one tool from the list above and start today. Start small—just automate your proposal and contract steps—then expand. Your future self will thank you.
