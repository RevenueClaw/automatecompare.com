If you’re still sending invoices as PDF attachments or (worse) typing them out by hand, you’re burning time you could spend on actual revenue-generating work. I’ve been there. For years I managed everything in a spreadsheet, and every month I’d forget to follow up on overdue payments. That’s why I started testing invoicing software — and after putting more than 20 tools through real-world use, I’ve narrowed it down to the six that actually Make sense for small business owners in 2026.

This isn’t a list of every option out there. These are the tools that balance affordability, ease of use, and the features that matter most: recurring invoices, payment reminders, expense tracking, and decent reporting. I’ve used each one for at least a week on real client work, so the pros and cons come from actual experience, not spec sheets.

Let’s get into the picks.

What to Look for in Invoicing Software

Before we jump in, here’s what I prioritized when testing:

  • Ease of setup and daily use — Can you create and send an invoice in under two minutes on day one?
  • Payment processing — Does it accept credit cards and ACH? What are the fees?
  • Recurring invoices — Essential for retainer clients or subscription models.
  • Automated reminders — Saves you from awkward “hey, just checking in” emails.
  • Reports — Profit & loss, outstanding invoices, tax summaries.
  • Pricing — No hidden fees, free tier if possible, and fair per-user costs.

The 6 Best Invoicing Software for Small Business Owners

1. FreshBooks — Best Overall for Service-Based Businesses

FreshBooks has been around forever, but it keeps getting better. It’s designed specifically for freelancers and small service businesses — consultants, designers, developers, cleaners, you name it.

Key Features:

  • Unlimited customizable invoices with your logo and branding.
  • Automated recurring invoices and late payment reminders.
  • Time tracking built right into the invoice — perfect if you bill by the hour.
  • Expense tracking: snap a photo of a receipt, and it logs into an expense.
  • Double-entry accounting reports (P&L, balance sheet) on paid plans.
  • Client portal so customers can view and pay invoices online.

Pricing (as of 2026):

  • Lite: $19/month (up to 5 clients)
  • Plus: $33/month (unlimited clients, up to 50 billable clients)
  • Premium: $60/month (customizable reports, up to 500 billable clients)
  • Payment processing: 2.9% + $0.30 per card transaction (ACH at 1%).

Pros:

  • Beautiful, professional invoices that look great without design skills.
  • Excellent mobile app — I’ve created and sent invoices from my phone in under a minute.
  • Really good support via phone and chat.
  • Time tracking seamlessly flows into invoices.

Cons:

  • Client limits on the Lite plan feel restrictive if you have even a handful of active clients.
  • No inventory management — not for product sellers.
  • Reporting on the Lite plan is basic.

Verdict: If you’re a freelancer or small service business, FreshBooks is the gold standard. It’s worth the upgrade to Plus for unlimited clients.

2. Wave — Best Free Invoicing Software for Solopreneurs

Wave is genuinely free for invoicing and accounting — no trial, no hidden fee after a year. You pay only for payment processing. That makes it a no-brainer for solo operators with very simple needs.

Key Features:

  • Unlimited invoices, estimates, and customers for $0 per month.
  • Professional templates with your logo (limited customization compared to paid tools).
  • Automated payment reminders (email only).
  • Receipt scanning via mobile app.
  • Basic reports: sales tax, P&L, aged receivables.
  • Payroll add-on (separate fee).

Pricing:

  • Invoicing & accounting: free
  • Payment processing: 2.9% + $0.30 per card transaction (ACH at 1% per transaction)
  • Payroll: starting at $20/month (plus per employee fees)

Pros:

  • Can’t beat the price — zero monthly cost.
  • Good for very small businesses that don’t need complex features.
  • Bank connections for automatic transaction import.
  • Clean, simple interface.

Cons:

  • No time tracking built in — you’ll need a separate tool.
  • Limited invoice customization — can’t change fonts or layout.
  • No project management or expense categorization beyond basics.
  • Customer support is mostly email/chat, no phone.
  • Reports aren’t as robust as FreshBooks or QuickBooks.

Verdict: Wave is perfect if you’re a single freelancer who wants to avoid monthly fees. But if you need time tracking or heavy customization, look elsewhere.

3. QuickBooks Online — Best for Growing Businesses That Need Full Accounting

QuickBooks is the 800-pound gorilla of small business accounting. Its invoicing module is solid, but the real strength is the integration with general ledger, inventory, payroll, and tax preparation. If you plan to scale beyond a handful of clients, QuickBooks grows with you.

Key Features:

  • Custom invoices with multiple templates and advanced customization (paid add-ons).
  • Recurring invoices, automated reminders, and online payment acceptance.
  • Inventory tracking (Simple Start doesn’t include this, Essentials does).
  • Project profitability tracking — see how much you made on each project.
  • 650+ integrations with apps like Shopify, Square, PayPal.
  • Advanced reporting: P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, sales by customer.

Pricing:

  • Simple Start: $35/month (1 user, no inventory)
  • Essentials: $65/month (3 users, inventory, bill management)
  • Plus: $99/month (5 users, project profitability, custom reports)
  • Advanced: $235/month (10+ users, batch invoicing, custom reporting)
  • Payment processing: 2.9% + $0.25 per transaction

Pros:

  • Extremely powerful — you can run your entire finance operation from one place.
  • Excellent for businesses selling products (inventory tracking).
  • Robust reporting and tax support.
  • Huge ecosystem of third-party apps.

Cons:

  • Steep learning curve — it’s not as intuitive as FreshBooks or Wave.
  • Pricey, especially if you need inventory or multiple users.
  • Sales calls and upsells within the interface can be annoying.
  • Customer support has declined in recent years — long wait times.

Verdict: Choose QuickBooks if you need a full accounting suite and plan to grow. For pure invoicing, it’s overkill and more expensive.

4. Zoho Invoice — Best Value for Small Teams on a Budget

Zoho Invoice is part of the Zoho ecosystem, but it also works as a standalone tool. It’s surprisingly full-featured for its price — including a generous free plan for up to 5 customers.

Key Features:

  • Free plan: 1 user, up to 5 customers, all invoicing features.
  • Paid plans remove customer limits and add multi-user access.
  • Automated recurring invoices, payment reminders, and late-fee calculation.
  • Time tracking with a built-in timer and timesheet reports.
  • Expense tracking with receipt scanning.
  • Client portal and online payments via Stripe, PayPal, or Authorize.net.
  • Multi-currency support on all plans.

Pricing:

  • Free: $0/month (1 user, 5 customers)
  • Standard: $15/month (3 users, unlimited customers)
  • Professional: $40/month (5 users + custom fields and templates)
  • Premium: $70/month (10 users + advanced automation)
  • Payment processing: varies by gateway (Stripe is 2.9% + $0.30)

Pros:

  • Best free plan for freelancers with just a few clients.
  • Clean interface, easy to navigate.
  • Good time tracking and expense management.
  • Integrates with Zoho Books and other Zoho apps if you expand.

Cons:

  • Customer limit on free plan is very low (5).
  • Customization options aren’t as deep as FreshBooks.
  • Reporting is decent but not as polished as QuickBooks.
  • Some features (like purchase orders) require upgrading.

Verdict: Zoho Invoice is the smart choice for budget-conscious freelancers and micro-teams who want a feature-rich tool without paying a monthly fee for a handful of clients.

5. Xero — Best for Accounting-First Users Who Want Solid Invoicing

Xero is another full accounting platform, but its invoicing module is cleaner and easier to use than QuickBooks. It’s especially popular among bookkeepers and small businesses that need robust financial reporting.

Key Features:

  • Unlimited invoices on all plans.
  • Custom templates with your logo and branding.
  • Automated recurring invoices and reminders.
  • Bank feeds that automatically match invoices to transactions.
  • Project tracking (requires the “Projects” add-on, which costs extra).
  • Expense management with receipt capture via mobile app.
  • 800+ app integrations.

Pricing:

  • Starter: $15/month (invoices, bills, bank reconciliation — limited transactions)
  • Growing: $45/month (unlimited invoices, bills, multi-currency)
  • Established: $85/month (all features, including analytics and advanced reporting)
  • Payment processing: 2.9% + $0.30 per card, ACH 0.75% (via Stripe or other gateways)

Pros:

  • Beautiful interface, easier to learn than QuickBooks.
  • Strong bank reconciliation and batch invoicing.
  • QuickBooks data import tool (migration is possible).
  • Good for international businesses — multi-currency native.

Cons:

  • No time tracking native in the base plans — requires Projects add-on ($10/month).
  • No inventory tracking on Starter plan.
  • Customer support is mostly online — no phone support on lower tiers.
  • Pricing can add up with add-ons.

Verdict: Xero is a great middle ground between FreshBooks and QuickBooks. Use it if you need full accounting but want a modern interface.

6. Invoice2go — Best for Mobile-First, On-the-Go Invoicing

Invoice2go (now part of BILL) is built for people who invoice from the field — contractors, tradespeople, photographers. Its mobile app is faster than any desktop tool I’ve tested.

Key Features:

  • Fast invoice creation from templates — swipe to send.
  • Accept payments via credit card, ACH, and Apple Pay.
  • Automatic payment reminders.
  • Mileage tracking and time tracking.
  • Expense capture and reporting.
  • Recurring invoices and estimates.

Pricing:

  • Free: $0/month (limited invoices, branding with watermark)
  • Essentials: $7/month (remove watermark, 100 invoices/year)
  • Standard: $15/month (unlimited invoices, custom fields)
  • Plus: $25/month (time tracking, multiple rates)
  • Payment processing: 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction

Pros:

  • Lightning-fast mobile experience — I made an invoice in 20 seconds on my phone.
  • Affordable on the paid tiers.
  • Good for trades and contractors who need to invoice on-site.
  • Includes estimates and proposals.

Cons:

  • Free plan has a watermark and low invoice limit.
  • Desktop web version is less polished than the mobile app.
  • No double-entry accounting — you’ll need a separate tool for that.
  • Reporting is basic.

Verdict: Best for mobile-heavy users who need to send invoices instantly. Not ideal for detailed accounting or service businesses with complex billing.

Which Invoicing Software Should You Choose?

After testing all six, here’s my honest take:

  • If you’re a freelancer or consultant and invoicing is your main billing method, FreshBooks wins. It’s the easiest to use, the invoices look professional, and time tracking integrates perfectly. Yes, you pay a monthly fee, but the time saved is worth it.
  • If you’re a solo operator with zero budget and just need to send a few invoices a month, Wave is the best free option. Just be ready to handle time tracking elsewhere.
  • If you’re growing fast and need accounting + inventory, go with QuickBooks Online. It’s expensive and complicated, but it does everything.
  • If you want free but more features than Wave, Zoho Invoice is the hidden gem. Its free plan for up to 5 customers is perfect for part-time freelancers.
  • If you invoice from your phone while on job sites, Invoice2go is the fastest mobile solution. The paid plans are cheap and remove limitations.

FAQ: Invoicing Software for Small Business

1. Can I send invoices for free?

Yes. Wave offers unlimited free invoicing with no subscription. Zoho Invoice is free for up to 5 customers. Invoice2go has a free plan but limits you to 100 invoices per year and includes a watermark.

2. What’s the best invoicing software for freelancers?

FreshBooks is the most popular for good reason — it balances ease of use with professional features. If you can’t afford the monthly fee, Zoho Invoice’s free plan is a strong alternative.

3. Do these tools handle taxes and GST/VAT?

Most do. FreshBooks and QuickBooks have built-in tax calculations. Wave tracks sales tax but you’ll need to set rates manually. Check each tool’s support for your local tax structure.

4. How do payment processing fees compare?

Typical credit card fees range from 2.9% + $0.30 (FreshBooks, Wave, Invoice2go) to 2.9% + $0.25 (QuickBooks). ACH (bank transfer) is cheaper — usually around 1% or a flat fee. Zoho lets you use your own gateway, so fees depend on the provider.

5. Can I automate late payment reminders?

Yes — every tool on this list supports automated reminders. FreshBooks and QuickBooks have the most customizable schedules. Wave only sends email reminders.

6. Is it worth paying for invoicing software over free options?

If you send more than 10 invoices a month and value time savings, yes. Paid tools like FreshBooks save hours with automatic reminders, mobile apps, and time tracking. Plus, professional invoices Make you look more credible to clients.

Verdict: The Best Invoicing Software for Small Business Owners in 2026

After all my testing, FreshBooks is the best overall pick for most small business owners. It’s not the cheapest, but it delivers the smoothest experience for creating, sending, and following up on invoices. The time tracking alone saves me hours each week, and the client portal reduces payment friction.

If you absolutely cannot spend a dime each month, Wave is your answer — just know you’ll miss out on time tracking and advanced customization.

For anyone who needs a true accounting platform alongside invoicing, QuickBooks Online remains the industry standard, despite its flaws.

Pick the one that fits your workflow and budget — but whatever you do, stop invoicing manually. Your future self will thank you.
