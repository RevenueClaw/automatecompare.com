Let’s be real for a second: invoicing is the least fun part of running a small business. But it’s also the part where money actually shows up. If you’re still typing out invoices in Word or cobbling together PDFs by hand, you’re wasting hours you could spend on literally anything else.

I’ve tested, used, and sometimes screamed at over a dozen invoicing platforms this year. Some impressed me. Others made me wonder how they’re still in business. Below are the six that actually earned a spot on my shortlist.

What Makes Great Invoicing Software for Small Business?

Before we get into the list, here’s what I looked for:

  • Ease of setup and daily use – If it takes a PhD to send a bill, I’m out.
  • Fair pricing – No hidden fees, no forced upgrades to get basic features.
  • Payment processing – Clients should be able to pay in one click.
  • Automation – Recurring invoices, payment reminders, late fees.
  • Reporting – You need to know who owes you money and when.

1. FreshBooks – Best All-Around for Freelancers

FreshBooks has been around forever, and there’s a reason for that. It just works. The interface is clean, the mobile app is solid, and it handles the whole workflow from estimate to payment.

What I liked:

  • Automated payment reminders actually work. You set it once and forget it.
  • The “time tracking” feature is baked in. If you bill by the hour, this saves major headaches.
  • Client portal lets customers see their full invoice history and pay without logging in every time.

What could be better:

  • The $19/mo plan only covers 5 billable clients. That’s tight if you’re growing.
  • Reporting is basic compared to QuickBooks. You won’t get deep financial insights.

Pricing: $19, $33, $60 per month. Check FreshBooks ->

Verdict: If you’re a freelancer or agency billing 10+ clients per month and want zero friction, FreshBooks is your pick.

2. Wave – Best Free Option (With Trade-offs)

Wave is the unicorn of invoicing: actually free. No hidden charges for sending invoices, no limits on clients. You only pay when someone pays you by credit card.

What I liked:

  • Unlimited invoices, unlimited customers. At zero cost.
  • Bank connection for reconciliation. Your invoices sync with your transactions.
  • Receipt scanning via mobile app is surprisingly accurate.

What could be better:

  • Customer support is almost nonexistent on the free plan. Good luck getting help fast.
  • Payment processing fees are slightly higher than competitors (2.9% + $0.60 vs. 2.9% + $0.30).
  • No project management or time tracking built in.

Pricing: Free, plus processing fees. Check Wave ->

Verdict: Perfect for solo operators or side hustlers who send fewer than 20 invoices per month and don’t need hand-holding.

3. QuickBooks Online – Best for Accounting-Heavy Businesses

QuickBooks is the 800-pound gorilla. It’s not the most user-friendly tool on the list, but if you need real accounting (inventory, payroll, tax categories), it’s the only serious option.

What I liked:

  • In-depth reporting. Profit and loss, balance sheets, tax summaries.
  • Recurring invoices are reliable and easy to set up.
  • Integrates with hundreds of apps (PayPal, Stripe, Shopify).

What could be better:

  • The learning curve is real. Expect to spend an afternoon setting things up.
  • Price jumps quickly. The $35 plan is the cheapest, but you’ll hit limits fast.
  • The mobile app is clunky. Editing invoices on the go is frustrating.

Pricing: $35, $62, $99 per month. Check QuickBooks ->

Verdict: Choose QuickBooks if you need full accounting software and plan to hire an accountant later. Skip it if you just need to send bills.

4. Zoho Invoice – Best Free for Micro-Businesses

Zoho Invoice is the best “actually free” option if you have fewer than five customers. It’s part of the Zoho ecosystem, so if you already use Zoho apps, it integrates instantly.

What I liked:

  • Clean, simple interface. You can send an invoice in under two minutes.
  • Client portal with payment history and downloadable invoices.
  • Automated late payment reminders. Set the schedule and language.

What could be better:

  • The free plan caps you at 5 customers. After that, you need Zoho Books ($15/mo).
  • Limited customization on invoice templates. You get templates, but they’re not deeply editable.
  • Payment processing only through Zoho Payments (Stripe or PayPal optional, but clunky).

Pricing: Free for up to 5 customers. Check Zoho Invoice ->

Verdict: If you have 1–4 clients and want a no-fuss free tool, Zoho Invoice is your best bet. The moment you hit client #5, switch to FreshBooks or Wave.

5. Xero – Best for Teams With Complex Billing

Xero is QuickBooks’ main competitor, and honestly, I prefer its interface. It’s cleaner, less cluttered, and better at handling multi-currency and project-based billing.

What I liked:

  • Support for unlimited users on all paid plans (unlike QuickBooks which charges per user).
  • Strong inventory tracking if you sell physical products.
  • Excellent bank reconciliation. Matches transactions automatically.

What could be better:

  • The Starter plan is $14/mo but limits you to 20 invoices and 5 bills. That’s very restrictive.
  • Customer support is 24/7 but often slow to respond during peak hours.
  • No built-in time tracking. You need a third-party app (TSheets or Harvest).

Pricing: $14, $42, $78 per month. Check Xero ->

Verdict: Great for small teams (3-10 people) who need strong accounting features but want a cleaner interface than QuickBooks. Not ideal for solo freelancers.

6. Hiveage – Best for Recurring Billing & Subscriptions

Hiveage (formerly Curdbee) is a niche player that excels at one thing: recurring invoices and subscription billing. If you have clients on monthly retainers or auto-renewing plans, Hiveage handles it elegantly.

What I liked:

  • Auto-billing with credit card or direct debit. Once you set up a recurring invoice, it runs itself.
  • Customer self-service portal. Clients can update their payment method without bugging you.
  • Multiple language and currency support. Useful if you work internationally.

What could be better:

  • The free plan lets you send invoices to only 1 customer. That’s basically a demo.
  • No mobile app. You can manage via web browser, but it’s not optimized for phones.
  • Limited integrations compared to FreshBooks or QuickBooks.

Pricing: Free (1 customer), $19, $39, $79 per month. Check Hiveage ->

Verdict: If your business runs on monthly subscriptions or recurring retainers, Hiveage is worth the look. For one-off invoices, there are better options.
