Intro

If you’re running a small business, invoicing is probably one of those tasks you know you should automate but haven’t quite gotten around to. Maybe you’re still sending PDFs from a template, manually marking payments received, or chasing clients with “just following up” emails. I’ve been there. It sucks.

The good news? The invoicing software landscape in 2026 is better than ever. There are tools that handle everything from recurring invoices and automatic payment reminders to expense tracking and multi-currency support. But with so many options, it’s easy to get overwhelmed.

I spent the last month testing the top contenders. I looked at ease of use, features, pricing, scalability, and—most importantly—how well they play with other tools you might already use (like Stripe for payments, Zapier for automation, or Hubspot for CRM). After dozens of hours of hands-on testing, I narrowed it down to six winners.

Whether you’re a solo freelancer sending five invoices a month or a growing agency with dozens of clients, there’s something here for you. Let’s find your fit.

How We Picked

I didn’t just read reviews and call it a day. I signed up for free trials (or used free tiers), created invoices, tested payment integrations, and poked around settings to see what broke and what worked beautifully.

Here’s what I evaluated each tool on:

  • Ease of use – Can a non-technical business owner set it up in under 10 minutes?
  • Core invoicing features – Recurring invoices, automatic reminders, payment links, late fees.
  • Payment processing – Does it integrate with Stripe, PayPal, Square? What are the transaction fees?
  • Integrations – Can it connect to your accounting software, CRM, email marketing, and automation tools like Zapier?
  • Scalability – Will it still work for you when you double your client base?
  • Pricing & value – Is the free tier good enough? Are paid plans worth it?
  • Support – Is there live chat, phone, or a solid knowledge base?

I also paid attention to real-world use cases. For example, if you send international invoices, currency conversion matters. If you manage projects, time tracking integration might be a must.

The 6 Best Invoicing Software for Small Business in 2026

1. FreshBooks – Best for Freelancers and Service-Based Businesses

FreshBooks has been a staple for years, and it keeps getting better. It’s designed for people who bill by the hour or project, which makes it perfect for freelancers, consultants, and creative agencies.

Key Features:

  • Unlimited invoices on all paid plans (the Lite plan allows up to 5 billable clients).
  • Built-in time tracking – log hours directly from a timer or manual entry.
  • Recurring invoices with auto-send.
  • Automatic payment reminders and late fees.
  • Accept credit cards, ACH, and bank transfers via Stripe or PayPal.
  • Expense tracking with receipt scanning.
  • Client portal where customers can view and pay invoices.

Pricing:

  • Lite: $19/month (5 billable clients)
  • Plus: $33/month (50 billable clients)
  • Premium: $60/month (unlimited clients)

All plans include unlimited invoices, expense tracking, and time tracking. Transaction fees are 2.9% + $0.30 for credit cards, 1% for ACH.

What I Liked:

  • The interface is clean and intuitive. You can create and send an invoice in under a minute.
  • Time tracking integrates smoothly with invoicing – perfect for billable hours.
  • The mobile app is excellent for on-the-go invoicing.

What Could Be Better:

  • The “billable clients” limit on lower tiers is a bit restrictive. If you have more than 5 clients, you need the Plus plan.
  • Advanced reporting is limited compared to QuickBooks or Xero.

Best For: Freelancers, solo service providers, and small agencies who need time tracking and professional invoices.

Integrations: Zapier, Stripe, PayPal, Gusto, Mailchimp, and more. You can connect FreshBooks to your email marketing (like Convertkit or Moosend) via Zapier to automatically send follow-up sequences after invoice payment. Check FreshBooks ->

2. Wave – Best Free Invoicing for Micro-Businesses

Wave is a favorite among the “I just need something simple and free” crowd. And honestly, for a free tool, it’s surprisingly powerful.

Key Features:

  • Unlimited invoices, estimates, and customers.
  • Automatic payment reminders.
  • Recurring invoices.
  • Receipt scanning (via mobile app).
  • Basic accounting reports (profit & loss, sales tax).
  • Accept credit cards and bank payments (fees apply).

Pricing:

  • Invoicing and accounting: Free.
  • Payment processing: 2.9% + $0.30 for credit cards, 1% for bank payments (no monthly fee).
  • Payroll: add-on at $20/month + per employee fees.

What I Liked:

  • It’s genuinely free – no hidden limits on invoices or customers.
  • The dashboard gives a clear snapshot of outstanding invoices and cash flow.
  • Bank connections automatically import transactions.

What Could Be Better:

  • Customer support is limited on the free plan (email only, no phone).
  • No time tracking or project management built-in.
  • The interface feels a bit dated and can be slow.
  • No multi-currency support (only USD and CAD).

Best For: Solo entrepreneurs, side hustlers, and very small businesses on a tight budget who don’t need advanced features.

Integrations: Stripe (for payments), PayPal, and a few others. You can use Zapier to connect Wave to your email marketing or CRM. For example, trigger an email sequence in Moosend when an invoice is paid. Check Wave ->

3. QuickBooks Online – Best for Growing Businesses That Need Full Accounting

QuickBooks Online is the 800-pound gorilla of small business accounting. It’s not just invoicing – it’s a full suite of accounting, expense management, inventory, and reporting. If you’re scaling and need more than just sending invoices, this is your tool.

Key Features:

  • Unlimited invoices and estimates.
  • Recurring invoices with auto-send.
  • Automatic payment reminders and late fees.
  • Expense tracking with receipt capture.
  • Inventory tracking (on higher plans).
  • Project profitability tracking.
  • 650+ integrations.
  • Robust reporting (P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, etc.).

Pricing:

  • Simple Start: $35/month (1 user)
  • Essentials: $65/month (3 users)
  • Plus: $99/month (5 users)
  • Advanced: $235/month (25 users)

Payment processing via QuickBooks Payments: 2.9% + $0.30 for swiped/dipped, 3.4% + $0.30 for keyed.

What I Liked:

  • The depth of features is unmatched. You can manage the entire financial life of your business.
  • Excellent bank reconciliation and categorization.
  • Integrates with HubSpot CRM – great for businesses that want to sync invoices with deal tracking. Check HubSpot integration ->

What Could Be Better:

  • Steep learning curve. It’s not as intuitive as FreshBooks or Wave.
  • Pricey, especially if you need more than one user.
  • Customer support can be hit-or-miss.

Best For: Small businesses that need full accounting, inventory, and advanced reporting. Ideal if you already use HubSpot or other business tools.

Integrations: HubSpot, Stripe, PayPal, Zapier, Shopify, Square, and hundreds more. Use Zapier to automate invoice creation from new deals in your CRM. Check QuickBooks Online ->

4. Xero – Best for International Invoicing and Multi-Currency

Xero is a strong competitor to QuickBooks, especially if you work with clients or vendors in different currencies. It’s cloud-native, beautifully designed, and packed with features.

Key Features:

  • Unlimited invoices and quotes.
  • Multi-currency support – send invoices in over 160 currencies.
  • Automatic bank feeds and reconciliation.
  • Recurring invoices with auto-send.
  • Expense tracking with receipt capture.
  • Project tracking and time recording.
  • Inventory management (on higher plans).

Pricing:

  • Starter: $29/month (20 invoices, 5 bills, bank reconciliation)
  • Standard: $46/month (unlimited invoices and bills)
  • Premium: $62/month (multi-currency, analytics)

Payment processing via Stripe or PayPal (transaction fees vary).

What I Liked:

  • Multi-currency support is excellent – automatic exchange rates and balance updates.
  • Clean, modern interface that’s easier to navigate than QuickBooks.
  • Strong reconciliation features – matches bank transactions to invoices.
  • 1,000+ integrations.

What Could Be Better:

  • The Starter plan caps invoices at 20 per month – that’s too low for most businesses.
  • No built-in payment processing (you need Stripe or PayPal).
  • Customer support is mostly email and chat; phone support limited.

Best For: Small businesses with international clients, or those who need multi-currency accounting without the QuickBooks complexity.

Integrations: Stripe, PayPal, Zapier, HubSpot, Salesforce, and many more. You can automate invoice reminders via Zapier when a payment is overdue. Check Xero ->

5. Zoho Invoice – Best Free Tier for Very Small Teams

Zoho Invoice is part of the larger Zoho ecosystem, but it stands on its own as a solid, free invoicing tool for micro-businesses. The free plan is generous – up to 5 customers, unlimited invoices, and basic features.

Key Features:

  • Unlimited invoices and estimates (free for up to 5 customers).
  • Recurring invoices.
  • Automatic payment reminders.
  • Time tracking and expense tracking.
  • Multi-currency support (paid plans).
  • Client portal.
  • Customizable invoice templates.

Pricing:

  • Free: up to 5 customers (unlimited invoices)
  • Standard: $15/month (unlimited customers, multi-currency, custom fields)
  • Professional: $25/month (more advanced features)

Payment processing: Stripe, PayPal, and others (fees apply).

What I Liked:

  • The free plan is genuinely useful for very small businesses with few clients.
  • Clean, simple interface.
  • Integrates with other Zoho apps (CRM, Books, Projects) if you use the ecosystem.
  • Mobile app works well.

What Could Be Better:

  • The 5-customer limit on free plan is restrictive – you’ll quickly outgrow it.
  • Reporting is basic compared to dedicated accounting tools.
  • Customer support can be slow on free plan.

Best For: Solo operators who have fewer than 5 clients and want a free, no-fuss invoicing tool.

Integrations: Stripe, PayPal, Zapier, Zoho CRM, and other Zoho apps. Use Zapier to connect Zoho Invoice to your email marketing (like Moosend) for automated follow-ups. Check Zoho Invoice ->

6. Invoice Ninja – Best Open-Source Option for Customization

Invoice Ninja is a bit different – it’s open-source and offers a free self-hosted version. If you want full control over your data and don’t mind a bit of technical setup, it’s a powerful choice.

Key Features:

  • Unlimited invoices, clients, and products.
  • Recurring invoices and auto-billing.
  • Time tracking and project management.
  • Expense tracking.
  • Multi-currency support.
  • Customizable templates (HTML/CSS).
  • API access.

Pricing:

  • Free self-hosted: $0 (you provide hosting)
  • Free cloud: up to 20 clients, limited features
  • Paid cloud plans start at $10/month (unlimited clients, more features)

Payment processing: Stripe, PayPal, Square, and many others.

What I Liked:

  • Extremely customizable – you can tweak invoice templates, workflows, and even the code.
  • No monthly fees if you self-host.
  • Great API for developers.

What Could Be Better:

  • Self-hosting requires technical know-how (or a developer).
  • The free cloud tier is limited to 20 clients.
  • Support is community-based for self-hosted users.

Best For: Tech-savvy business owners or those who need a highly customizable invoicing system with no ongoing costs.

Integrations: Stripe, PayPal, Zapier, and many gateways. You can use Zapier to connect Invoice Ninja with your CRM or email tools. Check Invoice Ninja ->

Buying Guide: How to Choose the Right Invoicing Software

Before you pick a tool, ask yourself these questions:

1. How many invoices do you send per month?

If it's under 20, a free tool like Wave or Zoho Invoice (with 5 customers) might work. If you send dozens, you need unlimited invoices – FreshBooks, QuickBooks, or Xero.

2. Do you bill by the hour or by project?

Time tracking is essential. FreshBooks and Xero have it built-in. QuickBooks has a time tracker but it's an add-on. Wave and Zoho Invoice have basic time tracking.

3. Do you need full accounting or just invoicing?

If you need profit & loss statements, balance sheets, and tax support, QuickBooks or Xero are better. If you just need to send invoices and track payments, FreshBooks or Wave suffice.

4. Do you work with international clients?

Multi-currency is a must. Xero and QuickBooks (Plus/Advanced) handle it well. FreshBooks supports it on paid plans. Wave does not.

5. What payment methods do your clients prefer?

All tools integrate with Stripe and PayPal. If you need ACH or bank transfers, FreshBooks and Wave offer those. QuickBooks has its own payment processor.

6. How important are integrations?

If you use HubSpot CRM, QuickBooks has a native integration. For Zapier automation, all six tools connect. You can set up workflows like “when invoice paid → add to ConvertKit list” or “send Moosend email sequence after invoice sent.”
