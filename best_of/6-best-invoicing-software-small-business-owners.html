Let’s be real: invoicing isn’t anyone’s favorite part of running a small business. But getting paid on time? That’s the whole point. The right invoicing software does more than just send bills—it tracks who's overdue, automates reminders, and usually handles payment processing so you don't have to chase checks.

I tested over 25 invoicing tools this year, spending actual time setting up invoices, sending them, and monitoring payments. Some were overkill for a solo freelancer. Others felt like they were designed in 2012 and never updated.

Here are the six that actually deliver for small business owners in 2026.

How I Selected These Tools

Before we jump in, here’s what I looked for:

  • Pricing that makes sense for 1-10 person teams (free tiers, flat rates, no hidden fees)
  • Payment processing built in (not just a PDF generator that sits in someone’s inbox)
  • Automation for recurring invoices (because sending the same bill every month manually is a waste of time)
  • Mobile app quality (you shouldn’t need a laptop to send an invoice from a coffee shop)
  • Reporting basics (knowing who owes you what, when, and for how long)

Not everything here is perfect. I’ll tell you where each one falls short, too.

1. FreshBooks — Best for Freelancers and Service Pros

FreshBooks has been around forever, and for good reason. It nails the basics for solo operators and small service businesses—think consultants, designers, photographers, and plumbers.

What works: The invoice builder is clean. You pick a template, add your logo, line items, and tax rates, and hit send. It takes under two minutes. Clients get a professional-looking invoice with a "Pay Now" button that takes them to a secure checkout page.

Recurring invoices are easy to set up, and automated payment reminders go out on your schedule. I like that you can set different reminder frequencies for different clients—some people need a nudge after 3 days, others at 7.

Where it stumbles: FreshBooks gets pricey fast if you have more than a handful of clients. The Lite plan ($17/month) limits you to 5 billable clients, which is nothing. The Plus plan ($30/month) bumps that to 50, but then you’re paying more than most competitors for similar features.

The mobile app is functional but not pretty. It works for sending invoices and checking payment status, but don’t try to build a complex invoice on your phone.

Best for: Freelancers with 5 or fewer active clients who want a polished invoice experience.

2. Wave — Best Free Invoicing Software (With a Catch)

Wave has been the go-to free invoicing tool for years, and it’s still one of the best options if you’re on a tight budget. You can send unlimited invoices to unlimited clients for exactly $0 per month.

What works: You can literally start invoicing in five minutes. Sign up, add your business info, create a client profile, and send your first invoice. No credit card required, no free trial expiration. It does everything a paid tool does—recurring invoices, automatic payment reminders, sales tax tracking, and receipt scanning via mobile.

The dashboard shows you exactly who's overdue and by how much. I love that you can hover over an invoice and see the payment status without clicking into it.

Where it stumbles: The catch is payment processing. Wave charges 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction for credit card payments, same as most. But bank transfers (ACH) cost 1% per transaction with a $1 minimum. That’s more expensive than some competitors like Square (which charges 1% + $0 for ACH).

Also, Wave's customer support is not great. Free users get email support only, and response times can stretch to 24-48 hours. If something breaks, you're waiting.

Best for: Freelancers and micro-businesses that send fewer than 50 invoices per month and don't need phone support.

3. Xero — Best for Growing Small Businesses

Xero started as accounting software, but its invoicing module is now good enough to stand on its own. If you need to manage invoices alongside full accounting (COGS, inventory, payroll), Xero is the right call.

What works: The invoicing workflow is fast once you learn the layout. You can create invoice templates with your logo, default payment terms, and preferred tax rates. Recurring invoices are handled through "repeat invoices" which Xero copies automatically on your schedule.

The bank reconciliation feature is where Xero shines. When a client pays an invoice, Xero matches it to the bank deposit automatically. No manual entry. That alone saves hours each month.

Where it stumbles: Xero has a learning curve. The navigation isn't as straightforward as FreshBooks or Wave. New users often struggle to find where to set up invoice numbering, payment terms, or default tax rates.

Pricing also jumps quickly. The Starter plan ($29/month) limits you to 20 invoices and quotes per month. To send unlimited invoices, you need the Standard plan ($46/month). For a tool that’s positioned as small business software, that feels steep.

Best for: Small businesses with 2-10 employees who need proper accounting, not just invoicing.

4. Zoho Invoice — Best Value for Money

Zoho Invoice is part of Zoho’s massive ecosystem of business apps, but it works just fine as a standalone invoicing tool. And here’s the kicker: it’s free for up to 5 users.

What works: For zero dollars per month (for the first five users), you get automated payment reminders, recurring invoices, client portal, time tracking, expense tracking, and project management for invoicing. You’d pay $30-$50/month for comparable features in FreshBooks or Xero.

Custom invoices are easy to design. You can add fields like PO numbers, shipping addresses, or custom notes per invoice. The client portal lets clients view their invoice history and Make payments without emailing you.

Where it stumbles: Zoho’s interface feels busy. There are tabs and options everywhere. It’s not as clean as FreshBooks or as straightforward as Wave. New users will spend time clicking around just to find where to set up a recurring invoice template.

Customer support is mostly self-service (knowledge base, forums, email). Phone support is only available on paid plans, which start at $9/month per organization. For free users, expect email-only responses.

Best for: Tiny teams (1-5 users) that want zero-cost invoicing without sacrificing features.

5. Invoice Ninja — Best for Tech-Savvy Solo Operators

Invoice Ninja is an open-source invoicing platform that gives you serious control over your billing. It’s not as polished as the others, but if you want customization, this is your tool.

What works: The free plan is generous—you get unlimited invoices and up to 20 clients. Paid plans start at $10/month. Compared to FreshBooks ($30/month for 50 clients), that’s a steal.

You can create invoice templates from scratch using HTML/CSS if you want. Or use one of the pre-built templates and customize it with drag-and-drop fields, custom fonts, and brand colors. It also supports recurring invoices, automated reminders, and partial payments.

The API is open. If you have a custom workflow (e.g., generate invoices from your CRM), Invoice Ninja plays nice.

Where it stumbles: The interface looks like it was built by developers for developers. It’s not ugly, but it’s not as polished as FreshBooks or Wave. Setting up payment gateways requires you to copy-paste API keys from Stripe or PayPal, which is fine for tech-savvy users but intimidating for anyone else.

Customer support is community-driven unless you’re on a paid plan. The free plan gets forum access and email with slower response times.

Best for: Developers, tech-savvy freelancers, or businesses that need custom invoice workflows.

6. Hiveage — Best for Subscription Businesses

Hiveage (formerly Zinvio) is built for businesses that bill the same amount every month. If you run a subscription service, membership site, or retainer-based work, Hiveage handles the billing side with minimal effort.

What works: Recurring invoices are dead simple. You set a client, a frequency (weekly, monthly, quarterly, yearly), an amount, and Hiveage auto-generates and sends invoices on that schedule. Partial payments and late fees are built in.

The client portal is better than most. Clients can log in, see their invoice history, download PDFs, update payment info, and pay without emailing you. That reduces back-and-forth significantly.

Where it stumbles: Hiveage’s design feels stuck in 2015. The dashboard is functional but not pretty. Templates are limited compared to FreshBooks or Zoho Invoice.
