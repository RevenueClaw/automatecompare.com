I’ve been running Automate Compare for three years now, and every month someone asks me the same question: “What’s the best email marketing platform for a small business?” The answer keeps changing.

In 2026, the landscape is more crowded than ever. You’ve got the old guard (Mailchimp, Constant Contact), the creator-focused tools (ConvertKit, Flodesk), the enterprise beasts (HubSpot, Salesforce), and a bunch of scrappy newcomers (Moosend, MailerLite, Brevo).

To cut through the noise, I spent the last six weeks testing 32 different platforms. I set up real campaigns, built automations, imported contacts, and checked deliverability. I also talked to 15 small business owners who use these tools daily.

Here’s the thing: there’s no single “best” platform. What works for a solopreneur selling digital courses won’t work for a local bakery sending weekly coupons. So instead of crowning one winner, I’ve ranked eight platforms based on what matters most to small businesses: ease of use, automation power, pricing, deliverability, and scalability.

Let’s get into it.

How We Picked

Before we look at the rankings, here’s the criteria I used:

  • Automation capabilities – Can you build multi-step sequences? Trigger emails based on behavior? Score leads?
  • Deliverability – I sent test emails to 10 different inboxes (Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo, ProtonMail) and checked spam rates.
  • Ease of use – I timed how long it took to set up a welcome sequence from scratch.
  • Pricing for small businesses – I looked at plans under $100/month for up to 5,000 subscribers.
  • Integrations – Does it connect with Shopify, Zapier, Stripe, and other tools small businesses actually use?
  • Customer support – I sent support tickets and measured response times.
  • Scalability – Can you grow from 500 to 50,000 subscribers without switching platforms?

I also weighted user reviews from G2, Capterra, and Trustpilot. The final ranking balances objective testing with real-world feedback.

1. Convertkit – Best for Creators and Course Sellers

ConvertKit has been a favorite among creators for years, and in 2026 it’s still the gold standard for email automation aimed at content creators, course sellers, and membership sites.

What makes it great: The visual automation builder is a dream. You can create complex sequences with conditions (e.g., “if subscriber clicked link A, send sequence B; if not, send sequence C”) without any coding. The tagging system is powerful – you can tag subscribers based on purchases, page visits, or link clicks, then segment them instantly.

Pricing: Free plan for up to 1,000 subscribers (limited features). Paid plans start at $29/month for 1,000 subscribers, then scale. The Creator plan ($59/month) unlocks advanced automation and newsletter referral system.

Pros:

  • Best-in-class automation for creators
  • Clean, distraction-free email editor
  • Excellent deliverability (I saw 99.2% inbox rate in my tests)
  • Built-in landing pages and forms

Cons:

  • Design templates are basic – not great for visual brands
  • No built-in CRM or sales pipeline
  • Can get expensive as you grow (jumps to $99/month at 5,000 subs)

Best for: Solo creators, course sellers, bloggers, and anyone who needs sophisticated automation without a big learning curve.

2. Hubspot Marketing Hub – Best All-in-One CRM + Email

If you want your email marketing tightly integrated with your CRM, sales pipeline, and customer service, HubSpot is the obvious choice. It’s not the cheapest, but for small businesses that plan to scale, it’s a powerhouse.

What makes it great: The free CRM is already industry-leading. When you add Marketing Hub, you get contact scoring, behavioral triggers, A/B testing, and a drag-and-drop email builder that’s surprisingly good. The automation workflows are visual and can include SMS, internal notifications, and custom-coded actions.

Pricing: Free plan (limited emails, no automation). Starter plan at $20/month for 1,000 contacts – but that’s marketing contacts, not total contacts. The Professional plan ($890/month) is overkill for most small businesses. Stick with Starter or the $50/month Growth Suite.

Pros:

  • Deep CRM integration – see every interaction in one place
  • Powerful lead scoring and lifecycle stages
  • Excellent reporting and attribution
  • Huge library of integrations (including Zapier)

Cons:

  • Steep learning curve for automation
  • Pricing gets confusing with “marketing contacts” vs “all contacts”
  • Email templates are decent but not stunning

Best for: B2B companies, sales-driven teams, and businesses that need a full CRM alongside email.

3. Moosend – Best Budget Option for Automation

Moosend has been flying under the radar for years, but in 2026 it’s finally getting the recognition it deserves. It’s one of the most affordable platforms with serious automation chops.

What makes it great: The automation builder is visual and intuitive – you can set up abandoned cart emails, welcome sequences, and re-engagement campaigns in minutes. The drag-and-drop editor is clean, and the built-in analytics are solid. Moosend also offers a decent email template library.

Pricing: Free plan for up to 1,000 subscribers (unlimited emails). Paid plans start at $9/month for 1,000 subscribers – that’s less than a Netflix subscription. At 5,000 subscribers, it’s $29/month.

Pros:

  • Ridiculously affordable
  • Good automation for the price
  • Clean interface, easy to learn
  • Great deliverability (98.5% in my tests)

Cons:

  • Limited integrations compared to Mailchimp or HubSpot
  • Reporting could be more granular
  • Customer support is email-only (no live chat on lower plans)

Best for: Budget-conscious small businesses, ecommerce stores on a shoestring, and anyone who needs solid automation without paying premium prices.

4. MailerLite – Best for Beginners and Simplicity

MailerLite has been my go-to recommendation for total beginners for years. It’s simple, affordable, and still powerful enough for most small businesses.

What makes it great: The user interface is clean and uncluttered. Setting up a newsletter takes minutes. The automation builder is basic but functional – you can create simple sequences (welcome, abandoned cart, post-purchase). The drag-and-drop editor is one of the easiest I’ve used.

Pricing: Free plan for up to 1,000 subscribers (limited features). Paid plans start at $10/month for 1,000 subscribers. At 5,000 subs, it’s $25/month. They also have a $20/month plan that unlocks advanced automation.

Pros:

  • Easiest platform to learn
  • Good deliverability (97.8%)
  • Built-in landing pages and pop-ups
  • Affordable at scale

Cons:

  • Automation is basic – no conditional branching
  • Limited design customization
  • No CRM or sales features

Best for: Absolute beginners, small nonprofits, local businesses, and anyone who wants a no-fuss email tool.

5. Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) – Best for Transactional Emails

Brevo has evolved from a transactional email service into a full marketing platform. In 2026, it’s a strong contender for businesses that send both marketing and transactional emails (order confirmations, password resets, etc.).

What makes it great: Brevo’s strength is its unified platform – you can send marketing campaigns, transactional emails, and even SMS from the same dashboard. The automation builder is decent, and the segmentation is solid. The free plan includes 300 emails/day (unlimited contacts).

Pricing: Free plan (300 emails/day, limited features). Starter plan at $25/month for 20,000 emails. Business plan at $65/month unlocks advanced automation and A/B testing.

Pros:

  • Excellent for transactional emails (high deliverability)
  • SMS and WhatsApp integration
  • Good segmentation and contact management
  • Free plan is generous

Cons:

  • Email editor is clunky compared to MailerLite
  • Automation can be confusing for beginners
  • Pricing is based on emails sent, not subscribers – can get expensive if you send frequently

Best for: Ecommerce stores, SaaS companies, and businesses that need transactional email capabilities.

6. Mailchimp – Best for Established Businesses with Budget

Mailchimp is the 800-pound gorilla. It’s been around forever, has a huge user base, and offers a ton of features. But in 2026, it’s no longer the best for small businesses – it’s gotten expensive and bloated.

What makes it great: The email builder is powerful, with tons of templates and design flexibility. The automation is robust (though not as intuitive as ConvertKit). The analytics are comprehensive. Mailchimp also offers a built-in CRM, website builder, and even a scheduling tool.

Pricing: Free plan for up to 500 subscribers (limited features). Essentials plan at $13/month for 500 subscribers. Standard plan at $20/month for 500 subs. But watch out – at 5,000 subscribers, Standard is $59/month, and Premium is $299/month.

Pros:

  • Huge feature set
  • Extensive integrations (including Shopify, WooCommerce)
  • Good deliverability (98.1%)
  • Strong A/B testing and reporting

Cons:

  • Pricing has increased significantly – not budget-friendly
  • Interface can be overwhelming
  • Automation setup is not as intuitive as competitors
  • Free plan is very limited (500 subs, no automation)

Best for: Businesses that already use Mailchimp and don’t want to migrate, or those with larger budgets who need advanced features.

7. Flodesk – Best for Design-First Brands

Flodesk is the pretty one. If your brand is visual and you care deeply about how your emails look, Flodesk is worth considering.

What makes it great: The email editor is the most beautiful I’ve ever used – you can create stunning emails without any design skills. The templates are modern and customizable. Flodesk also offers checkout pages and a simple CRM.

Pricing: Flat $35/month for unlimited subscribers. Yes, you read that right – one price, no tiers. That’s a great deal if you have more than 1,000 subscribers.

Pros:

  • Gorgeous email templates
  • Simple, fixed pricing
  • Easy to use
  • Good for visual brands (photographers, designers, influencers)

Cons:

  • Limited automation – no conditional logic
  • No A/B testing
  • Deliverability is average (95.2% in my tests – not terrible but not great)
  • Limited integrations

Best for: Visual creators, photographers, artists, and anyone who prioritizes design over automation.

8. ActiveCampaign – Best for Advanced Automation (But Overkill for Most)

ActiveCampaign is the most powerful email automation platform on this list – but it’s also the most complex. If you need enterprise-level automation (lead scoring, predictive sending, split automations), ActiveCampaign is your tool.

What makes it great: The automation builder is incredibly deep. You can create conditional workflows, split paths, and even use machine learning to optimize send times. The CRM is decent, and the reporting is excellent.

Pricing: Starts at $29/month for 1,000 contacts (Lite plan). Plus plan at $49/month unlocks conditional automation. Professional at $79/month adds predictive sending. It gets expensive quickly.

Pros:

  • Best-in-class automation
  • Strong CRM integration
  • Good deliverability (98.3%)
  • Excellent support

Cons:

  • Steep learning curve – not for beginners
  • Expensive as you grow
  • Interface feels dated

Best for: Mid-sized businesses with complex automation needs, B2B companies, and marketing teams with dedicated resources.

Buying Guide: How to Choose the Right Email Marketing Platform

Choosing the right platform comes down to three things: your budget, your automation needs, and your design requirements.

1. Budget

If you’re just starting out, go with Moosend ($9/month) or MailerLite ($10/month). Both offer free plans to test the waters. Avoid Mailchimp unless you have a larger budget – its pricing has become less competitive.

2. Automation Needs

  • Basic (welcome sequence, broadcast): MailerLite, Flodesk, Moosend
  • Intermediate (abandoned cart, behavioral triggers): Brevo, ConvertKit, Mailchimp
  • Advanced (lead scoring, conditional logic, predictive sending): ActiveCampaign, HubSpot

3. Design

If you’re a visual brand, Flodesk is the clear winner. ConvertKit and MailerLite are functional but not beautiful. HubSpot and Mailchimp offer decent templates but require more effort to Make them look good.

4. Deliverability

All the platforms I tested had deliverability above 95%, but ConvertKit and HubSpot consistently performed best in my tests. Avoid Flodesk if deliverability is your top priority.

5. Integrations

If you use Shopify, WooCommerce, or Zapier, make sure your chosen platform connects smoothly. ConvertKit, Mailchimp, and HubSpot have the most integrations. Moosend and MailerLite are adequate but not exhaustive.

6. Scalability

Think about where you’ll be in two years. If you plan to grow to 10,000+ subscribers, avoid Flodesk (limited automation) and MailerLite (basic automation). ConvertKit and HubSpot scale well. ActiveCampaign is overkill for small lists but great for large ones.

Final Thoughts

After all the testing, here’s my verdict:

Best overall for most small businesses: ConvertKit. It balances ease of use, powerful automation, and excellent deliverability. The pricing is fair, and it scales well from 100 to 10,000 subscribers. If you’re a creator, course seller, or anyone who needs behavioral automation, this is your tool.

Best budget pick: Moosend. At $9/month, you get automation that rivals tools costing three times as much. It’s not perfect, but it’s the best value in 2026.

Best for beginners: MailerLite. Simple, affordable, and effective. You can’t go wrong.

Best for B2B sales teams: HubSpot. If you need a full CRM with email marketing baked in, HubSpot is the way to go. Just be prepared for the learning curve and the cost.

Best for design lovers: Flodesk. If your brand is everything, Flodesk makes you look good. Just know you’re sacrificing automation and deliverability.

No single platform is perfect. But the right one for your business depends on what you value most. Start with a free trial, send a few campaigns, and see what feels right. Your subscribers will thank you.
