Shopify vs Squarespace vs Wix: Best E-Commerce Platform for Beginners

If you've spent any time searching for a way to sell online, you've run into the same three names. Shopify, Squarespace, and Wix show up in nearly every "best ecommerce platform" list, and they all promise roughly the same thing: get a store online fast, without hiring a developer.

The problem is that they're built for different people. Pick the wrong one and you'll spend your first month fighting the tool instead of selling your product. Pick the right one and you'll wonder why anyone makes this complicated.

I've built stores on all three. Here's what actually matters when you're starting out, where each platform shines, and where each one will frustrate you.

The short version

  • Shopify is the best choice if selling is your main goal. It's the strongest dedicated ecommerce platform, but it costs more and expects you to learn its system.
  • Squarespace is the best choice if your brand's look matters as much as the products. Gorgeous templates, simple checkout, weaker on deep customization.
  • Wix is the best choice if you want maximum flexibility to build anything. It's the most open-ended, which is both its strength and its weakness.

Keep reading for the details, because "best" depends entirely on what you're selling and how you want to run it.

What beginners actually need

Before comparing features, it helps to know what trips people up in the first 90 days. In my experience, new store owners struggle with four things:

  • Getting the store live without a design degree. Templates and drag-and-drop editors matter more than people admit.
  • Setting up payments and shipping correctly. A confusing checkout kills sales.
  • Managing inventory without spreadsheets from hell. Especially once you hit 20+ products.
  • Not getting nickel-and-dimed. Monthly fees plus app subscriptions plus transaction fees add up fast.

So when I compare these platforms, I'm weighing them against those four realities — not against a feature checklist nobody uses.

Feature breakdown

Ease of setup

All three let you build a functional store in an afternoon. The differences show up in how much hand-holding you get.

Wix is the most beginner-friendly on day one. Its drag-and-drop editor (Wix Editor) lets you place elements anywhere on the page. You can also answer a few questions and let Wix's AI build a starting site for you. The tradeoff: total freedom means you can also build something ugly or broken if you don't know what you're doing.

Squarespace takes a more structured approach. You pick a template, and the layout is largely locked in — which sounds limiting but is actually a gift for beginners. You can't easily break the design. You edit content within a well-designed framework. The learning curve is gentle, and the results look professional almost by default.

Shopify sits in the middle. Its theme editor is structured like Squarespace's, but the platform assumes you're running a real store, so there's more to configure: shipping zones, tax settings, product variants, payment gateways. It's not hard, but it's more setup than the other two.

Winner for pure ease of use: Squarespace. Winner for flexibility: Wix.

Templates and design

This is where Squarespace has historically dominated, and it still does.

Squarespace's templates are genuinely well-designed. They're built by actual designers, they're responsive out of the box, and they handle typography and spacing in a way that makes non-designers look competent. There are around 100+ templates, and switching between them preserves your content.

Wix offers 800+ templates — more than anyone — but quality varies wildly. You'll find some real gems and some that look like they were built in 2012. Wix's newer AI-generated sites are hit or miss.

Shopify's theme store has free themes and premium themes that run roughly $180 to $400 one-time. The free themes (like Dawn) are solid and fast, but they're clearly "starter" designs. If you want a distinctive look, you'll likely pay for a premium theme or hire a designer.

Winner: Squarespace for design quality, Wix for sheer volume.

Product management and inventory

This is Shopify's home turf, and it's not close.

Shopify handles unlimited products, variants (size, color, material — up to 2,000 variants per product on higher plans), collections, tags, and multi-location inventory. It tracks stock across warehouses, syncs with suppliers, and integrates with tools like ShipBob and Amazon FBA. If you're selling physical goods at any real volume, this matters enormously.

Squarespace Commerce handles unlimited products too, but variants are capped at 100 per product, and inventory tools are simpler. For a boutique with 30 products in a few colors, that's fine. For a store with 500 SKUs across multiple sizes, you'll feel the ceiling.

Wix Stores handles unlimited products and variants, with decent inventory tracking. It's more capable than Squarespace for inventory, but less polished than Shopify, and the interface takes getting used to.

Winner: Shopify, clearly.

Payments and checkout

All three support major payment processors, but the fee structures differ in ways that matter.

Shopify charges credit card rates of about 2.9% + 30¢ per online transaction on the Basic plan (dropping to 2.6% and 2.4% on higher tiers). If you use a third-party gateway instead of Shopify Payments, Shopify adds a penalty fee of 0.5% to 2% on top. That's a real cost many people don't notice until they're locked in.

Squarespace charges 0% transaction fees on all plans — you only pay whatever your payment processor (Stripe or PayPal) charges. That's a meaningful advantage for beginners.

Wix also charges 0% transaction fees on its Business plans, though its payment processing rates through Wix Payments are comparable to Stripe's.

Shopify's checkout is the most customizable and conversion-optimized — abandoned cart recovery, one-click checkout via Shop Pay, and tons of upsell apps. Squarespace's checkout is clean and simple but less flexible. Wix's is functional but has historically been the weakest of the three for conversion.

Winner: Shopify for checkout power, Squarespace and Wix for fee transparency.

Marketing tools

Shopify's app store is enormous — 8,000+ apps — covering email marketing (Klaviyo), SMS, loyalty programs, upsells, and just about anything else. The catch: most of the good apps charge monthly subscriptions on top of your Shopify plan.

Squarespace includes built-in email campaigns and decent SEO tools, but its app ecosystem is tiny by comparison. You get fewer marketing options, but less subscription fatigue.

Wix has a growing app market and built-in email marketing, plus strong built-in SEO tools. It's more capable than Squarespace for marketing out of the box, but less powerful than Shopify's ecosystem.

Winner: Shopify for depth, Wix for built-in value.

SEO

Squarespace and Wix have both improved dramatically here. Wix's SEO tools are now genuinely good — you get guided setup, schema markup, and solid technical foundations. Squarespace's SEO is clean and reliable, with automatic sitemaps and good control over meta content.

Shopify's SEO is solid but historically weaker on blog/content SEO, which matters if you're planning to drive traffic through content marketing. That's improved, but if organic content is your main strategy, Squarespace often edges ahead.

Winner: Squarespace and Wix, roughly tied.

Pros and cons

Shopify

Pros

  • The most capable ecommerce engine, by a wide margin
  • Handles inventory, variants, and multi-channel selling better than anyone
  • Huge app ecosystem for marketing, upsells, and automation
  • Shop Pay and abandoned cart recovery boost conversions
  • Scales with you — from 10 products to 10,000
  • Strong POS for in-person selling too

Cons

  • Highest monthly cost of the three
  • Apps add up fast — $20–$40/month is common
  • Transaction penalty if you don't use Shopify Payments
  • More setup and learning required than Squarespace or Wix
  • Free themes are basic; a good look costs money
  • Weaker for content/blog SEO

Which one should you actually pick?

Here's how I'd decide, based on what you're selling:

Pick Shopify if:

  • You're selling physical products and expect to grow
  • You have 50+ SKUs or complex variants
  • You want to sell on Amazon, Instagram, TikTok, and your own site from one dashboard
  • You're okay spending $60–$100/month all-in
  • You want the strongest checkout and conversion tools

Pick Squarespace if:

  • Your brand's visual identity is central to your product
  • You have a small, curated catalog (under ~100 products)
  • You're selling services, digital products, or memberships
  • You want 0% transaction fees and fewer subscriptions
  • You'd rather not think about design at all
