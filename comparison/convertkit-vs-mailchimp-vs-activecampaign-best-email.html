Three names keep coming up: Convertkit, Mailchimp, and ActiveCampaign. Each promises to help you grow your list, send the right messages, and actually Make money from your email list.

But which one should you actually use? I’ve spent weeks testing all three, sending campaigns, building automations, and pulling my hair out over deliverability. Here’s the honest breakdown.

Detailed Feature Breakdown

ConvertKit – Built for creators, but is it too simple?

ConvertKit was designed specifically for creators—bloggers, authors, course creators. Its whole thing is “creator-first.” What does that mean in practice?

Landing pages that actually work. ConvertKit’s landing pages are surprisingly good for a tool that isn’t a full website builder. You can create an opt-in, a sales page, or a thank-you page in minutes. They load fast and look clean on mobile.

Visual automations. The visual builder lets you drag and drop triggers, tags, and actions. For example: “When someone joins via landing page A, tag as ‘freebie_optin’, send sequence B, and if they click link C, move to sequence D.” It’s straightforward, but if you need complex branching (like if/then based on multiple conditions), it can feel limited.

Tags and segments. Tags are the backbone of ConvertKit. You tag people based on what they do (click, buy, etc.) and then send emails based on those tags. It works, but there’s no native CRM or lead scoring.

Pricing. The free plan supports up to 1,000 subscribers but limits you to basic email broadcasts and one automation. The Creator plan at $9/month for up to 300 subscribers unlocks everything (automations, landing pages, unlimited emails). However, when your list grows, costs jump quickly—$29/month for 1,001-3,000 subscribers.

Where it falls short: No A/B testing for subject lines (though they claim it’s coming). No built-in CRM. No advanced reporting. And if you want to send transactional emails (like order confirmations), you’ll need a separate tool.

Mailchimp – The jack-of-all-trades, master of some

Mailchimp is the old guard. Most people try it first because it’s free for up to 500 contacts and has brand recognition. But Mailchimp has changed a lot in the last few years. It’s now more of a marketing platform than just an email tool.

Free plan. The free tier is generous: up to 500 contacts, 1,000 sends per month, basic templates, a landing page, and one automation. But there’s a catch—Mailchimp adds its branding to your emails and landing pages unless you pay.

Audience management. Mailchimp’s segmentation is decent. You can group contacts by tags, purchase history, activity, and custom fields. But the interface is cluttered—too many menus, too many options. It feels like using a Swiss Army knife when you just need a screwdriver.

Automation. The classic automation builder is fine for simple sequences (welcome series, abandoned cart). But the newer “customer journey builder” is more flexible—you can set up conditional splits and merge paths. However, it’s not as intuitive as ConvertKit’s visual drag-and-drop. And to access advanced features like multivariate testing, you need a paid plan.

Pricing. The free plan is good for beginners, but once you cross 500 contacts, the cheapest paid plan starts at $13/month (Essentials) for 500 contacts. For 1,500 contacts, it’s $26/month. The Standard plan ($20/month for 500 contacts) adds A/B testing and more automation.

Where it falls short: The interface is overwhelming. Deliverability can be inconsistent—I’ve had emails land in spam even with clean lists. And Mailchimp is aggressive about enforcing its acceptable use policy (e.g., no affiliate marketing on the free plan). Also, the platform has gotten slower over the years.

ActiveCampaign – The power user’s playground

ActiveCampaign is for creators who want serious automation and customer relationship management. If you think of your email list as a sales pipeline, ActiveCampaign is your best friend.

Automation engine. This is where ActiveCampaign shines. Its visual automation builder supports conditionals, actions based on goals, split testing, and even machine learning (predictive sending). You can create workflows like: “If subscriber scores > 50 and visited pricing page, send sales sequence; otherwise, send nurturing sequence and tag for follow-up.” It’s powerful, but the learning curve is real.

Built-in CRM. ActiveCampaign includes a full CRM with deal stages, contact scoring, and pipeline management. If you sell courses, coaching, or digital products, you can track leads from opt-in to purchase. ConvertKit and Mailchimp don’t offer this.

Segmentation and tagging. Tags, custom fields, and lists work together. You can create dynamic segments based on behavior, profile data, or scores. ActiveCampaign also supports “contact scoring” where you assign points for actions (e.g., +5 for email click, -3 for unsubscribed). This is huge for prioritizing hot leads.

Pricing. The Starter plan is $9/month for up to 500 contacts, but it’s limited—no CRM, no scoring, no split paths. The Plus plan ($49/month for 500 contacts) unlocks the good stuff: CRM, contact scoring, and conditional content. For 1,000 contacts on Plus, it’s $79/month. That’s steep for a creator just starting.

Where it falls short: The interface is dense. Setting up a simple welcome sequence takes more time than in ConvertKit. The built-in email designer is average—you’ll probably want to use custom HTML. And the free trial only lasts 14 days, which isn’t enough to test everything.

Pros and Cons

ConvertKit

Pros

  • Super easy to set up landing pages and opt-in forms.
  • Clean, distraction-free interface.
  • Great for digital creators who sell courses or memberships.
  • Good deliverability out of the box.
  • Free plan supports up to 1,000 subscribers.

Cons

  • No A/B testing for subject lines or content.
  • Limited automation depth (no conditional splits like “if contact opened email A AND visited page B”).
  • Expensive as list grows (e.g., $59/month for 5,000 subs).
  • No native CRM or lead scoring.
  • Reporting is basic.

Mailchimp

Pros

  • Generous free plan (500 contacts, 1,000 sends/month).
  • Templates and drag-and-drop editor are decent.
  • Integrates with tons of apps (Shopify, WordPress, etc.).
  • A/B testing available on paid plans.
  • All-in-one (landing pages, postcards, social ads).

Cons

  • Interface is bloated and confusing.
  • Free plan includes Mailchimp branding.
  • Deliverability issues reported by many users.
  • Automation builder is not intuitive.
  • Pricing jumps quickly once you hit 500+ contacts.
  • No built-in CRM.

ActiveCampaign

Pros

  • Industry-leading automation capabilities (conditional logic, split testing, goal-based triggers).
  • Built-in CRM with deal pipeline and contact scoring.
  • Excellent segmentation and tagging.
  • Great deliverability when configured properly.
  • Predictive sending and machine learning.

Cons

  • High learning curve – not for beginners.
  • Email editor feels outdated.
  • Expensive for small lists (Plus plan $49/mo for 500 contacts).
  • No free plan, only 14-day trial.
  • Overkill if you only need simple broadcasts.

Verdict: Which Should You Choose?

For most creators starting out: ConvertKit.

If you’re a solo creator selling a course, building a newsletter, or launching a membership, ConvertKit gives you the essential tools without the complexity. The free plan supports up to 1,000 subscribers, which is generous. The landing pages are beautiful, the automation builder is simple enough to use without a tutorial, and deliverability is reliable. You’ll miss A/B testing, but you can always upgrade later.

For beginners who want a free, all-in-one solution: Mailchimp.

If you’re on a tight budget and need to handle email, a basic landing page, and maybe some social ads, Mailchimp’s free plan works—just be prepared for the learning curve and possible deliverability headaches. But as soon as you hit 500 contacts, consider switching. Mailchimp gets expensive and cluttered fast.

For serious creators who treat email as a sales engine: ActiveCampaign.

If you have a growing list (1,000+) and want to segment aggressively, score leads, and build complex automations that convert, ActiveCampaign is worth the investment. The CRM is a game-changer for tracking leads from email to purchase. Yes, it’s pricey and takes time to learn, but for data-driven creators who sell high-ticket items (coaching, courses over $200), it pays for itself.

Final pick: ConvertKit – it’s the sweet spot of simplicity, price, and creator-focused features for most people. If you outgrow it, you can always move to ActiveCampaign later.

FAQ

Can I migrate from Mailchimp to ConvertKit or ActiveCampaign?

Yes. ConvertKit offers a free migration service if you’re on a paid plan. ActiveCampaign has import tools, but you may need to clean up your list first. Expect some manual work with custom fields and tags.

Which tool has the best deliverability?

ActiveCampaign generally has the highest deliverability when configured with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC. ConvertKit is also strong. Mailchimp has had more complaints in recent years due to shared IPs and aggressive filtering.

Do I need a CRM for email marketing?

Not necessarily. If you only send newsletters and promote a simple product, ConvertKit’s tagging is enough. But if you track leads through a sales process (e.g., freebie → email course → call → purchase), ActiveCampaign’s CRM saves you from juggling separate tools.

Which one is best for a podcast or newsletter?

ConvertKit is the favorite among newsletter writers. Its simplicity, subscriber growth features (like “recommendations”), and beautiful landing pages make it ideal for building a loyal readership.

Is Mailchimp still free?

Yes, but the free plan limits you to 500 contacts and 1,000 sends per month, plus Mailchimp branding on emails and landing pages. It’s fine for testing, but most serious creators outgrow it within a few months.

Can I use ActiveCampaign for a small list under 500?

You can, but it’s overkill. The Starter plan ($9/mo) is very limited, and the Plus plan ($49/mo) is expensive for a small list. Start with ConvertKit or Mailchimp, then upgrade when you need advanced automation.

Still not sure? Most tools offer free trials or plans. Give ConvertKit a spin for 14 days—you can always export your list and move later.
