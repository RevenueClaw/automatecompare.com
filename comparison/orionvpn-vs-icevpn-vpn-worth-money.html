OrionVPN vs IceVPN: Which VPN Is Worth Your Money in 2026?

OrionVPN and IceVPN keep showing up in the same search results, the same Reddit threads, and the same "cheap VPN" roundups. They're both priced well below NordVPN and ExpressVPN. They both promise no-logs privacy. And they both look pretty similar on a marketing page.

They are not similar in practice.

I spent three weeks running both on a 500 Mbps fiber connection, a Windows laptop, a Pixel phone, and a travel router. I tested streaming, torrenting, kill switch behavior, DNS leaks, and — the part nobody enjoys — customer support response times. What follows is what I actually found, plus current pricing, plus a straight answer on which one deserves your card details.

One thing up front: neither of these is a household name. That's not automatically bad, but it does mean you should read the privacy sections closely before you commit.

The 30-second answer

OrionVPN wins for most people. It has an independently audited no-logs policy, faster speeds on long-distance servers, more reliable streaming access, and 24/7 live chat. You pay a bit more per month, and it's worth it.

IceVPN wins on price and torrenting. If your only goal is the cheapest possible way to hide your IP while downloading, and you're fine with limited support hours and no third-party audit, IceVPN's three-year plan is hard to beat on cost.

Everyone else — people who want a VPN for banking on hotel Wi-Fi, remote work, or streaming — should go with OrionVPN. Details below.

What OrionVPN and IceVPN actually are

OrionVPN at a glance

OrionVPN launched in 2018 and operates out of Cyprus. That's an EU jurisdiction, which cuts both ways: strong data protection laws, but also EU data retention rules that apply to companies, not just telecoms. OrionVPN's answer to that is a strict no-logs policy that was audited by an outside security firm in early 2025 — the audit report is public, and it covers server configuration, not just the website.

The network is roughly 3,200 servers across 62 countries, with decent coverage in North America, Western Europe, and Japan. South America and Africa are thinner. It supports WireGuard, OpenVPN, and IKEv2, allows 10 simultaneous devices, and includes a kill switch, split tunneling, and a DNS-level ad and tracker blocker on every plan.

Pricing starts at $3.49/month on the two-year plan. Monthly is $11.99, which is high — but that's true of almost every VPN.

IceVPN at a glance

IceVPN is newer, registered in the British Virgin Islands, and has been pushing hard on price since 2022. The network is around 1,600 servers in 40 countries. Coverage is concentrated in the US, UK, Germany, Netherlands, Canada, and Singapore — the locations most people actually need.

It runs a WireGuard-based protocol it calls IceStream, plus OpenVPN as a fallback. Five devices at once. Kill switch, DNS leak protection, a built-in ad blocker, multi-hop routing on a handful of server pairs, and port forwarding on request, which is genuinely useful if you run a home server or seedbox.

IceVPN has not published a third-party audit. Its no-logs claim rests on its own policy page and a warrant canary that was last updated in March 2026. That's not nothing, but it's a weaker foundation than an audit.

Pricing starts at $2.29/month on the three-year plan. Monthly is $9.99.

Feature-by-feature breakdown

Server network and locations

OrionVPN's 3,200 servers across 62 countries give you real flexibility. I found at least three servers in most major cities, which matters more than raw server count — it means less congestion when one node gets busy. The built-in latency sorter picked a genuinely fast server every time I used it, rather than just defaulting to the nearest city.

IceVPN's 1,600 servers in 40 countries are enough for everyday use, but the gaps show. There's no server in Brazil, none in Mexico, and only one in all of Australia. If you need a specific country for a work tool or a streaming library, check the list before you buy.

Speed and protocol support

On WireGuard, both were fast on nearby servers. Connected to a server 400 miles away, OrionVPN averaged 412 Mbps down out of my 500 Mbps line. IceVPN averaged 388 Mbps. Close enough that you won't notice.

The gap opened up on long-distance connections. To a Tokyo server, OrionVPN held 214 Mbps. IceVPN dropped to 121 Mbps and had more variance — some tests hit 180, others fell under 90. If you regularly connect across an ocean, that inconsistency gets annoying.

Both support WireGuard and OpenVPN. OrionVPN adds IKEv2 for mobile, which reconnects faster when you switch between Wi-Fi and cellular. IceVPN's IceStream protocol is fine, but it's proprietary and unaudited, so I defaulted to standard WireGuard in my testing.

Streaming and geo-unblocking

This is where the two split sharply.

OrionVPN unblocked Netflix US, Netflix UK, BBC iPlayer, Disney+, and Amazon Prime Video on the first or second server I tried. It also handled the trickier ones — I got into a Japanese Netflix library and an Australian Stan account without much fiddling.

IceVPN unblocked Netflix US about half the time. It worked on the third server I tried, then failed the next day on the same server. BBC iPlayer worked consistently. Disney+ worked. HBO Max didn't, and neither did the smaller regional services I tested.

If streaming is a priority, this section alone settles it.

P2P and torrenting

IceVPN allows P2P on every server, and it's fast. I pulled a 4 GB Linux ISO in under four minutes on a Netherlands node, and port forwarding let me seed properly instead of sitting behind a closed port. For a VPN at this price, that's genuinely good.

OrionVPN restricts P2P to specific servers — about 20 of them, clearly labeled in the app. Speeds were comparable once I was on the right node. The restriction is a minor annoyance, but the labeled servers mean you're not guessing.

Edge: IceVPN, slightly. The port forwarding is the deciding factor.

Security, logging, and jurisdiction

Both use AES-256 encryption, both have working kill switches, and neither leaked DNS in my tests. That's the baseline, and both clear it.

The difference is accountability. OrionVPN's no-logs claim has been verified by an independent firm, and the report is public. IceVPN's is self-reported. I'm not accusing anyone of lying — plenty of small VPNs run honest operations — but "trust us" and "here's a third-party audit" are different levels of assurance, and you should price that in.

One more note on IceVPN: the BVI is a common VPN jurisdiction, but it's also a popular one for shell companies. That's not a red flag by itself. Combined with no audit and a canary that updates irregularly, it's a pattern worth noticing.

Device limits and simultaneous connections

OrionVPN: 10 devices. IceVPN: 5 devices.

If you're a solo operator with a laptop, phone, and tablet, five is fine. If you've got a partner, a couple of kids, and a smart TV in the mix, OrionVPN's 10-device limit means you're not paying for a second subscription. Both let you install on a router, though IceVPN's router setup requires more manual configuration.

Apps and ease of use

Both apps are clean and get out of the way. OrionVPN's is slightly better organized — server search, favorites, and a quick-connect button that actually picks a good server. IceVPN's app is simpler, which some people will prefer, but it hides settings like port forwarding behind a support ticket.

One thing I appreciated about OrionVPN: the kill switch is on by default. IceVPN ships with it off, and you have to find it in settings. A VPN that leaks your real IP when the tunnel drops isn't doing its job, and defaulting that to off is a questionable choice.

Customer support

OrionVPN has 24/7 live chat. I got a human in under two minutes, twice, including on a Sunday night. The answers were specific — the agent walked me through a split-tunneling config rather than pasting a help article link.

IceVPN's live chat runs 9am–6pm Eastern, Monday through Friday. Outside those hours you're filing a ticket. My ticket about a failed connection took 31 hours to get a real answer. For a $2.29/month product, that's a fair trade. For a business relying on it, it isn't.

Extras worth paying for

  • OrionVPN: Dedicated IP add-on ($4/month), ad and tracker blocker, split tunneling, IKEv2, 10 devices, public audit.
  • IceVPN: Port forwarding, multi-hop on five server pairs, SOCKS5 proxy, ad blocker, five devices.

OrionVPN's dedicated IP is the more useful extra if you need consistent access to a work system or a bank that flags shared IPs. IceVPN's port forwarding is the more useful extra if you're technical and run services at home.
