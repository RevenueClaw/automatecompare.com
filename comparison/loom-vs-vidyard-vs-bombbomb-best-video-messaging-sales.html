Loom vs Vidyard vs BombBomb: Best Video Messaging for Sales in 2026

Here's the thing nobody tells you when you start shopping for a sales video tool: Loom, Vidyard, and BombBomb get lumped together in every "best video messaging" list, but they were built for three different jobs.

Loom was built so a product designer could explain a bug to an engineer without a meeting. Vidyard was built so a sales rep could send 40 personalized videos and know exactly which prospect watched to the 80% mark. BombBomb was built so a real estate agent could put their face inside an email and get a reply.

That difference matters more than any feature checklist. I've set all three up, sent video from all three, and watched what happens on the other end. Here's the honest breakdown of where each one wins, where each one will annoy you, and which one I'd actually pay for in 2026.

Quick verdict (for the impatient)

  • Best overall for sales teams: Vidyard. Deepest analytics, real CRM plumbing, and a free plan you can genuinely test with.
  • Best for email-first relationship selling: BombBomb. Video that plays inside the inbox, not a link someone has to click.
  • Best for quick updates and internal use: Loom. Fastest record-to-send flow of the three, cheapest paid plan, weakest sales tooling.

Now the details.

What each tool is actually for

Loom records your screen, your camera, or both, then drops a share link on your clipboard before you've finished closing the tab. Since Atlassian bought it, the roadmap has leaned hard toward team documentation — video comments in Jira, walkthroughs in Confluence. It still works great for sales follow-ups. It just isn't where the product team's attention is.

Vidyard is a video hosting and engagement platform with a sales layer on top. Videos live in a library, get embedded anywhere, and every play feeds data back into your CRM. It's the closest thing to a measurement system for video outreach.

BombBomb is video email. It's not really a video host that happens to do email — it's an email platform with recording built in, so your video thumbnail animates inside Gmail or Outlook instead of sitting behind a link.

Recording and sending speed

Loom wins this one and it isn't close. Hit the extension, talk, hit stop, link is copied. The free plan caps you at 25 videos and 5 minutes each, which is fine for follow-ups and useless for a 12-minute product walkthrough.

Vidyard's Chrome extension (GoVideo) is nearly as fast, and if you're already in Gmail it records straight into a compose window. Setup takes longer though — you'll want to configure your library, thumbnails, and CRM connection before it feels smooth.

BombBomb is the slowest of the three, mostly because it's doing more work: recording, then attaching to an email, then tracking that email. The mobile app matters here — a lot of BombBomb users record from their phone in the car after a client meeting, and it handles that better than either competitor.

One practical note: all three cap recording length somewhere on lower tiers. If you send long demos, check the limit before you commit.

Video in email vs. video as a link

This is the biggest real-world difference, and most comparison articles skip it.

Loom and Vidyard send a link. The video plays on their site. You get full analytics, but you're asking the recipient to click. In cold outreach, click-through is your bottleneck.

BombBomb sends an animated GIF thumbnail inside the email body, which clicks through to the hosted video. Recipients see your face moving in the inbox without doing anything. Reply rates on that format tend to be better — not because the technology is magic, but because it looks like a person sent a person something.

The catch: animated images in email trigger spam filters more often than plain text, and the rendering varies across Gmail, Outlook desktop, and Apple Mail. BombBomb has spent a decade on that problem, but it's still a variable you don't control. Loom and Vidyard links always render. They just might not get clicked.

Analytics: what you can actually learn

All three tell you someone watched. The difference is what happens after that.

Vidyard is the deepest. You see a retention curve showing exactly where attention dropped, which pages were viewed, which CTAs were clicked, and which viewer accounts (by company, if you're using it right) are watching. It'll notify you in Slack when a prospect hits 50% of a video. That notification is the single most useful sales feature across all three tools.

BombBomb tracks email opens, video plays, replays, and link clicks, and ties them to the contact record. Solid, if less granular than Vidyard on the in-video side.

Loom shows views, reactions, comments, and — on paid plans — engagement
