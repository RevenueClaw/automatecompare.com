Loom vs Vidyard vs BombBomb: Best Video Messaging for Sales in 2026

You send a proposal. Then you wait. Three days later you get "let me think about it," which is polite for "I didn't read it."

Video fixes that. Not because video is magic, but because a 90-second clip of your face and your screen is harder to ignore than a wall of text. Buyers watch it, and you find out they watched it — which is a follow-up cue you'd never get from an email.

The problem is that three tools dominate this space and they're built for genuinely different jobs. Loom is the one everyone already uses. Vidyard is what sales orgs graduate to. BombBomb is the email-first option that sales veterans defend in LinkedIn comment sections.

I've run all three through real sales workflows — cold outreach, demo follow-ups, proposal walkthroughs, onboarding handoffs. Here's how they actually compare.

The short answer

  • Pick Loom if you want speed and you're already living in Slack/Atlassian. It's the least friction, cheapest, and good enough for most follow-ups.
  • Pick Vidyard if video is a real part of your sales process and you need to know exactly who watched what, plus CRM logging that doesn't require manual work.
  • Pick BombBomb if your sales motion is email-heavy and you want videos to play inside the inbox instead of behind a link.

Now the detail.

What each tool is actually for

Loom started as a screen recorder for async work and got acquired by Atlassian in 2023. Its center of gravity is internal communication — bug reports, design reviews, "here's how this feature works" — and it happens to be great for sales follow-ups because it's so fast. Hit record, talk, paste link. Done in under two minutes.

Vidyard was built for business video from the start, and its sales tooling is the most developed of the three. Watch pages, in-video forms, engagement heatmaps, prospect-level notifications, and native push into Salesforce and Outreach. It's the tool a 15-person sales team standardizes on.

BombBomb isn't really a screen recorder company. It's a video email company. The core idea: instead of sending a link to a video, BombBomb embeds an animated thumbnail that plays when the recipient clicks — no redirect, no "watch on Loom" page. It also tracks email opens alongside video plays. That's a meaningfully different approach for cold outreach.

Feature breakdown

Recording and video limits

All three record screen, camera, or both. Differences show up in the caps.

Loom's free plan gives you 25 videos at 5 minutes each. The Business plan removes the video count cap and lifts the length limit. Recording happens through a Chrome extension, a desktop app on Mac/Windows, or mobile — and the share link starts generating while you're still talking, which is why people reach for it mid-call.

Vidyard's free tier is more generous on length: 25 videos, up to 30 minutes each. Paid plans extend that further. The recorder is a browser extension plus desktop and mobile apps, and it handles Webcam-only, screen-only, or screen-with-bubble.

BombBomb caps videos shorter — roughly 10 minutes on most plans — and that's deliberate. A 40-minute BombBomb video defeats the purpose. The mobile app is genuinely good, which matters because a lot of BombBomb users record from their phone in the parking lot after a sales call.

What happens after you hit send

This is where the three split hardest.

Loom tells you when someone watches, how far they got, whether they rewatched a section, and how many times. You get email notifications and Slack alerts. It's solid, and for a solo operator it's honestly enough.

Vidyard goes deeper. You get a per-viewer engagement timeline showing exactly where attention dropped, plus "heatmap" views across all viewers so you can see which part of your pitch loses people. It also flags returning viewers — someone who watched your demo video three times is a different conversation than someone who watched 12 seconds. Vidyard's prospecting layer (marketing calls it Prospector) surfaces this as a notification feed you can work through like a call list.

BombBomb tracks opens and plays. So you know your email was opened, and separately whether the video played and how much of it. For cold outreach that's a useful signal stack: opened but didn't play means the thumbnail didn't land. Played 80% means call them now.

Email integration — BombBomb's home turf

This is BombBomb's whole reason to exist, and it's not marketing fluff.

When you send a BombBomb video through Gmail or Outlook, the recipient sees a thumbnail of your face with a play button — inside the email body. Click it and the video plays right there. No link click, no new tab, no "sign in to view" wall. In practice that produces noticeably more plays than a plain link.

Loom and Vidyard both have Gmail and Outlook extensions that insert a thumbnail image linking out. It looks similar in the inbox
