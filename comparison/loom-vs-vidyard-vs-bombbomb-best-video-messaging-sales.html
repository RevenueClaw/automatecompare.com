Loom vs Vidyard vs BombBomb: Best Video Messaging for Sales in 2026

Somewhere between the fourth follow-up email and the voicemail nobody returns, most salespeople figure out the same thing: a 60-second video of an actual human face gets replies that text doesn't.

The problem is that "video messaging" means three different products depending on who you ask. Loom is a screen recorder that turned into a communication layer. Vidyard is a video hosting platform that grew sales tools on top. BombBomb is a video email system built for people who live in their inbox.

They overlap enough to be compared, and they're different enough that picking wrong costs you real money each month. Here's how they actually stack up.

What actually matters in a sales video tool

Before comparing features line by line, it helps to agree on what you're grading. In my experience, five things separate a tool that books meetings from one that collects dust:

  • Recording speed. If it takes more than about 15 seconds from "I should send a video" to recording, you won't do it consistently.
  • What happens after the click. Does the viewer land on a branded page with a calendar link, or a bare file player?
  • Who watched and how much. "They opened it" is nearly worthless. "They watched 82% and rewatched the pricing section twice" is a reason to call.
  • Where the data goes. If views don't land in HubSpot, Salesforce, or your CRM of choice, someone has to copy-paste, and that someone stops doing it by week three.
  • Cost per seat at your actual headcount. All three charge per user, and the jump from one seat to five is where budgets break.

Keep those in mind, because the three tools are strong in different columns.

Loom: the default for a reason

Loom got acquired by Atlassian in 2023 and has only tightened its grip on the "record something and send it" category since. The Chrome extension and desktop app are the fastest in this comparison. Hit record, talk, hit stop, link appears on your clipboard. That loop is under ten seconds once installed.

What you get on paid plans: unlimited videos, up to 5 minutes on free and longer limits on paid, screen plus camera bubble, viewer insights (who watched, percentage watched, rewatch counts, emoji reactions), comments with timestamps, trimming and basic edits, chapters, password protection, link expiry, and custom CTA buttons on the Business plan and up. Loom AI generates titles, summaries, chapters, and action items from the transcript.

Where it's genuinely good for sales: the speed and the watch data. If you're sending a walkthrough of a proposal, a bug repro to a client, or a "here's what I'd change about your onboarding flow" video, Loom's watch-percentage graph tells you whether the prospect actually engaged before you follow up.
