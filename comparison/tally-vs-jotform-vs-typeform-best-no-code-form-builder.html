Tally vs Jotform vs Typeform: Best No-Code Form Builder in 2026

Every automation workflow starts with a trigger. And for most small businesses, that trigger is a form.

A lead fills out a contact form. A client submits an onboarding questionnaire. A customer answers five questions before you send them a quote. That submission is the first domino — it kicks off the email sequence, creates the CRM record, adds the row to the spreadsheet, and fires the Slack notification.

Which means the form builder you choose isn't just a design decision. It's the foundation of everything downstream. Pick the wrong one and you'll be fighting response limits, duct-taping Zapier steps together, or paying $99 a month for a tool you use twice a week.

Tally, Jotform, and Typeform are the three names that come up most often when someone asks about a form builder no-code setup. They're all good. They're also very different, and the gap between them gets wider the more you use them.

I've built forms in all three — lead capture, client intake, event registration, payment collection, internal surveys. Here's what actually matters.

The short answer

  • Tally is the best value by a mile. Unlimited forms and unlimited submissions on the free plan. If you're a freelancer or small team, start here.
  • Jotform is the best all-rounder for complex, business-critical forms. 10,000+ templates, offline collection, HIPAA options, e-signatures.
  • Typeform is the best for conversion-focused, consumer-facing forms — but it's the most expensive and the response caps will bite you.

Now let's get into the details.

Tally: the doc-style builder that refuses to charge you

Tally launched in 2020 out of Belgium, and its pitch was almost annoyingly simple: build forms the way you'd write a document.

There's no drag-and-drop canvas. You open a blank page, type a question, and hit / to insert a block — a multiple choice field, a file upload, a payment field, a page break. It feels like writing in Notion, which is exactly the point. If you've ever used Notion, you'll be building your first Tally form in about four minutes.

The headline feature, though, is the free plan. Unlimited forms. Unlimited questions. Unlimited submissions. No per-response metering, no "you've hit 100 responses this month" wall. For a solo operator running three or four intake forms, that's genuinely all you need forever.

What you get on free is more than most competitors give on paid: conditional logic, hidden fields, answer piping, file uploads, and integrations with Google Sheets, Notion, Airtable, Slack, and webhooks.

Where Tally falls short: the template library is small compared to Jotform, there's no mobile app for offline collection, and the design options are deliberately minimal. Your Tally form will look clean, but it will look like a Tally form. If you need pixel-level control over branding, that's a problem.

Pro (around $29/month, or roughly $24/month billed annually) unlocks Stripe payments, calculations, custom domains, partial submissions, redirects on completion, and custom thank-you pages. Business ($89/month) adds multi-user workspaces and priority support.

Jotform: the one that does everything, including the things you didn't know you needed

Jotform has been around since 2006, which in SaaS years makes it ancient. That longevity shows up in two ways: an enormous feature set, and a user interface that occasionally feels like it was designed by committee.

The template library is the big draw. Over 10,000 templates covering everything from patient intake forms to pizza order forms to employee performance reviews. You will almost never start from scratch.

But Jotform's real advantage is the ecosystem around the form builder. There's Jotform Tables (spreadsheet-style response management), Jotform Apps (turn a form into a mini web app), Jotform Sign (e-signatures), and Jotform Approvals (multi-step approval workflows). If you need a form plus a lightweight process engine behind it, Jotform gives you both without leaving the platform.

It also does things the other two can't. Offline form collection through the mobile app — useful for field service, events, inspections. HIPAA-compliant forms on the higher tiers. 30+ payment gateways, not just Stripe. Widgets that embed maps, calculators, and electronic signatures directly into a form.

Check Jotform Current Deal →

Where Jotform falls short: the free plan is tight — 5 forms, 100 submissions per month, 100 MB of storage. The interface is dated and busy, and Jotform is aggressive about upselling you mid-build. Forms also load slower than Tally or Typeform, which matters if you're embedding on a landing page.

Pricing scales steeply: Bronze is $34/month billed annually (1,000 submissions), Silver $39/month (2,500), Gold $99/month (10,000), Platinum $139/month (100,000). Monthly billing adds roughly 15–30% on top.

Typeform: the one people actually enjoy filling out

Typeform's whole thing is the one-question-at-a-time format. Instead of a wall of fields, the respondent sees a single question, answers it, and the next one slides in. It's a conversation rather than a form.

That format converts. For consumer-facing quizzes, lead magnets, and surveys where completion rate is the metric you care about, Typeform consistently outperforms a standard stacked form. There's a reason so many brands use it for their "what's your [X] personality" quizzes.

The design tools are the best of the three. Custom themes, background images, video questions, question groups, and animations that Make the whole thing feel like a product rather than a form.

Logic is solid too — branching, jump logic, hidden fields, answer piping, and calculations (on Plus and above). Integrations run 120+ natively, including HubSpot, Salesforce, Slack, and Google Sheets.
