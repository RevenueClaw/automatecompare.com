Tally vs Jotform vs Typeform: Best No-Code Form Builder

Building forms is one of those tasks that seems simple until you actually have to do it. You need a lead capture page, an order form, or a client intake questionnaire. Suddenly you're drowning in options, conditional logic, and design decisions.

I've spent the last few weeks putting three of the most popular no-code form builders through their paces: Tally, Jotform, and Typeform. Each one takes a very different approach to solving the same problem.

Here is the honest breakdown of which one you should actually use.

The Contenders at a Glance

Tally is the new kid on the block. It looks and feels like a Notion doc. You type where you want fields to go, and it just works. It is minimalist to a fault, but that minimalism is exactly what makes it so fast to use. It targets freelancers and small teams who want something that looks custom without the work.

Jotform is the old guard. It has been around for years, and it shows in the best and worst ways. It has 20,000+ templates, a massive app store, and enterprise features like HIPAA compliance. It is the workhorse. It also feels like a clunky dashboard from 2012.

Typeform is the design darling. It pioneered the conversational, one-question-at-a-time format. If you want a form that feels like a premium survey experience, this is it. But you pay a premium price for that polish, and the free plan is practically unusable for serious work.

Tally is the clear winner for value. The free plan gives you unlimited submissions across five forms. For most freelancers, this is all you will ever need. The Pro plan at $29/mo is cheap compared to the competition.

Jotform has a famously restrictive free plan. 100 submissions a month is nothing. You will hit that wall fast if you are actually growing. The pricing jumps quickly as you need more submissions and storage.

Typeform is expensive. The Essentials plan at $59/mo is a big ask for a small business. The free plan is a trial, not a tool. You get 10 responses a month. That is not enough to validate an idea, let alone run a business.

Building Experience: Canvas vs Drag-and-Drop vs Conversation

Tally: The Blank Canvas

Tally uses a "slash command" system. If you have used Notion or Roam, you will feel right at home. You click into the form, type /, and pick a field type. Text, email, dropdown, file upload. It all happens inline.

This is incredibly fast. I built a client intake form in about 4 minutes. The downside is that there is no visual drag-and-drop grid. If you want two fields side by side, you have to use columns, which are a bit clunky.

Best for: People who think visually in terms of documents, not tables.

Jotform: The Feature Factory

Jotform throws everything at you. A massive sidebar, a form builder, a conditions tab, a workflow builder. It is powerful, but it is noisy
