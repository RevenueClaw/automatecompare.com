Dubsado vs HoneyBook vs 17hats: Best Client Management Suite for Freelancers

Let’s be real: running a service-based business means juggling proposals, contracts, invoices, schedules, and client communication. Do it manually, and you’ll burn out or drop a ball (or both). The three most popular all-in-one solutions — Dubsado, HoneyBook, and 17hats — promise to clean up your workflow. But which one actually delivers without making you want to throw your laptop?

I’ve spent weeks inside each platform, setting up projects, sending invoices, and testing automations. Here’s how they stack up.

What Each Tool Brings to the Table

Dubsado

Dubsado is the customizer’s dream. It gives you total control over forms, emails, and workflows — but that power comes with a learning curve. You can build templates for almost everything and set up automated triggers that fire off proposals, contracts, and reminders based on client actions.

Who’s it for? Freelancers and small agencies who want to brand every touchpoint and are willing to invest time upfront.

HoneyBook

HoneyBook leans into simplicity. It’s designed to handle the full client lifecycle from inquiry to payment with a polished, guided experience. The interface is modern, the onboarding is smooth, and the mobile app is solid. HoneyBook also has a built-in booking calendar and lead capture forms that are easy to embed on your website.

Who’s it for? Creatives, event planners, and photographers who want a tool that works fast out of the box.

Check HoneyBook ->

17hats

17hats is the budget-friendly Swiss Army knife. It packs a surprising number of features — CRM, invoicing, project management, bookkeeping, and even a simple website builder — all in one subscription. The interface feels older and less polished, but it gets the job done without extras you won’t use.

Who’s it for? Solo entrepreneurs and side hustlers who need maximum functionality for minimum cost.

Feature Breakdown

Proposals & Contracts

All three let you create professional proposals and contracts, but the level of customization differs.

  • Dubsado offers the most flexible editor. You can build proposals from scratch or use one of their templates, then add conditionals (hide a section if a client chooses a certain option). Contracts support e-signatures natively. You can also send contracts in a different language if needed.
  • HoneyBook provides beautiful, modern templates that are easy to tweak. The proposal builder includes option galleries (good for showing packages) and the ability to collect deposits at signing. Contracts are straightforward, with e-signature and audit trails.
  • 17hats lets you create proposals and contracts with drag-and-drop form fields. It’s functional but less polished. You can attach multiple versions and set up conditional logic, but it’s clunkier to set up than Dubsado. E-signatures work, but the signature capture looks dated.

Winner: Dubsado (for flexibility), HoneyBook (for ease + design).

Invoicing & Payments

  • Dubsado invoices can be recurring, automated, and linked to project milestones. Payment integrations include Stripe, PayPal, and Square. You can set up automatic late fees and send invoice reminders based on your workflow triggers.
  • HoneyBook shines here with its payment processing — clients can pay via credit card, ACH bank transfer, or even Venmo (US only). Deposit schedules are easy to create, and the dashboard shows exactly who has paid and who hasn’t.
  • 17hats includes invoicing with Stripe and PayPal integration. It also has a built-in bookkeeping module (QuickBooks sync) and the ability to create expense reports. But the invoice design options are limited.

Winner: HoneyBook (for payment range), Dubsado (for automation).

Scheduling & Calendars

  • Dubsado offers a scheduling feature, but it feels like an afterthought. You can set availability and share a booking link, but it doesn’t sync as smoothly with Google Calendar as HoneyBook or 17hats. Many Dubsado users rely on Calendly on the side.
  • HoneyBook has a polished scheduling tool. Clients can book consultations directly from your website or proposal. It syncs two-way with Google Calendar and iCloud, and you can set buffers between appointments.
  • 17hats recently revamped its calendar. It offers appointment types, duration options, and integration with Google and Outlook. It works well for one-on-one meetings but doesn’t handle group events or complex availability rules.

Winner: HoneyBook (for reliability), 17hats (for basic needs on budget).

Workflow Automation

This is where Dubsado truly stands out.

  • Dubsado calls them “workflows.” You can create branching automations that respond to client actions: send proposal → client signs → send contract → client pays → send welcome email → set next task. You can add delays, conditions, and custom actions. It’s a powerful system once you master the logic.
  • HoneyBook has “automations” that are simpler but effective: send a proposal when a client type is selected, trigger a thank-you email after payment. No conditional branching, but the most common workflows are covered.
  • 17hats has “workflows” (formerly called pipelines) where you can drag and drop steps. It supports basic triggers like “when a lead is added” or “when an invoice is paid,” but the depth of automation is limited. Good for linear processes.

Winner: Dubsado (by a mile).

Client Portal & Communication

All three offer a client portal where clients can view their documents, messages, and invoices.

  • Dubsado’s portal is branded and clean. Clients can log in, download files, and send messages. Internal communication is split between email and in-app messages.
  • HoneyBook’s portal (called “Client Project”) is intuitive. All communication, files, and payments live in one thread. Clients can respond via email or the portal, and it stays organized.
  • 17hats’s portal is basic but functional. It includes a messaging system that also logs email replies. The user interface is not as attractive, but it works.

Winner: HoneyBook (for user experience).

Reporting & Analytics

  • Dubsado: Provides basic reports on invoices, projects, and client activity. You can export data, but no advanced dashboards.
  • HoneyBook: Has a “Business Insights” section showing revenue, booked projects, and lead sources. Charts are clear and helpful.
  • 17hats: Includes an income/expense report, profit & loss, and tax summaries. For a small business owner who also does light bookkeeping, this is a standout feature.

Winner: 17hats (for financial reporting).

Pros and Cons

Dubsado

Pros

  • Unmatched workflow automation — set it and forget it
  • Deep customization for forms, emails, and client-facing materials
  • Affordable annual plan (around $33/mo)
  • Strong community and templates marketplace

Cons

  • Takes time to learn — expect a few frustrating afternoons
  • Scheduling feature is basic; many users add a separate tool
  • No built-in bookkeeping or expense tracking
  • Customer support can be slow via email (no phone)

HoneyBook

Pros

  • Easiest to set up and start using
  • Best payment options (ACH/Venmo included)
  • Clean, modern interface that clients love
  • Great mobile app for on-the-go management

Cons

  • No native project management boards (kanban, task lists)
  • Automation limited to simple triggers
  • Higher premium tier ($79/mo) for features like two-way calendar sync
  • No bookkeeping or reporting beyond basic metrics

17hats

Pros

  • Lowest starting price — great for tight budgets
  • Includes bookkeeping, expense tracking, and tax reports
  • 30-day free trial (longest of the three)
  • Simple CRM and lead management

Cons

  • Outdated user interface and clunky navigation
  • Customization is limited compared to Dubsado
  • Workflow automations are basic
  • Customer support response times vary

Verdict: Which Should You Pick?

There’s no single winner because your needs decide. Here’s how I’d match you:

Choose Dubsado if…

You run a service business with repeatable processes — like a wedding photographer who sends the same proposal, contract, and email sequence. If you’re willing to spend a few days setting up automated workflows, Dubsado saves you hours every week. It’s also the best option if you want full brand control.

Choose HoneyBook if…

You value speed and simplicity. If you want to send your first proposal in under 10 minutes, HoneyBook is your tool. Event planners, designers, and consultants who need a polished client experience without technical fuss will love it. It also handles payments better than the others.

Choose 17hats if…

Budget is your primary concern, or you want bookkeeping integrated with your client management. Solopreneurs just starting out — especially handypeople, coaches, or freelancers with simple workflows — get the most bang for their buck here. The 30-day trial lets you test it extensively.

My personal pick:

If I had to recommend one for most small businesses, it’s HoneyBook. It strikes the best balance between capability and ease of use. Dubsado is more powerful, but most people don’t need that power, and they give up on it before they see results. HoneyBook works from day one and grows with you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use Dubsado for free?

Dubsado offers a 7-day free trial without requiring a credit card. After that, you pay $40/month or $400/year.

Does HoneyBook integrate with QuickBooks?

Yes, HoneyBook integrates with QuickBooks for invoice sync. It also connects with Zapier for hundreds of other apps.

Is 17hats good for photographers?

It can work, but most photographers prefer Dubsado or HoneyBook because of better proposal galleries and client portals. 17hats lacks the visual appeal photographers often need.

Can I migrate from one tool to another?

Yes, but it’s a manual process for most data (contacts, projects, invoices). Some third-party migration services exist, but the easiest path is to start fresh and import only active clients.

Do any of these offer a mobile app?

All three offer iOS and Android apps. HoneyBook’s app is the most refined; 17hats’s app feels like a scaled-down web version.

Which is best for managing multiple businesses?

Dubsado allows you to switch between “brands” within one account. HoneyBook and 17hats require separate accounts for each business.

Do I need a separate email marketing tool?

None of these replace dedicated email marketing platforms like Mailchimp or Convertkit. They handle one-on-one client communication, not broadcast newsletters.
