Dubsado vs HoneyBook vs 17hats: Which Client Management Suite Actually Fits Your Business?

I've set up all three of these tools for real businesses — a wedding photographer, a two-person design studio, and a solo consultant who was drowning in Google Docs. Every time, the same thing happened: the owner picked the tool that looked nicest in the demo video, then spent three weeks fighting it.

That's the trap with client management suites. Dubsado, HoneyBook, and 17hats all promise to replace your scattered stack of spreadsheets, invoice templates, and calendar links. They all do it. But they do it in very different ways, and the "best" one depends almost entirely on how your business actually runs.

So let's skip the marketing pages and talk about what each one is genuinely good at, where it falls down, and who should buy which.

The short version, if you're in a hurry

  • Dubsado is the most customizable and the most work to set up. Best for service businesses with complex, multi-step client journeys.
  • HoneyBook is the easiest to learn and has the best mobile experience. Best for solo creatives who want to send a proposal and get paid today.
  • 17hats is the middle ground with built-in bookkeeping. Best for freelancers who want one tool for clients and their books without paying for QuickBooks.

All three run in the $20–$50/month range depending on tier and billing cycle. None of them are free forever. More on that below.

What these tools actually do

Before comparing, it's worth being clear about the category. These aren't project management apps like Asana, and they aren't CRMs like HubSpot. They sit in a specific niche: the client-facing admin work of a small service business.

That means:

  • Capturing a lead from your website and storing their details
  • Sending a proposal or quote
  • Getting a contract signed electronically
  • Collecting a deposit or retainer
  • Scheduling a call or session
  • Sending automated reminders and follow-ups
  • Giving the client a portal to find their documents and invoices

If your business is "client sends me an email, I do the work, I send an invoice," you probably don't need any of these. If you're juggling five clients at once with deposits, contracts, and recurring check-ins, this is exactly the category you need.

Feature breakdown

Lead capture and contact management

All three offer embeddable contact forms and a contact database. The differences are in the details.

Dubsado gives you the most control. You can build multi-step lead capture forms, add custom fields, and trigger an entire workflow the moment a form is submitted — auto-send a welcome email, create a project, assign tasks, and set a reminder to follow up in three days. That's genuinely powerful if you sell a high-consideration service.

HoneyBook has a cleaner, more polished lead experience. Its "clientflow" tool bundles your inquiry form, brochure, and scheduler into a single branded link you can drop in an Instagram bio. It's less configurable but dramatically faster to launch. HoneyBook also has a built-in directory of event vendors, which matters if you're in weddings or events.

17hats handles lead capture competently but plainly. Forms exist, custom fields exist, but the whole thing feels like software from 2015 — because it largely is. It works. It just doesn't impress anyone.

Proposals, contracts, and e-signature

This is where HoneyBook pulls ahead for most people.

HoneyBook's proposal builder produces documents that look genuinely good with minimal effort. You can combine a proposal, contract, and invoice into one flow the client signs and pays in a few clicks. Templates are strong out of the box, and the AI writing assistant helps draft the copy.

Dubsado's documents are more customizable — you can build contract templates with conditional clauses, dynamic fields that pull from contact records, and multi-part questionnaires. But "more customizable" means "more time spent building it." Expect a real learning curve before your first contract looks the way you want.

17hats sits in the middle. Contracts and e-signature work fine, templates are decent, but the design feels dated and the editing experience is clunkier than either competitor.

The processor question matters more than the headline price. Dubsado and 17hats let you connect the Stripe or PayPal account you already have, so you pay standard processing rates (roughly 2.9% + 30¢ for cards) and nothing extra. HoneyBook routes payments through HoneyBook Payments, which adds its own fee on top — commonly cited around 1.5% for bank transfers and a bit more for cards.

On a $3,000 project, that difference is real money. If you invoice high-ticket work, run the math before you pick.

Scheduling

All three include a scheduling tool with calendar sync. HoneyBook's is the smoothest — clients pick a slot, the session gets added to your project, and reminders go out automatically. Dubsado's scheduler works well but takes more configuration to get the buffers and availability rules right. 17hats does the job without fuss.

Automation and workflows

This is Dubsado's home turf and the main reason people put up with its setup time.

Dubsado's workflow builder lets you chain actions with conditional logic: if a client selects package A, send contract A and invoice A; if they select package B, do something else. You can build multi-step sequences that fire on form submission, contract signing, invoice payment, or a date trigger. For a business with several service tiers or a long sales cycle, this is the difference between "a tool that stores my documents" and "a tool that runs my back office."

HoneyBook's automation is more limited. You get automated reminders, payment follow-ups, and some triggered emails, but the conditional branching is shallower. It covers the common cases well and stops there. The cheaper Starter tier also caps some automation features, so check what's actually included before buying the entry plan.

17hats offers workflow automation too, but the builder feels clunky and the options are narrower than Dubsado's
