You’ve got a growing client list, invoices to send, contracts to sign, and a calendar that looks like a Jackson Pollock painting. You need a tool that handles the client management side of your business so you can focus on the actual work.

Three names keep coming up: Dubsado, HoneyBook, and 17hats. They all promise to streamline your workflows, automate follow-ups, and Make you look professional. But they approach the problem differently, and one of them will probably fit your specific needs better than the others.

I’ve spent the past few weeks testing all three with real workflows—onboarding a new freelance client, sending proposals, collecting payments, and managing a project from start to finish. Here’s what I found.

Dubsado: The Automation Powerhouse

Dubsado markets itself as a “client management system for creative entrepreneurs.” That’s a bit vague, but the reality is it gives you the most control over your workflows.

What I loved

Workflow automation is genuinely advanced. You can set up triggers based on almost any event: when a lead submits a form, when a contract is signed, when an invoice is paid, even when a certain date passes. Then you chain actions—send an email, create a task, update a project status. It’s like having a virtual assistant that never sleeps.

For example, I built a workflow that automatically sends a welcome email, creates a project, and adds a task to review the client’s questionnaire—all triggered when a lead fills out my contact form. That took about 10 minutes to set up.

Customization is deep. You can brand every form, invoice, and proposal with your own CSS. The template editor lets you control layout, fonts, and colors far beyond basic white-label options. If you’re a designer or someone who cares about visual consistency, this is a big plus.

No transaction fees on your plans. Dubsado doesn’t take a cut of your payments (beyond what Stripe or Square charges). That’s refreshing, especially compared to some competitors that add a percentage fee unless you’re on a higher tier.

Where it falls short

The learning curve is real. Dubsado’s power comes with complexity. Setting up workflows, custom fields, and templates takes time. I watched a few of their tutorial videos and still had to redo some automations because I missed a condition. If you’re not comfortable digging into settings, this might feel overwhelming.

No built-in calendar or booking. You can integrate with Zapier to connect to Google Calendar or Calendly, but it’s not native. For a client management system in 2026, that feels like a missing piece. HoneyBook and 17hats both offer a booking page where clients can pick a time slot directly.

Pricing jumps after the first tier. The “Premier” plan ($40/mo) adds things like automated email sequences and advanced reporting, which smaller businesses might not need—but if you outgrow the basic plan, the price doubles.

Best for: Freelancers and small agencies who handle complex projects and want to automate repetitive tasks. Not ideal if you just want a simple way to send invoices and contracts.

HoneyBook: The Polished All-in-One for Creative Pros

HoneyBook started as a platform for event professionals (photographers, planners, florists) but has grown into a broader client management suite. It’s probably the most popular tool in this space, and for good reason.

What I loved

Onboarding is smooth. From the moment you sign up, HoneyBook guides you through setting up your profile, adding services, and creating a proposal template. It took me about 30 minutes to send my first proposal. The UI is clean and doesn’t bury options in nested menus.

Built-in booking and payment collection. Clients can schedule a consultation directly from your proposal or your custom booking page. Payments integrate with Stripe, Square, and PayPal. HoneyBook also supports payment plans and retainers natively—no extra setup.

Email tracking and read receipts. You can see when a client opens your proposal or invoice, and even get notifications when they do. That’s a nice touch for follow-ups. Dubsado and 17hats have this too, but HoneyBook makes it more visible.

Strong template library. They offer dozens of pre-made templates for contracts, proposals, invoices, and emails. You can customize them without touching code. For non-designers, this is a huge time saver.

Where it falls short

Transaction fees on lower plans. The “Starter” plan ($16/mo) charges 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction. The “Pro” plan ($39/mo) drops that to a flat 1% fee. If you process a lot of transactions, that adds up fast. Dubsado and 17hats don’t add extra fees on top of the payment processor.

Automations are basic. HoneyBook’s workflows are more like “if this, then do that” with a limited set of triggers. You can’t chain multiple conditions or actions as deeply as in Dubsado. For example, you can’t automatically create a task only if a client selects a specific package—you’d need to do that manually.

Limited customization for branding. While you can upload your logo and choose colors, you can’t edit the HTML/CSS of your forms or invoices. For most people that’s fine, but if you want pixel-perfect control, Dubsado wins.

Best for: Event planners, photographers, and other creative professionals who need an easy-to-use system that covers the basics well. Good for businesses that don’t need complex automation.

17hats: The Jack-of-All-Trades with a Focus on Simplicity

17hats positions itself as an “all-in-one business management software.” It includes invoicing, proposals, contracts, bookkeeping, a CRM, and even a simple accounting ledger. For a solo operator, that could be enough to replace several separate tools.

What I loved

Truly all-in-one. 17hats includes a built-in bookkeeping system that tracks income and expenses, plus a to-do list and a calendar. Most client management tools don’t touch accounting. If you’re a freelancer who wants one place for everything, this is appealing.

Native booking and calendar sync. Clients can book appointments through your customized page, and it syncs with Google Calendar or iCal. No extra integration needed. The calendar also shows your tasks and events, giving you a dashboard view of your day.

Price is competitive. The “Starter” plan at $15/mo (billed yearly) gives you unlimited clients and projects, invoicing, contracts, and booking. The “Pro” plan ($39/mo) adds automated email sequences, advanced reporting, and priority support. Even at the top tier, it’s comparable to the others.

Mobile app is functional. I tested the iOS app and could send invoices, check upcoming appointments, and view client notes. It’s not as polished as HoneyBook’s app, but it works.

Where it falls short

The interface feels dated. Let’s be honest: 17hats looks like it was designed in 2015. The menus are cluttered, the fonts are small, and some pages load slowly. It’s not terrible, but HoneyBook and Dubsado have much more modern UIs.

Automation is limited to templates. You can set up automated email sequences (e.g., send a thank-you email 3 days after a contract is signed), but there’s no condition-based workflow builder like Dubsado. And the sequences are linear—you can’t branch based on client actions.

Customer support is slow. I reached out via email with a question about integrating with QuickBooks. It took 48 hours to get a reply. Dubsado and HoneyBook both responded within a few hours during business hours.

No advanced reporting on lower plans. The basic plan only gives you a simple income report. For deeper analytics (project profitability, client lifetime value), you need the Pro plan.

Best for: Solopreneurs who want a single tool for CRM, invoicing, booking, and basic bookkeeping. Less ideal for teams or businesses that need sophisticated automation.

Detailed Feature Comparison

Proposals & Contracts

All three tools let you create professional proposals and contracts with e-signatures. Here’s how they differ:

  • Dubsado gives you the most control. You can create multi-page proposals with conditional logic (show/hide sections based on client choices). Contracts can include custom fields for client details, and e-signatures are legally binding. You can also set expiration dates for proposals.
  • HoneyBook offers a streamlined proposal builder with a drag-and-drop interface. It doesn’t support conditional logic, but you can add packages and pricing tables easily. Contracts are standard templates with e-signature.
  • 17hats has a simple editor for proposals and contracts. You can add text, images, and prices, but the formatting options are limited. It gets the job done but lacks polish.

Workflow Automations

This is where the biggest gap appears.

  • Dubsado: You build workflows with a visual editor. Triggers include form submissions, payment status changes, dates, and manual actions. You can add conditions (if client chooses Package A, send email X; if Package B, send email Y). It’s powerful but takes time to set up.
  • HoneyBook: Offers “Automations” that are basically email sequences. You can send a follow-up after a proposal is sent, or a reminder after an invoice is overdue. No conditional branching or task creation based on client choices.
  • 17hats: Has “Email Sequences” similar to HoneyBook. You can schedule a series of emails based on triggers like a new lead or a signed contract. No task automation or conditional logic.

Client Portal

All three offer a client portal where clients can view their projects, invoices, contracts, and messages. The quality varies:

  • Dubsado portal is customizable and shows a timeline of activity. Clients can upload files and leave notes. It’s functional but not pretty.
  • HoneyBook portal is clean and modern. Clients see a dashboard with upcoming tasks, invoices due, and messages. It feels more polished.
  • 17hats portal is basic. Clients can view invoices and contracts, but the interface is clunky and not very inviting.

Which Should You Choose?

Pick Dubsado if…

  • You manage multiple projects with complex workflows.
  • You need conditional automation (e.g., different emails based on package selected).
  • You want to avoid transaction fees.
  • You’re willing to invest time in setup.

Pick HoneyBook if…

  • You’re a creative professional (photographer, event planner, designer).
  • You want something that works out of the box with minimal fuss.
  • You need built-in booking and payment plans.
  • You don’t mind paying transaction fees for ease of use.

Pick 17hats if…

  • You’re a solo freelancer who wants one tool for everything.
  • You need basic bookkeeping without adding another app.
  • You prefer a low-cost option with unlimited clients.
  • You don’t need advanced automation.

Final Verdict

After testing all three with real client workflows, HoneyBook is the best choice for most small businesses—especially creative service providers. It strikes the best balance between ease of use, feature set, and client experience. The transaction fees are annoying, but the time saved in setup and daily use makes up for it.

However, if you’re willing to climb the learning curve, Dubsado is the more powerful tool long-term. You get more control, no transaction fees, and automation that can save you hours every week. For agencies or consultants with repeatable workflows, Dubsado pays for itself.

17hats is a solid budget option if you need basic CRM, invoicing, and booking, but its outdated interface and limited automation Make it hard to recommend over the other two unless you specifically need built-in bookkeeping.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Dubsado or HoneyBook better for photographers?

Both are popular among photographers. HoneyBook has a slight edge because of its booking page and proposal templates designed for photography sessions. But Dubsado’s workflow automation can handle complex client types (e.g., weddings vs. portraits) with different packages. If you shoot a lot of weddings with multiple albums and timelines, Dubsado might save you more time.

Can I use these tools for free?

None offer a permanent free plan. HoneyBook has a 7-day free trial, Dubsado and 17hats offer 14-day trials. You need to enter a credit card, but you can cancel before the trial ends.

Do these tools integrate with QuickBooks?

  • Dubsado: Yes, via Zapier or direct API (manual export options).
  • HoneyBook: Yes, native integration with QuickBooks Online (export invoices and payments).
  • 17hats: Yes, built-in bookkeeping can export reports, and there’s a QuickBooks sync on the Pro plan.

Which tool has the best mobile app?

HoneyBook’s app is the most polished. Dubsado’s app is functional but lacks some features (like building workflows). 17hats’ app is basic but covers the essentials (invoicing, calendar, messages).

Can I use these for a team?

  • Dubsado: Supports team members on the Premier plan ($40/mo) with permission levels.
  • HoneyBook: Team features are available on the Pro plan ($39/mo) and above, with roles for admin and staff.
  • 17hats: Team collaboration is limited to sharing access; no granular permission controls. Better for solo users.

Which is the cheapest overall?

17hats Starter at $15/mo is the cheapest entry point. But if you consider transaction fees, Dubsado can be cheaper for high-volume billing because it doesn’t add extra fees. HoneyBook becomes more expensive the more you bill.

Bottom line: Start your free trial with the tool that matches your workflow complexity. If you’re just starting out, go with HoneyBook. If you’re scaling and want to automate, go with Dubsado. Skip 17hats unless you absolutely need that built-in bookkeeping and are okay with a less modern experience.
