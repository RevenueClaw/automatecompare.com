Quick Verdict

If you run an online store or SaaS business and need developer-friendly tools, Stripe is the winner. If you’re a freelancer or solo operator who just wants a simple way to send invoices and get paid fast, PayPal still works fine. And if you have a physical retail shop or do pop-ups, Square is the obvious choice.

But here’s the honest truth: most small businesses should start with Stripe. It offers the best mix of features, transparent pricing, and global reach. PayPal is a close second for simplicity, but its hidden fees and clunky dashboard can frustrate you as you scale. Square is the king of in-person payments but lags behind online.

In 2026, the payment processor landscape hasn’t changed dramatically, but a few key updates matter. Let’s break it all down.

What Is Stripe?

Stripe is a payment infrastructure platform. It’s not just a “PayPal clone” – it’s a set of APIs that let you build exactly how you want to accept payments. Founded in 2010, Stripe now processes hundreds of billions of dollars annually. It’s the backbone for companies like Shopify, Lyft, and DoorDash, but it also works for a solo freelancer.

The key thing about Stripe is that it’s developer-first. If you can write a few lines of code (or use a no-code tool like Zapier), you can customize your checkout flow completely. Stripe Checkout is a prebuilt hosted payment page that looks professional and converts well.

For small businesses, Stripe offers:

  • Stripe Billing (subscriptions, invoicing, metered billing)
  • Stripe Connect (marketplace payouts)
  • Stripe Terminal (in-person card reading)
  • Stripe Atlas (incorporate a US company)
  • Stripe Tax (automated sales tax calculation)

It’s not perfect – the dashboard can be overwhelming, and customer support is mostly email/chat (no phone for basic plans). But the reliability and feature depth are unmatched.

What Is PayPal?

PayPal is the old guard. Founded in 1998, it was the first mainstream digital wallet. Today it’s owned by eBay (well, not anymore – spun off in 2015) and still widely used. For small businesses, PayPal offers:

  • PayPal Payments Standard (buy now buttons)
  • PayPal Payments Pro (custom checkout, $30/month)
  • PayPal Here (card reader for in-person)
  • PayPal Invoicing (free, with reminders)
  • PayPal Checkout (hosted page)

PayPal’s biggest advantage is trust. Millions of people already have a PayPal account and prefer to pay with it. If you sell to consumers who are wary of entering credit card info on a new site, PayPal gives them comfort.

But PayPal has gotten worse over the years. The fees are higher than Stripe for online transactions (2.99% + $0.49 vs 2.9% + $0.30). They hold funds for up to 21 days for new sellers. And their chargeback policy is notoriously seller-unfriendly.

Still, for a freelancer sending invoices to clients who demand PayPal, it’s hard to avoid. And the PayPal Business Debit Card gives you 1% cash back on purchases – a nice perk.

What Is Square?

Square started in 2009 with a simple idea: let anyone accept credit cards with a tiny card reader plugged into a phone. Today Square is a full ecosystem: payments, POS, online store, appointment booking, payroll, and even business loans.

For small businesses, Square shines in three areas:

  • In-person payments – The best hardware (Square Reader, Square Stand, Square Register) and software (Square POS) for retail and restaurants.
  • Transparent pricing – No monthly fees for basic accounts. Flat rates for all card types (including Amex). No hidden fees.
  • Fast funding – With Square Checking, you can get next-day deposits (even on weekends) for a small fee.

Square’s online payment tools are decent but not as powerful as Stripe. The API is good but not as flexible. And Square only works in a handful of countries – if you plan to sell globally, Stripe is better.

Square also offers a free card reader (just pay shipping) and a free POS app. For a coffee shop or boutique, it’s the obvious choice.

Feature Comparison

Online Checkout

  • Stripe: Customizable Checkout (hosted or embedded). Supports Apple Pay, Google Pay, Link (autofill), and 40+ payment methods. Conversion-optimized.
  • PayPal: PayPal Checkout (hosted) or Payments Pro (custom). Supports PayPal, Venmo, credit/debit cards, and some buy now pay later (PayPal Pay Later).
  • Square: Square Online Checkout (hosted) or Square APIs. Supports Apple Pay, Google Pay, Afterpay, and cards. Less customizable than Stripe.

Winner: Stripe – more payment methods, better customization, higher conversion rates.

Recurring Billing

  • Stripe: Stripe Billing is a full-featured subscription engine. Supports metered billing, proration, coupons, dunning, and multiple plans. Integrates with Zapier, Hubspot, etc.
  • PayPal: Recurring payments via PayPal Subscriptions. Basic – can’t do metered billing easily. Limited webhooks.
  • Square: Square Subscriptions – works for simple recurring invoices or memberships. Not as robust as Stripe.

Winner: Stripe – no contest.

Invoicing

  • Stripe: Stripe Invoicing – free, send via email or link. Supports recurring invoices, partial payments, and payment links. Good for B2B.
  • PayPal: PayPal Invoicing – free, with templates, reminders, and online payment. Works well for freelancers.
  • Square: Square Invoices – free, with customization, scheduling, and payment tracking. Good for service businesses.

Winner: Tie – all three offer solid free invoicing. Stripe is best for recurring; PayPal is easiest for one-off; Square is best for service-based businesses.

Point of Sale (In-Person)

  • Stripe: Stripe Terminal – you can build your own POS using their SDKs. No pre-built POS app. Requires development work.
  • PayPal: PayPal Zettle – a decent POS app with inventory management. The card reader is small and portable.
  • Square: Square POS – the gold standard. Full-featured POS with inventory, employee management, CRM, and marketing tools. Multiple hardware options.

Winner: Square – by a mile.

International Payments

  • Stripe: Supports 135+ currencies, 45+ countries. Payouts to 70+ countries. Excellent multi-currency support.
  • PayPal: Supports 200+ markets, but many countries have restrictions. Currency conversion fees are high (2.5% above wholesale).
  • Square: Only 6 countries. US, UK, Canada, Japan, Australia, Ireland. No multi-currency support for sellers.

Winner: Stripe – global reach without the headache.

  • Stripe: $0 – no monthly fees for basic account. Stripe Billing has volume discounts.
  • PayPal: $0 for Payments Standard. $30/month for Payments Pro (if you want custom checkout).
  • Square: $0 for basic. Square for Retail starts at $60/month (includes advanced inventory and staff management).

  • PayPal: $20 per chargeback – non-refundable even if you win. This is a huge pain point.
  • Square: $0 for the first $250 in chargebacks per month, then $15 each. No fee if you win? Actually Square doesn't refund the fee even if you win – same as PayPal. But the first $250 free is generous.

Winner on pricing: Stripe – lower online rates, lower chargeback fees (refundable), no monthly fee for basic. Square is cheaper for in-person. PayPal is the most expensive overall.

Pros and Cons of Stripe

Pros

  • Developer-friendly: Best API docs, SDKs for every language, webhooks, etc.
  • Global reach: 135+ currencies, 45+ countries. Easy to sell internationally.
  • Subscription tools: Stripe Billing is the best out-of-the-box solution for recurring revenue.
  • Transparent fees: No hidden charges, no monthly fees, no setup fees.
  • Fast payouts: 7-day rolling (can be reduced to 2 days with good history). Instant payouts available for 1% fee.
  • Integrations: Works with Zapier, HubSpot, Convertkit, and thousands of other tools.

Cons

  • Learning curve: The dashboard is complex. If you’re not technical, you might feel lost.
  • Customer support: No phone support for basic accounts. Email and chat only. Response times can be slow.
  • Account holds: Stripe can freeze your funds if they detect unusual activity (e.g., high-risk business). This is rare but scary.
  • In-person limitations: Stripe Terminal is not a full POS – you need to build your own interface. Not for retail.
  • No built-in invoicing templates: Invoicing is functional but not as polished as PayPal or Square.

Pros and Cons of PayPal

Pros

  • Ubiquity: Millions of consumers already have PayPal accounts. They trust it.
  • Easy to start: Just create an account, copy/paste a button, and you’re accepting payments.
  • Invoice tools: Free, professional-looking invoices with reminders. Great for freelancers.
  • PayPal Pay Later: Offer buy now pay later (Pay in 4, Pay Monthly) – can boost conversion.
  • Debit card: PayPal Business Debit Mastercard gives 1% cash back on purchases (no annual fee).

Cons

  • Higher fees: 2.99% + $0.49 is more expensive than Stripe and Square.
  • Fund holds: New sellers often have funds held for 21 days. Even after that, holds can happen for “high-risk” transactions.
  • Chargeback policy: $20 non-refundable fee. And PayPal often sides with buyers in disputes.
  • Clunky dashboard: Navigation is confusing. Reports are limited.
  • Limited customization: PayPal Checkout is less flexible than Stripe. Payments Pro costs $30/month and still isn’t as good.
  • Currency conversion: High markups (2.5% above wholesale). Bad for international sales.

Pros and Cons of Square

Pros

  • Best in-person: The POS hardware and software are top-notch. Free reader, affordable stand, full register.
  • Transparent pricing: No monthly fees for basic, flat rates for all card types.
  • Fast funding: Next-day deposit with Square Checking (even weekends). Instant transfer for 1.5% fee.
  • Free tools: Invoicing, online store, appointment booking, inventory management – all free (basic).
  • No chargeback fee for first $250/month: Great for low-dispute businesses.
  • Square ecosystem: Payroll, email marketing, loyalty, gift cards – all integrated.

Cons

  • Limited international support: Only 6 countries. If you plan to expand globally, Square won’t work.
  • Online checkout is basic: Customization options are limited. No hosted checkout that’s as polished as Stripe.
  • Account stability: Square is known for abruptly closing accounts of “high-risk” businesses (e.g., vape shops, CBD). No warning.
  • Higher in-person fee for keyed-in transactions: If you manually enter card numbers, it’s 3.5% + $0.15.
  • No true API for subscriptions: Square Subscriptions is basic – no metered billing, no proration.

Who Should Choose Stripe?

Choose Stripe if:

  • You run an online business (e-commerce, SaaS, membership site).
  • You need subscriptions with advanced features (metered billing, coupons, dunning).
  • You sell internationally or plan to.
  • You have some technical ability (or a developer) to customize the checkout.
  • You want the lowest online transaction fees overall.
  • You use tools like Zapier, HubSpot, or ConvertKit and need deep integrations.

Stripe is the best all-around payment processor for small businesses that operate primarily online. It’s not the easiest to set up, but it’s the most powerful.

Who Should Choose PayPal?

Choose PayPal if:

  • You’re a freelancer or solo operator who sends invoices to clients.
  • Your clients expect to pay via PayPal (common in creative industries, consulting).
  • You want a simple button or link to accept payments without any coding.
  • You need buy now pay later options (PayPal Pay Later is widely used).
  • You don’t mind higher fees for the convenience of an existing user base.

PayPal is still the go-to for many freelancers because of its ubiquity. But be aware of the fund holds and chargeback fees. If you can, use Stripe for online payments and keep PayPal as a secondary option.

Who Should Choose Square?

Choose Square if:

  • You run a physical retail store, restaurant, or service business that takes in-person payments.
  • You need a POS system with inventory, employee management, and customer profiles.
  • You want a free card reader and fast funding.
  • You operate only in the US, UK, Canada, Japan, Australia, or Ireland.
  • You want an all-in-one ecosystem (payments, payroll, marketing, appointments).

Square is unmatched for brick-and-mortar businesses. If you also sell online, Square Online is decent for a basic store, but you might outgrow it. For a coffee shop or boutique, Square is the clear winner.

Final Verdict

Stripe is the best payment processor for most small businesses in 2026. It offers the lowest online fees, the most features, the best developer tools, and the widest global reach. If your business is online-first, Stripe should be your primary processor.

Square is the best for in-person businesses. If you have a physical store or do events, Square’s POS hardware and software are superior.
