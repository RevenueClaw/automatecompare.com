You’ve got a small business to run. The last thing you want is to waste time on clunky payment tools or get hit with fees you didn’t expect. Stripe, PayPal, and Square are the three giants in online payments, but they’re not interchangeable. Each one has a sweet spot. Your choice depends on whether you’re selling online, in person, or both, and how much you care about developer flexibility versus plug-and-play simplicity.

I’ve processed payments through all three over the last five years (for my own side projects and for clients), and I’ve seen where each shines and where each stumbles. Let’s break them down so you can pick the right one for 2026.

Stripe: The Developer’s Darling

Stripe is built for businesses that want total control. If you’re launching a subscription SaaS, a marketplace, or a custom checkout flow, Stripe’s API is the gold standard. It’s not just a payment button — it’s a full toolkit.

What Stripe does really well:

  • Customization. You can build a checkout page that matches your brand down to the pixel. Stripe Elements let you embed individual fields (card number, expiry, CVC) so you never redirect customers away from your site.
  • Subscriptions & billing. Stripe has the best recurring billing engine out of the three. You can create pricing models with tiers, trials, prorations, and usage-based billing without needing third-party add-ons.
  • Global payments. Stripe supports 135+ currencies and accepts local payment methods like Alipay, WeChat Pay, iDEAL, and Bancontact. Perfect if you sell internationally.
  • Fraud protection. Radar (Stripe’s fraud tool) uses machine learning and you can write custom rules. Free version has basic protection; Radar for Fraud Teams costs extra ($0.02 per evaluation).

Where Stripe falls short:

  • No built-in POS hardware. Stripe Terminal exists, but you need to buy third-party readers or use their SDK. Not ideal for coffee shops or pop-up booths.
  • Steep learning curve. Non-technical users will struggle. You can use Stripe Checkout (a hosted page) to avoid coding, but it’s still less turnkey than PayPal or Square.
  • Customer support. It’s mostly email and chat. Phone support is limited to higher-tier plans. If your payment flow breaks at 8 PM on a Saturday, you’re on your own.

Pricing: No monthly fee. Online: 2.9% + $0.30. In-person: 2.7% + $0.05. Chargeback fee: $15. Payouts: next day (free) or instant (1.5% fee).

Best for: Developers, B2B SaaS, platforms, any business that needs a fully custom checkout.

PayPal: The Familiar Giant

PayPal is everywhere. Chances are, your customers already have an account. That trust factor is huge for reducing cart abandonment — especially for first-time buyers who don’t want to type in their card details.

What PayPal does really well:

  • Buyer trust. The PayPal logo alone can boost conversions. Many shoppers prefer to pay with PayPal because they know the buyer protection policy.
  • PayPal Checkout. Their latest hosted checkout is fast and mobile-friendly. You can add a “Pay with PayPal” button to your site with a few lines of code, no developer needed.
  • Marketplaces & payouts. If you run a multi-vendor marketplace, PayPal Payouts lets you send money to hundreds of sellers at once. Venmo integration is also a nice touch for peer-to-peer.
  • PayPal Zettle. They acquired iZettle in 2018 and rebranded it as PayPal Zettle. It’s a decent POS system for in-person sales, with card readers and a free app.

Where PayPal falls short:

  • Higher fees. Online transaction fee is 2.99% + $0.49 unless you use PayPal Checkout (2.59% + $0.49). That $0.49 per transaction adds up fast — especially for low-ticket items.
  • Account freezes. PayPal has a notorious history of holding funds or freezing accounts without warning. It’s improved, but the risk is still there. If you sell “high-risk” items (e.g., supplements, digital courses), you might get flagged.
  • Clunky dashboard. The seller dashboard is bloated with upsells. Finding transaction reports or dispute details can take five clicks.
  • No true API-first design. You can integrate PayPal via REST APIs, but it’s not as clean as Stripe. Webhooks and subscription management feel like an afterthought.

Pricing: Free account. Online: 2.99% + $0.49 (standard), 2.59% + $0.49 (Checkout). In-person: 2.7% (Zettle). Chargeback: $20. Instant transfers: 1.5% fee (cap $10).

Best for: Online stores that want quick setup, marketplaces, businesses with customers who already trust PayPal.

Square: The Omnichannel All-Rounder

Square started as a simple card reader for small businesses, but it’s grown into a full commerce ecosystem. If you sell both online and in person — or run a restaurant or retail shop — Square’s unified backend is hard to beat.

What Square does really well:

  • In-person payments. Square’s card readers are affordable ($10-$250). Their POS app works on tablets and phones, and they offer specialized software for retail, restaurants, and appointments.
  • Square Online. You get a free online store (with Square payments built in). It’s not as flexible as Shopify or WooCommerce, but for simple product catalogs it works well.
  • Inventory & CRM. Square’s dashboard syncs inventory across online and offline sales. You can track customers, send email campaigns, and manage staff — all from one place.
  • No chargeback fee. Square doesn’t charge a separate chargeback fee (though you still pay the disputed amount if you lose). That saves $15-$20 per dispute compared to Stripe and PayPal.
  • Square for Restaurants. A dedicated POS with menu management, table mapping, and kitchen display integration. No other processor offers this out of the box.

Where Square falls short:

  • Limited global reach. Square is only available in the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and Japan. If you need to sell in Europe or Asia outside those countries, you’re stuck.
  • Online customization. Square Online stores are basic. You can’t change the checkout URL, and customization is limited compared to WooCommerce or a custom Stripe integration.
  • Higher in-person fees than Stripe. Square’s basic in-person rate is 2.6% + $0.10. Stripe Terminal is 2.7% + $0.05 — but for small transactions, Square’s fixed $0.10 can actually be cheaper than Stripe’s $0.05 if the sale is under $10. Still, Square’s rate is slightly higher on larger transactions.
  • Payment holds. Square occasionally holds funds for new accounts, especially if you process large amounts quickly. They’re more lenient than PayPal, but it happens.

Pricing: Free plan (2.6% + $0.10 in-person, 2.9% + $0.30 online). Square POS plan: $29/mo (includes lower in-person rate of 2.5% + $0.10). No chargeback fee. Instant transfers: 1.5% fee.

Best for: Retail shops, restaurants, businesses that need a unified online + offline system.

Feature Deep Dive: What Actually Matters

Subscription Billing

  • Stripe: Best. Native support for complex billing models. You can set up metered billing, tiered pricing, and prorated upgrades without any plugins.
  • PayPal: Works for simple subscriptions via PayPal Subscriptions, but no advanced features. You’ll need a third-party app for usage-based billing.
  • Square: Square Subscriptions is decent for flat-rate recurring payments, but lacks flexibility. No proration or trial customization without workarounds.

Chargeback Handling

  • Stripe: Provides detailed evidence templates. You can submit proof via API or dashboard. $15 fee per chargeback, refunded if you win.
  • PayPal: Buyer-friendly. Merchants often lose disputes even with tracking proof. $20 fee, non-refundable even if you win (they call it a “dispute fee”).
  • Square: No separate chargeback fee. Dispute process is straightforward, but Square’s protection is average. They’ll side with you if you have strong evidence.

Payout Speed

All three offer next-day standard settlements. For instant access:

  • Stripe: Instant Payouts (1.5% fee, minimum $0.50). Available 24/7.
  • PayPal: Instant Transfer to bank (1.5% fee, capped at $10). Also available 24/7.
  • Square: Instant Transfer (1.5% fee, minimum $0.25). Available 24/7.

Square’s minimum fee is the lowest, but the differences are minor.

Developer Experience

  • Stripe: Unbeatable. Documentation is clear, SDKs are well-maintained, and the API is RESTful. Test mode works great.
  • PayPal: Clunky. Two different API versions (REST and Classic NVP). Webhooks can be inconsistent. Sandbox testing sometimes breaks.
  • Square: Good. Square has modern APIs and a developer portal, but fewer third-party libraries compared to Stripe.

Which One Should You Pick?

Pick Stripe if: You're a developer or have one on your team. You need custom checkouts, recurring billing with complicated tiers, or you sell to international customers. Stripe is the best choice for SaaS, membership sites, and platforms.

Pick PayPal if: You want the fastest setup for a simple online store, or you run a marketplace that needs to pay out multiple vendors. Also good if your customer base is older and more likely to trust PayPal. But watch those fees.

Pick Square if: You have a physical store, food truck, or pop-up shop. Square’s POS ecosystem is unbeatable for in-person sales. If you also sell online, Square Online works fine as a secondary channel. Restaurants should absolutely go with Square.

My honest verdict? For most small businesses in 2026, Stripe is the best all-around processor — if you have the technical chops or budget for a developer. If you don't, Square is the safer bet, especially if you sell in person at all. PayPal is my last choice unless you’re in a specific niche (marketplaces, buyer trust leverage).

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use Stripe and PayPal together?

Yes, many businesses do. You can add PayPal as a payment option on a Stripe-powered checkout using Stripe’s PayPal integration (available in the US). Or use a payment gateway like Spreedly to route transactions through multiple processors.

Does Square charge a monthly fee?

The basic Square account is free. Square POS plans start at $29/month for retail and $60/month for restaurants. The free plan includes all the essential features.

Which processor has the lowest fees for small transactions?

For a $5 transaction:

  • Stripe: 2.9% + $0.30 = $0.45 (9% of sale)
  • PayPal: 2.99% + $0.49 = $0.64 (12.8% of sale)
  • Square: 2.9% + $0.30 = $0.45 (9% of sale)

Stripe and Square are tied. PayPal is significantly worse for low-ticket items.

Can I accept international payments with Square?

Not easily. Square only processes in the countries where it’s based. For cross-border sales, you’d need Stripe or PayPal.

Do these processors work with WooCommerce?

All three have official WooCommerce plugins. Stripe’s is the most reliable. PayPal’s plugin is functional but sometimes conflicts with caching plugins. Square’s plugin syncs inventory between WooCommerce and Square’s POS.

What’s the best processor for a nonprofit?

Stripe offers discounted rates for registered nonprofits through its Stripe for Nonprofits program (2.2% + $0.30). PayPal also offers discounted rates (2.2% + $0.30) via PayPal Giving Fund. Square doesn’t have a specific nonprofit discount.

Bottom line: There’s no one-size-fits-all. Evaluate where you sell, how technical you are, and what your customers expect. Then pick the tool that fits your actual workflow — not the one with the flashiest ads.
