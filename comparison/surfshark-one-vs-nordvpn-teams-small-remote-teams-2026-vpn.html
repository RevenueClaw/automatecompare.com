Surfshark One vs NordVPN Teams for Small Remote Teams in 2026: VPN, Antivirus and Alerts Compared

Here's a scenario I run into constantly. A five-person agency goes fully remote. Everyone works from a laptop, half of them from coffee shops. Someone's client asks for a security questionnaire. Suddenly the owner is googling "VPN for team" at 11pm and staring at two very different products that look like they do the same thing.

They don't. That's the whole problem.

Surfshark One and NordVPN Teams get lumped together because both come from the VPN world and both cost money per person. But they're built for different buyers. Surfshark One is a protection bundle aimed at individuals, with a business tier bolted on top. NordVPN Teams — which is now called NordLayer — is a network-access product aimed at IT admins, with the consumer VPN DNA still visible underneath.

If you pick the wrong one, you either pay for admin tools nobody uses, or you hand five freelancers a pile of separate logins and hope for the best.

Let me break down what actually matters: what's in the box, what it costs for a small team, and where each one gets annoying.

Quick answer before the details

For a team of 1–10 people with no IT department, Surfshark One is the better buy. You get VPN, real antivirus, dark-web breach alerts and ad blocking in one subscription, at roughly half the per-seat price of NordLayer. The catch is that team management is thinner.

Choose NordVPN Teams / NordLayer if someone on your team will actually use the admin console — SSO, device posture checks, per-user access rules, provisioning. If you're not going to touch those, you're paying for software you'll ignore.

First, a naming problem worth clearing up

"NordVPN Teams" technically doesn't exist anymore as a product name. Nord rebranded that offering to NordLayer back in 2021. In 2026, people still search "NordVPN Teams" and land in one of two places:

  • NordLayer — the successor. A cloud-based access network (Zero Trust / SASE-ish) with per-seat pricing, an admin console, SSO and MFA.
  • NordVPN Business — consumer NordVPN sold with a central billing dashboard and a few admin features.

They're different products with different price tags, and Nord's own site doesn't always Make that obvious. If a comparison article tells you "NordVPN Teams costs $X", check which of those two they mean.

Surfshark has a similar split, just less confusing:

  • Surfshark One — the consumer bundle: VPN + Antivirus + Search + Alert.
  • Surfshark One+ — adds Incogni, the data-removal service.
  • Surfshark Business — per-seat licensing of the One bundle with an admin panel.

For the rest of this piece, "Surfshark One" means the bundle (and I'll flag where the Business tier differs), and "NordVPN Teams" means NordLayer plus the Business plan where relevant.

What's actually in each bundle

Surfshark One: four tools, one bill

Surfshark One bundles four things:

  • Surfshark VPN — 4,500+ servers in 100 countries, WireGuard-based protocol (WireGuard), unlimited simultaneous devices on one account, RAM-only servers, independently audited no-logs policy.
  • Surfshark Antivirus — real-time file protection, scheduled scans, Webcam protection, and a "block ads and trackers" layer on Windows, macOS, Android and iOS. It also runs an on-demand scan you can trigger from the app.
  • Surfshark Alert — dark-web monitoring. You feed it email addresses, and it flags breaches that expose them. (Yes, this is the same category of function as Have I Been Pwned, but it keeps watching instead of checking once.)
  • Surfshark Search — a private search engine with no ads or logs.

One+ adds Incogni, which sends removal requests to data brokers on your behalf. For a solo consultant whose home address shows up on people-search sites, that's genuinely useful. For a five-person team, it's a nice-to-have.

NordLayer (NordVPN Teams): access control first

NordLayer is not an antivirus product. It's a way to give remote people a private path into company resources, with rules.

  • Per-seat licensing with an admin console where you add users, assign them to gateways, and set activity rules.
  • SSO and MFA — SAML-based single sign-on with Google Workspace, Microsoft Entra ID, Okta.
  • Device posture checks — block a device that's missing a screen lock or has an out-of-date OS.
  • Dedicated IP and IP allowlisting, so your team can reach a client's firewall or a cloud database with a fixed address.
  • Threat Blocker / DNS filtering at the network level.
  • Smart Connect-style automatic gateway selection
