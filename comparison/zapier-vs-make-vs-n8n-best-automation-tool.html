Zapier vs Make (Integromat) vs n8n: Best Automation Tool in 2026

A client books a call. You want that booking to create a calendar event, add a row to your CRM, send a Slack ping, and drop a follow-up task in your project tool. Four steps, no manual work. That's the promise all three of these platforms Make — and they deliver it in very different ways, at very different prices.

I've built production workflows in all three. I've also watched a $9/month Make scenario quietly save a business owner 15 hours a week, and I've watched someone burn $180/month on Zapier because nobody told them how task counting works.

This comparison covers what actually matters: the pricing models (they're not the same unit, and that's the single biggest thing people get wrong), the real feature differences, and which one you should pick based on how you work. Prices below are current as of early 2026 and these companies change them often — verify before you commit.

The short answer

  • Zapier — easiest to learn, biggest app library by a wide margin, most expensive per step. Best if your time is worth more than your subscription.
  • Make — the best balance of price and power for most small businesses. Visual builder, real error handling, roughly 3–5x cheaper than Zapier at equivalent volume.
  • n8n — the only one you can self-host. Cheapest at high volume, most powerful for developers, and the only realistic option if you can't send customer data through a third party.

The pricing models are not the same unit (read this first)

This is where most comparisons go wrong, so let's get it out of the way.

PlatformBilling unitWhat counts
ZapierTaskEach successful action step in a Zap. Triggers are free. Filtered-out Zaps don't count.
MakeOperationEach module that runs, including triggers.
n8n (Cloud)ExecutionOne run of an entire workflow, no matter how many nodes it has.

Here's why that matters. Take a five-step workflow: new Shopify order → add row to Google Sheets → post to Slack → create invoice in QuickBooks → email the customer.

At 400 orders a month:

  • Zapier: 4 tasks per order (trigger is free) = 1,600 tasks/month. That lands you on the Professional 2,000-task tier, roughly $49/month billed annually.
  • Make: 5 operations per order = 2,000 operations/month. The Core plan gives you 10,000 operations for $9/month. You're using 20% of your quota.
  • n8n Cloud: 1 execution per order = 400 executions/month. Starter covers 2,500 for about $24/month. Self-hosted? $0 plus your server bill.

Same workflow. Roughly $588/year on Zapier, $108/year on Make, $288/year on n8n Cloud, or about $60–120/year in VPS costs if you self-host n8n.

Zapier's per-step pricing punishes long workflows. n8n's per-execution pricing rewards them. Make sits in the middle and is generous with volume. If your workflows are short (two or three steps), the gap narrows a lot. If they're long, it doesn't.

Zapier: the one you can hand to anyone

Zapier's advantage isn't a single feature — it's that a non-technical person can open it and have a working automation in twenty minutes. The editor walks you through each step, tests your connections, and tells you in plain English when something's misconfigured.

What it does well

  • 8,000+ app integrations. Roughly double Make's library. If you use a niche SaaS tool, Zapier is the most likely place to find a connector for it.
  • Zapier Tables, Interfaces, and Canvas. Not just automation — you get a lightweight database, a form/page builder, and a diagramming tool that connect directly to your Zaps. For a solo operator, this can replace three separate subscriptions.
  • AI features that are actually usable. Zapier Agents, Copilot for building Zaps from a prompt, and AI steps that parse unstructured text. The AI-by-prompt workflow builder is genuinely useful for people who don't know what a webhook is.
  • Reliability at scale. Zapier's uptime and retry handling are the best of the three. Failed steps retry automatically, and you get email alerts when a Zap turns off.

Where it falls short

  • Cost. There's no way around it. High-volume, multi-step workflows get expensive fast.
  • Rigid logic. Paths, filters, and Formatter steps exist, but building complex branching or looping logic in Zapier is painful compared to Make's router or n8n's code nodes.
  • No self-hosting. Your data flows through Zapier's servers, full stop. That's a dealbreaker for some regulated industries.
  • Limited code. The Code by Zapier step exists but has a short execution limit and no package support.
