Quick Verdict

Hubspot wins if you need a full CRM + email marketing in one platform and have the budget to grow into it. ActiveCampaign wins if you want advanced automation at a fraction of the cost and don’t need a full CRM suite. For most small businesses in 2026, ActiveCampaign delivers better value—especially if you’re running complex email sequences without the overhead of a sales pipeline.

Comparison Overview

HubSpot and ActiveCampaign are two of the most popular email marketing platforms for small businesses, but they approach the market from different angles. HubSpot started as a CRM and added email marketing; ActiveCampaign started as an email marketing automation tool and later built its own CRM. The result: two platforms that overlap in some areas but excel in different ones.

In 2026, both tools have matured significantly. HubSpot’s free tier remains a powerful entry point, but its paid plans have crept up in price. ActiveCampaign has improved its email deliverability and added more native integrations. The question isn’t which is better overall—it’s which fits your specific workflow.

What is HubSpot?

HubSpot is an all-in-one inbound marketing, sales, and CRM platform. Its Marketing Hub includes email marketing, landing pages, forms, SEO tools, and analytics. The free tier gives you core CRM features plus basic email (2,000 email sends/month, limited templates). Paid plans unlock more contacts, automation, and advanced reporting.

For email marketing specifically, HubSpot shines when you want to tie email campaigns directly to your CRM data. Every email sent is logged against a contact record, and you can trigger sequences based on deal stages, lead scores, or lifecycle changes. The drag-and-drop editor is intuitive, and the built-in A/B testing is solid.

However, HubSpot’s pricing scales quickly. The Marketing Hub Starter ($45/month) limits you to 1,000 contacts and 5 lists. The Professional tier ($800/month) is where real automation lives—and that’s out of reach for many small businesses.

Key features in 2026:

  • Unified CRM with contact, company, deal, and ticket objects
  • Visual automation builder (with branching logic in Professional+)
  • Smart content (personalise emails based on CRM data)
  • AI-powered send time optimisation
  • Native integration with 1,500+ apps via HubSpot App Marketplace

What is ActiveCampaign?

ActiveCampaign is an email marketing and automation platform that also includes a built-in CRM. It’s known for its powerful conditional automation—you can create complex “if this, then that” sequences without writing code. The platform also offers predictive sending, site tracking, and split automations.

ActiveCampaign’s CRM is more basic than HubSpot’s—you get contact management, deal pipelines, and task tracking, but no ticket system or company-level custom objects. However, the email automation engine is arguably the best in its price range. You can build automations that branch based on opens, clicks, page visits, deals won/lost, and more.

Pricing is more accessible: Starter starts at $15/month for 1,000 contacts, and the Plus plan ($49/month) unlocks conditional automation and landing pages. The Professional plan ($79/month) adds predictive sending and split automations.

Key features in 2026:

  • Advanced visual automation builder with conditions, goals, and site tracking
  • Predictive sending (AI picks best send time per contact)
  • Lead scoring (rule-based and predictive)
  • Built-in landing pages and forms (on Plus+)
  • SMS marketing (add-on)
  • Native integrations with 870+ apps (Shopify, WooCommerce, Zapier, etc.)

HubSpot’s Marketing Hub Professional (needed for conditional automation) starts at $800/month for 1,000 contacts—that’s a huge jump. ActiveCampaign’s Plus plan ($49) includes conditional automation and is far more affordable.

Hidden costs:

  • HubSpot charges for additional emails beyond the monthly send limit (typically $0.001/email overage).

Pros and Cons: HubSpot

Pros:

  • Best-in-class CRM integration – every email is tied to a contact record
  • Free tier is generous and useful for startups
  • Excellent onboarding and support (on paid plans)
  • Rich reporting dashboards (especially Professional+)
  • Huge app marketplace (1,500+ integrations)
  • Smart content personalisation using CRM fields

Cons:

  • Expensive for advanced automation ($800/mo for Professional)
  • Email-only plans are limited; you’re pushed toward the full Marketing Hub
  • Automation builder is less flexible than ActiveCampaign’s (no true conditional splits on Starter)
  • Overages can be costly
  • Learning curve for the full CRM can overwhelm small business owners

Pros and Cons: ActiveCampaign

Pros:

  • Powerful automation with conditional branching at a low price ($49/mo for Plus)
  • Excellent deliverability (dedicated IPs available on Professional)
  • Predictive sending and lead scoring included in Plus+
  • Flat pricing per contact – no sudden jumps in features
  • Good for ecommerce with deep Shopify/WooCommerce integrations
  • SMS marketing add-on works well alongside email

Cons:

  • CRM is basic – no companies object, no tickets, limited pipeline views
  • No free plan (only 14-day trial)
  • Automation builder can be intimidating for beginners
  • Reporting is decent but not as polished as HubSpot’s
  • Limited API rate limits on lower plans

Who Should Choose HubSpot?

Choose HubSpot if:

  • You need a full CRM with sales pipeline management alongside email marketing
  • Your team is already using HubSpot for sales or service and wants everything unified
  • You have the budget to invest in Professional or Enterprise tiers for advanced automation
  • You value a polished, all-in-one interface with strong customer support
  • You want to track email performance against deal revenue smoothly

Best for: Growing B2B companies, agencies managing multiple clients, and businesses that outgrow spreadsheets and need a true CRM.

Who Should Choose ActiveCampaign?

Choose ActiveCampaign if:

  • Email automation is your primary need and you want maximum flexibility for the price
  • You’re an ecommerce store owner who needs to trigger sequences based on purchase behaviour
  • You’re a solopreneur or small team with a tight budget (under $100/month)
  • You don’t need a full CRM – just contact management and deal tracking
  • You value deliverability and want dedicated IPs without spending $800/month

Best for: Online retailers, content creators, consultants, and service businesses that run complex email sequences.

Final Verdict

For most small businesses in 2026, ActiveCampaign is the better value – especially if your budget is under $200/month. You get advanced automation, good deliverability, and a capable (if basic) CRM. HubSpot only makes sense if you need the full CRM suite or have the budget for its Professional tier.

FAQ

Can I use HubSpot and ActiveCampaign together?

Yes, you can integrate them via Zapier or native apps, but it’s messy. You’ll have duplicate data and sync issues. Better to pick one.

Which platform has better email deliverability?

ActiveCampaign edges ahead, especially on Professional+ with dedicated IPs. HubSpot’s deliverability is good but requires careful warm-up and list hygiene.

Does ActiveCampaign have a free plan?

No. Only a 14-day free trial. HubSpot offers a permanent free plan with limited features.

Can I migrate from one to the other?

Yes. Both offer import tools. Expect some manual work for automation templates and custom fields.

Which is better for ecommerce email flows?

ActiveCampaign. Its native integrations with Shopify, WooCommerce, and BigCommerce are deeper than HubSpot’s (which requires the more expensive Marketing Hub Professional for ecommerce triggers).

Do I need coding skills to use these platforms?

No. Both have drag-and-drop editors and visual automation builders. ActiveCampaign’s automation is more powerful but also more complex to set up initially.
