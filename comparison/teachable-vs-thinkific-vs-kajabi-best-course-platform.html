Teachable vs Thinkific vs Kajabi: Which Course Platform Actually Works for Small Business Creators?

If you’re a coach, consultant, or solo creator looking to sell your knowledge online, you’ve probably heard the big three names: Teachable, Thinkific, and Kajabi. They all let you create and sell courses, but they’re not the same thing. Pick the wrong one, and you’ll either pay for features you never use or run into roadblocks when your business grows.

I’ve built courses on all three platforms, migrated clients between them, and helped dozens of small business owners choose the right fit. Here’s what I actually found.

What You’re Really Choosing Between

Before we get into the weeds, here’s the quick distinction:

  • Teachable is the simplest, most beginner-friendly option. It’s built for course creation first, community second.
  • Thinkific gives you more control over pricing and customization without making you feel overwhelmed.
  • Kajabi is the all-in-one platform that tries to replace your email marketing, website builder, and payment processor—but it costs more.

Which one works for you depends on how much complexity you want to manage, and how much you’re willing to spend.

Detailed Feature Breakdown

Teachable: The “Just Get It Done” Platform

Teachable has been around since 2013 and it shows in the polish. The course builder is drag-and-drop, and you can have a basic course live in under an hour. It handles video hosting, quizzes, certificates, and course comments.

What stands out:

  • Simple pricing model – No transaction fees on the paid plans, which is rare.
  • Built-in affiliate program – You can recruit affiliates without third-party tools.
  • Student engagement tools – Drip content, course completion certificates, and a decent quiz builder.

Where it falls short:

  • No native email marketing – You’ll need to connect Mailchimp, ConvertKit, or something else.
  • Design limitations – The course player looks fine, but customizing the look of your school takes work and often requires custom CSS.
  • Community features are basic – You get a comments section, not a real community hub.

Best for: Solo creators who want to sell a single course or a small library without fuss.

Thinkific: The “I Want Control” Option

Thinkific positions itself as the platform that doesn’t lock you in. You can sell courses individually, offer memberships, run bundles, and set your own pricing. It also has a free plan that actually works (though with some limits).

What stands out:

  • Flexible pricing – You can charge per course, subscription, or bundle. Plus you can set free trials and coupons easily.
  • Course builder is intuitive – Drag-and-drop, with sections, lessons, and multimedia support. No coding needed.
  • Native community – Each course can have its own discussion space, which helps with engagement.
  • Good integrations – Connects with Mailchimp, Zapier, Stripe, PayPal, and more.

Where it falls short:

  • Video hosting is limited – On the free and Basic plan, you’re capped at storage and bandwidth. Heavier users need the higher tiers.
  • Customization is powerful but can be fiddly – The theme editor gives you control, but it’s not as smooth as Teachable’s out-of-the-box look.
  • No built-in affiliate system – You need a third-party tool like FirstPromoter or ReferralCandy.

Best for: Creators who want to offer different pricing models and build a community around their courses.

Kajabi: The “Everything but the Kitchen Sink” Solution

Kajabi is the most expensive of the three, and it tries to replace your entire tech stack. It includes a website builder, email marketing, sales funnel builder, and a pipeline for automations. You don’t need separate tools for landing pages, email sequences, or checkout pages.

What stands out:

  • All-in-one – You can create a landing page, set up a three-email sequence, and sell a course without leaving Kajabi.
  • Sales pipeline – It has a built-in CRM that tracks leads and customers. Useful if you’re doing high-ticket offers.
  • Automation rules – You can trigger emails based on actions (e.g., “after 3 days, email student with next lesson”).
  • Better analytics – You see conversion rates, revenue trends, and student progress more clearly than on Teachable or Thinkific.

Where it falls short:

  • Price – The cheapest plan is $149/month (annual) and includes 1 product with 3 pipelines. That’s steep if you’re just starting.
  • Learning curve – Because it does so much, it takes longer to set up. You’re not just building a course; you’re building a mini business.
  • Course builder is less flexible – It works, but it’s not as polished for just course content. Thinkific and Teachable are better for pure course creation.

Best for: Established creators who want to replace several tools and don’t mind paying for the convenience.

Pros and Cons

Teachable

Pros:

  • Lowest entry price for a paid plan with no transaction fees.
  • Very easy to set up a basic course.
  • Built-in affiliate system saves you a third-party tool.

Cons:

  • No native email marketing – you must connect an external tool.
  • Customizing the look of your school is clunky.
  • Community features are barely there.

Thinkific

Pros:

  • Free plan lets you test the platform properly.
  • Flexible pricing options (subscriptions, bundles, etc.).
  • Good native community features for student interaction.

Cons:

  • Video storage limits on lower plans can be a problem for video-heavy courses.
  • No built-in affiliates – you’ll need an extra tool or workaround.
  • Theme customization can be slow and frustrating.

Kajabi

Pros:

  • Truly all-in-one – you can run your entire business from one dashboard.
  • Powerful email and automation tools.
  • Great for high-ticket offers and sales funnels.

Cons:

  • Expensive, especially for beginners.
  • Steep learning curve – expect to spend a weekend setting up.
  • Course builder feels like an afterthought compared to dedicated platforms.

Final Verdict: Which One Should You Pick?

If you’re a solo creator just starting out and want to get a course live without headaches, Teachable is the best bet. The $39/month plan gives you everything you need, and you can add email marketing later. It’s not the most powerful, but it works.

If you want more control over pricing and a built-in community, go with Thinkific. It’s especially good if you plan to sell multiple courses or memberships. The free plan is a great way to test before you commit.

If you’re already making $5,000+ per month and want to consolidate your tech stack, Kajabi makes sense. But don’t start there – it’s overkill unless you’re scaling.

My pick for most small business creators: Thinkific. It hits the sweet spot between power and price, and the free plan lets you validate your idea without spending a dime.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I migrate from one platform to another?

Yes, but it’s work. You can export course content from all three, but student data, certificates, and custom settings may not transfer cleanly. Plan for a weekend of manual work.

Which platform has the best student experience?

Teachable and Thinkific are roughly equal for student experience. Kajabi’s course player is fine, but it feels less polished for pure learning.

Do I need a separate email marketing tool?

With Teachable and Thinkific, yes. With Kajabi, no – but you pay for it. If you already use Mailchimp or ConvertKit, Teachable or Thinkific integrate easily.

Which platform handles memberships best?

Thinkific has the most flexible membership options (drip content, subscriptions, bundles). Kajabi also does memberships, but it’s more expensive. Teachable is weakest here.

Is there a free plan that actually works?

Thinkific’s free plan is the only one that lets you sell courses (with a transaction fee). Kajabi and Teachable only offer trials. Thinkific’s free plan is a solid way to start.

Can I sell coaching or one-on-one services?

Kajabi has built-in scheduling and pipeline tools for coaching. Thinkific and Teachable require third-party calendar tools like Calendly.

Bottom Line

There’s no “best” platform – only the one that fits your current stage. If you’re just testing an idea, start with Thinkific’s free plan. If you want to launch fast, Teachable. If you’re ready to scale and have the budget, Kajabi. Pick one, build your course, and don’t overthink it. The tool matters less than the teaching.
