Norton VPN vs NordVPN: Which VPN Is Worth Your Money in 2026?

Most VPN comparisons end the same way: NordVPN wins, everyone nods, and you move on. That's boring, and it's not always right.

Here's the thing — Norton VPN isn't really competing with NordVPN on the same terms. It comes bundled inside Norton 360, which millions of small business owners already pay for. If you're already running Norton antivirus on a few work laptops, adding a VPN for "free" sounds a lot better than paying for a second subscription.

So the real question isn't "which VPN is more powerful?" NordVPN wins that on paper. The real question is whether NordVPN's extra power is worth paying for separately — or whether Norton's built-in option is good enough for the way you actually work.

I've spent time with both. Let's go through it properly.

Short answer up front: NordVPN is the better VPN for anyone who cares about speed, streaming, torrenting, or independent privacy audits. Norton VPN is the better value only if you're already a Norton 360 subscriber. If you're buying a VPN from scratch, NordVPN wins.

Server Network: Not Even Close

NordVPN runs roughly 7,000+ servers across 118 countries, with a heavy concentration in North America and Europe and solid coverage in Asia, South America, and Africa. That matters more than it sounds — the more servers in a city, the less crowded each one is, and the faster your connection stays at peak hours.

Norton VPN operates a much smaller network — servers in roughly 30 countries. Norton doesn't publish exact server counts the way NordVPN does, which tells you something in itself.

Why this matters for a small business:

  • If you travel or work with clients overseas, NordVPN gives you a local server almost anywhere. Norton will leave you connecting to a neighboring country, which adds latency.
  • More servers means better odds of finding an uncongested one during busy periods.
  • NordVPN also offers specialty servers: Double VPN (traffic routed through two servers), Onion Over VPN, obfuscated servers for restrictive networks, and P2P-optimized servers in 40+ countries. Norton has none of these.

Norton's counterargument is simplicity. You open the app, hit connect, and it picks something reasonable. For someone who just wants protection on hotel Wi-Fi, the smaller network genuinely doesn't matter.

Winner: NordVPN, by a wide margin.

Speed: NordVPN Is Noticeably Faster

Speed is where the gap gets uncomfortable for Norton.

In testing, NordVPN's NordLynx protocol (built on WireGuard) consistently retains around 85–90% of your baseline connection speed on nearby servers. Even on long-distance connections, it holds up better than most competitors. NordVPN has been near the top of independent speed tests for years, and 2026 is no different.

Norton VPN has improved since it added WireGuard support on Windows, Mac, Android, and iOS, but it still lands in the middle of the pack — typically keeping 60–75% of baseline speed on nearby servers, with bigger drops over distance.

What does that mean in practice?

  • Browsing and email: You won't notice much difference.
  • Video calls: NordVPN is safer. Zoom and Teams calls over Norton's VPN occasionally stutter.
  • Large file transfers or cloud backups: The gap is real. A 5 GB upload that takes 12 minutes on NordVPN might take 20+ on Norton.

If your work involves moving files around, NordVPN pays for itself in saved time.

Winner: NordVPN.

Privacy and Security: The Audit Question

This is the section most reviews gloss over, and it's the one that should matter most.

NordVPN:

  • Independently audited no-logs policy, verified repeatedly by Deloitte (most recently in a 2024 assessment).
  • RAM-only servers — nothing is written to disk, so there's nothing to seize.
  • Panama-based, outside the Five Eyes, Nine Eyes, and Fourteen Eyes intelligence alliances.
  • Kill switch on every platform, plus DNS leak protection and a built-in malware/ad blocker called Threat Protection Pro.
  • Post-quantum encryption on the NordLynx protocol, which future-proofs against "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks.

Norton VPN:

  • States it doesn't log browsing activity. Norton's privacy policy says it doesn't track or store the sites you visit.
  • No public third-party audit of its no-logs claims. That's not an accusation — plenty of providers skip audits — but it means you're taking Norton's word for it.
  • Owned by Gen Digital, a US-headquartered company (Arizona) that also owns Avast, AVG, LifeLock, and CCleaner. Being a US company means it operates under US legal jurisdiction, which is less ideal for privacy purists than Panama.
  • Kill switch support has historically been inconsistent across platforms. It's there on Windows; mobile support has lagged.
  • No split tunneling at all, on any platform. NordVPN offers it on Windows, Android, and macOS.
