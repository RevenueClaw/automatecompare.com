Bluehost vs InMotion Hosting: Which Hosting or Domain Deal Wins in 2026?

I've set up sites on both of these hosts. More than once, I've talked a friend out of Bluehost's $2.95 banner and into InMotion instead — and other times I've done the opposite, because InMotion's cheapest plan was overkill for what they actually needed.

That's the honest shape of this comparison: it isn't a blowout. Bluehost and InMotion Hosting sit at similar price points, both throw in a free domain for the first year, and both will happily auto-renew you at two to four times the promotional rate. The difference is in what happens after month twelve, when the honeymoon pricing expires and you're living with the server you actually bought.

So let's look at the plans, the renewal math, the domain deals, and the parts that only show up once your site is live.

The quick answer, before the details

  • Pick Bluehost if you want the lowest possible first-year outlay, you're building one simple site (portfolio, brochure, a first blog), and you're comfortable moving hosts later if it outgrows you.
  • Pick InMotion Hosting if this site is attached to a business that earns money, you want a US-based support team that answers without reading a script, and you'd rather not redo the migration in eighteen months.

Both offer free domains. Both offer free SSL. InMotion gives you 90 days to change your mind; Bluehost gives you 30.

What you're actually buying in 2026

Bluehost is one of the largest hosts in the world — millions of domains, owned by Newfold Digital, and still the name WordPress.org puts on its hosting page. That recommendation is a business arrangement, not a performance benchmark, and you should treat it that way. What you get with Bluehost is a very polished onboarding flow, a custom dashboard, and a price that's hard to argue with for year one.

InMotion Hosting is smaller and older — running since 2001, with its own data centers on the US East and West coasts rather than reselling someone else's racks. It's the host that IT people recommend to their cousins. The tradeoff is that InMotion generally costs a few dollars more per month and its marketing is nowhere near as slick.

If you're hunting for the best web hosting 2026 has to offer on a small-business budget, those two profiles matter more than any spec sheet.

Pricing: the numbers that actually matter

Here's where I need to be blunt. Neither of these prices is the price you'll pay in year two. The headline rates require a 36-month prepay, and renewals jump hard.

Two things jump out.

First, the entry prices are nearly identical. Bluehost is about a dollar a month cheaper at the bottom; InMotion's comparable tier (Power vs. Choice Plus) is within a couple of dollars. If you're choosing purely on year-one cost, you're comparing a sandwich to a sandwich.

Second, Bluehost's Basic plan is a trap for anyone with a real business. Ten gigabytes of storage and one website sounds fine until you add an email list, a few product images, and a staging copy. Storage on shared hosting doesn't scale gracefully — it just runs out. The Choice Plus tier is the realistic floor for a business, which puts Bluehost at roughly $5.45/mo promo and $17.99/mo renewal.

InMotion's Launch plan has more headroom out of the box, and their shared plans now run on NVMe storage, which reads and writes noticeably faster than the SATA SSDs still sitting under a lot of budget hosting.
