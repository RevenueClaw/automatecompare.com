Black Friday VPN Bundles for Remote Teams: An Honest Checklist for 2026

Most Black Friday VPN articles are written for one person protecting one laptop. That's not you. You've got four contractors in three time zones, a shared Drive folder with client contracts in it, and someone who keeps working from a coffee shop with "FreeWiFi_Guest" as the network name.

So the usual advice — "grab the $1.99/month deal, it's basically free" — falls apart fast. Consumer VPN deals are priced per person and usually forbid commercial use in the terms. Business VPNs are priced per seat and often don't discount much. Somewhere in the middle is a messy gray zone where a small team can save real money or waste a few hundred dollars on licenses nobody activates.

I've spent the last few weeks re-testing the options that actually come up for small teams, and this is the checklist I'd use before clicking buy on any of them. Prices below are what I'd expect to see during the November 2026 sale window based on prior years — treat them as estimates, not quotes, because these companies change tiers more often than they change their logos.

First: what "VPN bundle" actually means in 2026

The phrase gets used for four different things, and mixing them up is how people end up with the wrong product.

  • A business VPN with per-seat licensing (NordLayer, Proton VPN for Business, Check Point Harmony SASE). You get an admin console, user provisioning, and usually SSO.
  • A consumer VPN sold in a multi-year bundle (Surfshark, NordVPN, Norton VPN). Cheap per month, unlimited or 5–10 devices, no real admin tooling.
  • A zero-trust access tool with a generous free tier (Tailscale, Twingate, Cloudflare Zero Trust). Not a VPN in the traditional sense, but it solves the same problem for a distributed team.
  • A security suite that throws in a VPN (Norton 360, Bitdefender Premium). The VPN is a feature, not the product.

All four can be the right answer. The mistake is buying category 2 and expecting category 1.

The honest checklist

Run every deal you're considering through these ten questions. If a deal fails three or more, walk away — the discount isn't worth the migration pain in February.

1. Is commercial use actually permitted?

Read the terms, not the marketing page. Several budget consumer VPNs restrict use to personal, non-commercial purposes. In practice nobody's auditing your five-person design studio, but if you ever need support for a business connectivity issue, you'd rather not be told your use case is out of scope. Business tiers exist partly so you have a legitimate footing.

2. How many seats, and can you buy them one at a time?

A "5-device" bundle is not a 5-person plan. If your team grows by one person in March, can you add a single seat, or do you jump to the next tier and pay for eight? NordLayer and Proton both let you add seats individually. Norton's consumer bundles do not — you buy the device count you buy.

3. Does it have an admin console you'd actually use?

Minimum viable: invite a user, revoke a user, see who's connected. If offboarding a contractor means texting them your account password and hoping they uninstall, that's not a team product. This single feature is the biggest gap between consumer bundles and business ones.

4. SSO and directory sync — do you need them?

If you're five people on Google Workspace, probably not urgently. If you're fifteen people and hiring, yes, eventually. Some business VPNs charge extra for SAML SSO. Check whether it's in the tier you're buying or the one above.

5. What's the renewal price?

This is the one that bites. Norton Black Friday discounts are frequently 50–60% off the first year, and the renewal lands at full list price — often two to three times what you paid. Set a calendar reminder for the renewal date the day you buy. The same pattern shows up on consumer multi-year VPN plans: the 2-year price is great, the auto-renewal after that is not.

6. Device and OS coverage

Count the devices your team actually uses: laptops, phones, sometimes a home desktop. A 5-device bundle sounds generous until you realize one person alone uses three. NordLayer and Surfshark both let you cover multiple devices per user; Norton's tiers are device-counted.

7. Kill switch, DNS leak protection, and split tunneling

Non-negotiable on the first two. Split tunneling matters more than people expect — if your team needs to reach a local printer or a client's on-prem system while the VPN is up, you'll want it. It's standard on business tiers and hit-or-miss on consumer ones.

8. Logging and jurisdiction

Every provider claims a no-logs policy. The useful question is: which jurisdiction, and has a third party audited the claim? NordLayer (Panama), Proton (Switzerland), Mullvad (Sweden) all publish audits. Cloudflare and Tailscale take a different approach — they're access brokers, so there's less traffic to log in the first place.

9. Bandwidth and connection limits

Consumer plans sometimes throttle or cap concurrent connections. If your team runs video calls all day, a 10 Mbps cap will be noticeable and nobody will tell you it's the VPN. Business tiers are generally uncapped.

10. Refund window and cancellation

30 days is the norm. Some are 14. A few Make you email support. Know this before you buy, because the honest truth is that the best way to evaluate a VPN for a team is to put three people on it for two weeks and see what breaks.

The shortlist, with what each one is actually for

NordLayer — the default pick for a small team that needs a real admin console

NordLayer is the business arm of NordVPN, and it's the option I keep coming back to for teams of 3–25. You get a proper admin panel, per-user billing, SAML SSO on the higher tiers, and a gateway feature for reaching an office network. The client apps are unremarkable in a good way — they connect and stay connected.

Black Friday pricing on NordLayer has historically landed around 30–40% off the annual rate, which puts the entry tier somewhere near $5–6 per user per month for the first year. That's a real discount, not a rounding error.

Pros: Per-seat billing, real admin console, SSO available, works on the OSes your team uses, decent audit trail.

Cons: The cheap tier is thin — dedicated IP and some gateway features sit behind higher plans. Support is ticket-based and can be slow. The discount applies to year one; budget for list price after.

Proton VPN for Business — for teams that care about the privacy posture

Swiss jurisdiction, published audits, and a business tier that adds user management on top of the consumer product. Pricing runs around $8–9 per user per month annually, and Proton has been discounting business plans during Black Friday in recent years.

Pros: Strong privacy record, good speeds, straightforward per-user pricing, includes Proton's other tools on the suite plan.

Cons: Admin tooling is less mature than NordLayer's. Fewer networking features — no real site-to-site story. If you need to connect a small office, this isn't it.

Check Proton VPN for Business ->

Tailscale — the one most remote teams should probably try first

Tailscale isn't a VPN in the "route all my traffic through a server in Amsterdam" sense. It's a mesh network that connects your team's devices directly to each other and to your resources. For a remote team whose actual need is "let everyone reach the staging server and the file share," it's a better fit than a traditional VPN, and it's dramatically easier to set up.

Free for up to 3 users and 100 devices. Personal Plus runs about $5/month, and the Business tier lands around $6 per user per month. Black Friday discounts are modest — Tailscale doesn't discount much because the free tier does the selling.

Pros: Free tier covers a surprising number of small teams, no traffic bottleneck, excellent for accessing internal tools, setup takes minutes.

Cons: It doesn't hide your IP or encrypt your traffic to the open internet — it's not a privacy VPN for coffee shop WiFi. Different product, different job. Some people buy it expecting the other thing and are disappointed.

Norton 360 bundles — the budget option, with a caveat you need to read

Norton Black Friday deals are the loudest on the internet, and the value is genuinely there if you want a security suite rather than a VPN. Norton 360 Deluxe typically drops to around $20–30 for the first year covering 5 devices, and the bundled VPN has unlimited data on most current tiers.

Here's the caveat: it's a device-count product, not a user product. There's no admin console for your team. And the renewal price is the whole story — that $25 first year routinely renews at $100+.

Pros: Cheapest way to cover a handful of devices with antivirus plus VPN, unlimited VPN data, genuinely good malware protection.

Cons: No team management, no SSO, renewal pricing is steep, and the VPN is the weakest part of the suite compared to a dedicated provider.

Surfshark — best per-dollar if you just need encrypted connections

Surfshark's multi-year plans go as low as roughly $2/month during Black Friday, with unlimited device connections on a single account. For a tiny team that just wants everyone's traffic encrypted and doesn't need provisioning, it's hard to beat on price.

Pros: Unlimited devices, cheap, decent speeds, includes a kill switch.

Cons: One account shared across a team is a terms-of-service gray area and gives you zero offboarding control. No admin console worth the name.

Cloudflare Zero Trust — free for up to 50 users

Worth mentioning because it's free at your scale and it's a legitimate product, not a trial. You install the WARP client, define access policies, and your team reaches internal resources without exposing them to the internet.

Pros: Free tier covers most small teams, real access policies, integrates with Google/Microsoft identity.

Cons: Configuration is genuinely more involved than Tailscale, and it's not a consumer privacy VPN. Support on the free tier is community-only.

What I'd skip this Black Friday

Lifetime deals. Any VPN advertising a one-time lifetime payment is either overselling capacity or planning to change the terms. I've watched three of these fold or quietly throttle in the last five years.

Multi-year prepay on a team plan. A 3-year business VPN contract locks in today's feature set and today's seat count. Teams change too fast for that to be a good bet.

Buying the top tier for features you won't use in year one. Dedicated IPs and advanced gateway configs are worth paying for when you need them, not before. Start on the entry tier and upgrade — most providers prorate.

Consumer plans for a team of eight or more. The moment you need to remove someone's access on their last day, you'll regret it.

Verdict

For most remote teams of 3–25 people: NordLayer, bought on the Black Friday annual discount.
