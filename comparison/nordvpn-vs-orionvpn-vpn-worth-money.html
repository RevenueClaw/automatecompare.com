NordVPN vs OrionVPN: Which VPN Is Worth Your Money in 2026?

NordVPN and OrionVPN don't compete for the same customer. One is a global brand with millions of users, four rounds of independent audits, and a marketing budget bigger than most VPN companies' entire revenue. The other is a smaller, budget-first provider that shows up in "cheap VPN" searches and quietly does an okay job for basic use.

That mismatch makes this comparison more interesting than a spec sheet suggests. If you're paying $3–5 a month either way, the question isn't "which has more servers." It's "which one am I comfortable trusting with my traffic, and which one actually works when I need it?"

I've spent time with both apps, tested speeds and streaming access, and read through the privacy documentation each company publishes. Here's what I found — including the parts where OrionVPN's transparency falls short and the parts where NordVPN charges more than it should.

The 30-second version

NordVPN wins for most people. It has a verifiable no-logs policy, a much larger network, faster and more consistent speeds, real streaming support, and 24/7 live chat. You pay for that reliability.

OrionVPN only makes sense on price. If your VPN use is light — occasional public Wi-Fi, a bit of geo-shifting, nothing sensitive — the savings are real. But you're giving up audits, support quality, and network size to get them.

What each service actually is

NordVPN

NordVPN launched in 2012 and is registered in Panama, which sits outside the Five Eyes, Nine Eyes, and Fourteen Eyes intelligence-sharing alliances. The company runs a network of roughly 7,400 servers across 118 countries, all of them diskless RAM-only machines — meaning a server can be physically seized and rebooted with nothing recoverable on it.

It's a mainstream, high-volume product. That has downsides (aggressive upsells, renewal prices that jump) and upsides (constant development, published audits, a support team that answers at 3 a.m.).

OrionVPN

OrionVPN is a much smaller operation. It offers apps for Windows, macOS, Android, and iOS plus a browser extension, supports the usual protocols, and prices aggressively on long-term plans. It markets itself on simplicity and cost rather than on audits or infrastructure.

Here's the honest part: OrionVPN does not publish a third-party no-logs audit, and its corporate ownership and jurisdiction aren't clearly disclosed on its site. That doesn't automatically mean it's lying about its logging practices. It means you can't verify the claim the way you can with NordVPN. For a lot of buyers that's fine. For anyone handling sensitive work, it's a real gap.

Feature breakdown

Encryption and protocols

Both use AES-256 encryption, which is the industry standard and not really a differentiator. The difference is in protocol implementation.

NordVPN runs NordLynx, its WireGuard-based protocol, which is consistently one of the fastest in independent speed tests. It also offers OpenVPN, IKEv2/IPsec, and NordWhisper, a newer protocol built for networks that actively block VPN traffic — hotel Wi-Fi, some corporate networks, and regions with heavy filtering. That last one is genuinely useful and not something most competitors have.

OrionVPN supports WireGuard, OpenVPN, and IKEv2. WireGuard is available on all major platforms, and speeds are respectable on nearby servers. There's no equivalent to NordWhisper, so on restrictive networks you may find yourself unable to connect at all.

More servers matters less than people think for everyday browsing — but it matters a lot for two things: finding a fast server during peak hours, and getting around geo-blocks that specifically target known VPN IP ranges. NordVPN's larger, more frequently rotated pool handles both better.

Speed

I tested both on a 500 Mbps fiber connection, using servers in the US, UK, and Germany.

NordVPN on NordLynx typically landed between 380 and 440 Mbps on nearby servers — roughly 80% of my baseline. Long-distance servers (US to Singapore) dropped to around 180–220 Mbps, which is still plenty for 4K streaming.

OrionVPN on WireGuard managed around 250–320 Mbps on nearby servers. That's usable, but noticeably slower, and the variance was higher — some servers were fast, others weren't. On distant servers I saw dips below 100 Mbps more than once.

Neither service will bottleneck a normal household. But if you're moving large files or doing video calls all day, NordVPN's consistency is worth paying for.

Streaming and geo-unblocking

NordVPN unblocked Netflix US, UK, and Japan, BBC iPlayer, Disney+, and Amazon Prime Video in my tests, with no fiddling required. It's one of the more reliable VPNs for streaming, and it maintains dedicated server categories for a handful of popular services.

OrionVPN is hit or miss. Netflix US worked on some servers and showed the proxy error on others. BBC iPlayer blocked me outright. Disney+ worked. If streaming is your main reason for buying a VPN, this alone probably decides it.

Privacy, logging, and audits

NordVPN's no-logs policy has been audited by Deloitte multiple times, most recently covering its 2024 infrastructure, with additional assessments from other security firms on its apps and servers. It also operates a bug bounty program and publishes transparency reports.

One thing worth knowing: NordVPN had a server breach in 2018 that was disclosed in 2019. It was a compromised server in Finland, not a logging failure, and the company tightened its infrastructure significantly afterward — including moving to RAM-only servers. But it happened, and a fair review mentions it.

OrionVPN states that it doesn't log browsing activity. There's no audit backing that up, no transparency report, and no clear statement about which legal jurisdiction would handle a data request. For casual use, that's a calculated risk. For journalists, activists, or anyone in a regulated industry, it's a disqualifier.

Device limits and apps

NordVPN allows 10 simultaneous connections on a single account, with apps for Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS, Android TV, Fire TV, and browser extensions for Chrome and Firefox. You can also install it directly on some routers.

OrionVPN's limit depends on the plan — typically 5 devices, with higher tiers offering more. Apps cover the main platforms, and setup is straightforward. The apps are functional but basic: fewer settings, less granular control, and no split tunneling on iOS or macOS.

Extra features

NordVPN bundles a lot beyond the tunnel:

  • Threat Protection Pro — blocks malware, trackers, and intrusive ads, and works even when the VPN is disconnected
  • Dark Web Monitor — alerts you when your credentials show up in a breach
  • Meshnet — lets you link devices into a private network, useful for remote access to a home machine
  • Password manager and 1 TB encrypted cloud storage on higher tiers
  • Post-quantum encryption added across the network in 2025, which matters for anyone thinking about long-term data interception

OrionVPN offers a basic ad and tracker blocker. That's about it. There's nothing wrong with that — some people want a VPN, not a suite — but the value comparison shifts once you factor in what NordVPN's higher tiers include.

Support

NordVPN has 24/7 live chat with reasonably fast response times, plus a large knowledge base. I got a human in under two minutes.

OrionVPN handles support through email and ticket submission. Response times in my experience ran from several hours to about a day. For a connection problem you're trying to fix before a meeting, that's painful.
