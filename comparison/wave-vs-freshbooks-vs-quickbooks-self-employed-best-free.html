Wave vs FreshBooks vs QuickBooks Self-Employed: Which Free Accounting Tool Actually Works?

You're a freelancer. You send invoices, track a few expenses, and dread tax season. You've probably Googled "free accounting software" and gotten a wall of options that all look the same.

Here's the honest truth: Wave, FreshBooks, and QuickBooks Self-Employed are three very different tools that happen to share the "accounting for small business" label. One of them is genuinely free. One is free-ish but has hidden costs. And one is a paid tool that keeps trying to convince you it's not.

I've tested all three with real client workflows — not just clicked around the demo. Let me save you the trial-and-error.

Now let's dig into the details.

Wave: The Only Truly Free Option

Wave has been around since 2009 and it's still the only accounting platform that gives you full double-entry bookkeeping, unlimited invoicing, and unlimited expense tracking at $0. No trial period. No "premium features" paywall for basic stuff. Just free.

What Wave Does Really Well

Invoicing that actually looks professional. You can customize invoices with your logo, brand colors, and payment terms. Clients can pay via credit card, bank transfer, or Apple Pay. The invoice templates don't look like they were designed in 2005 — which is more than I can say for some paid tools.

Real accounting, not just income tracking. Wave gives you a proper chart of accounts, balance sheet, and P&L statement. If you ever need to hand your books to an accountant, they won't laugh at you. That's a big deal for freelancers who plan to grow.

Unlimited everything on the free plan. Unlimited invoices, unlimited expense entries, unlimited bank connections. Most "free" tools cap these. Wave doesn't.

Bank feeds that actually work. Connecting your bank account is simple, and Wave categorizes transactions reasonably well. It's not perfect — you'll still need to review and fix some categorizations — but it's far better than manually entering everything.

Where Wave Falls Short

The mobile app is basic. You can create invoices and snap receipts, but you can't do full bookkeeping from your phone. And there's no automatic mileage tracking — you'll need to log trips manually or use a separate app like MileIQ.

Customer support is slow. Free plan users get email support only, and response times can stretch to a few days. If you have a critical issue during tax season, you might be waiting.

Payments processing is where they get you. Wave takes a cut on payment processing: 2.9% + 30¢ for credit cards, 1% for bank transfers. That's standard, but if you invoice a lot and clients pay by card, those fees add up. The accounting software itself is free — the payment processing is not.

No payroll on the free plan. Wave Payroll is a separate paid service (starting around $20/month plus per-employee fees). For a solo freelancer, that's fine. If you have employees, budget for it.

Who Should Use Wave

  • Solo freelancers who want a genuine $0 solution
  • People who don't need automatic mileage tracking
  • Freelancers who want proper financial reports, not just income/expense tracking
  • Anyone who's tired of "free" tools that suddenly start charging for basic features

FreshBooks: The Polish That Costs You

FreshBooks is the tool I recommend to freelancers who hate doing their own books. It's not free — the Lite plan is $11/month (billed annually) after a 30-day trial. But it's the most pleasant accounting experience I've used.

What FreshBooks Does Really Well

Invoicing that feels like a superpower. FreshBooks has the best invoice builder of the three. You can add line items, set up recurring invoices, send payment reminders automatically, and let clients pay with a single click. The invoice templates are modern and customizable. Clients actually comment on how nice my invoices look — that's never happened with Wave.

Project tracking that makes sense. If you work with clients on a project basis, FreshBooks lets you track time, expenses, and income against each project. You can see at a glance whether a project is profitable. That's genuinely useful, not just a feature checkbox.

The mobile app is excellent. You can create invoices, track expenses, and log time from your phone. The app is fast and doesn't feel like a stripped-down web version. It's the best mobile experience of the three by a wide margin.

Automatic mileage tracking. FreshBooks uses your phone's GPS to track trips automatically. You can classify them as business or personal with a swipe. It's not perfect — sometimes it logs a trip to the grocery store that you have to manually reclassify — but it's way better than manual logging.

Client portal that reduces "where's my invoice?" emails. Clients get a portal where they can view invoices, pay, and download receipts. This alone saves me several emails per month.

Where FreshBooks Falls Short

It's not free. The trial is 30 days, then you're paying. The Lite plan ($11/month) only allows 5 clients. If you have more than that, you need the Plus plan at $17/month. That's not outrageous, but it's not the "free accounting software" you might have been hoping for.

The reporting is limited on lower tiers. You get basic P&L and expense reports, but the more useful reports (like profit by client) are locked behind higher tiers. For a freelancer just starting out, that's frustrating.

No true double-entry accounting. FreshBooks is designed for simplicity, which means you don't get a full chart of accounts or balance sheet. If you need proper accounting reports, you'll want to export to an accountant anyway. For most freelancers, that's fine. For people who want control over their books, it's limiting.

Customer support is hit-or-miss. The chat support is decent, but phone support is only available on higher plans. And some of their help articles feel like they're trying to upsell you rather than solve your problem.

Who Should Use FreshBooks

  • Freelancers who invoice a lot and want the process to be painless
  • People who want automatic mileage tracking without fiddling with separate apps
  • Those who don't mind paying $11–$17/month for a better experience
  • Freelancers who want to track project profitability

QuickBooks Self-Employed: The Mileage King, But Clunky

QuickBooks Self-Employed is Intuit's answer to freelancers who don't need full QuickBooks but want tax help. It's not free — it's $7.50/month (billed annually) after a trial. But it has one feature that the others can't match.

What QuickBooks Self-Employed Does Really Well

The best automatic mileage tracking in the business. This is the main reason to choose QuickBooks Self-Employed. The app uses your phone's GPS to track every trip automatically, then lets you classify them with a swipe. It's genuinely impressive. If you drive for work — rideshare, deliveries, client visits — this alone is worth the subscription because you'll capture deductions you'd otherwise miss.

Tax estimates that are actually useful. QuickBooks calculates your estimated quarterly taxes based on your income and expenses. It's not perfect, but it gives you a solid ballpark. I've had freelancers use this to avoid underpayment penalties — that's real value.

Integration with TurboTax. If you already use TurboTax for your personal taxes, having your business income and expenses already categorized makes filing way easier. The data flows directly into your return.

Simple, focused design. QuickBooks Self-Employed is intentionally minimal. It tracks income, expenses, mileage, and tax estimates. That's it. There's no inventory, no project management, no client portal. For a solo freelancer, that's actually a feature — less clutter.

Where QuickBooks Self-Employed Falls Short

The free plan is a trap. The "free" version is basically a 30-day trial. After that, you're paying $7.50/month (billed annually) or $15/month month-to-month. If you want the full QuickBooks Self-Employed experience, that's the price.

The accounting is shallow. QuickBooks Self-Employed is designed for freelancers who just want to track income and expenses. It doesn't give you a proper chart of accounts or balance sheet. You get a P&L and a tax summary, and that's about it. If you ever need to dig into your books, you'll feel limited.

The interface is clunky. I don't know how Intuit manages to Make a modern product feel so dated, but they do. The dashboard is information-dense without being useful. The mobile app is fine for mileage tracking but frustrating for anything else.

Mileage tracking is the star, but it has quirks. The automatic mileage tracking is genuinely good — it uses GPS and classifies trips with decent accuracy. But the app sometimes logs the same trip twice, and manually correcting entries is clunky.

No double-entry accounting. QuickBooks Self-Employed is designed for Schedule C filers. It tracks income and expenses but doesn't give you a proper balance sheet. If you ever need to show your books to an accountant, they'll have to recreate the full picture.

Who Should Use FreshBooks

  • Freelancers who bill clients regularly and want the nicest invoicing experience
  • People who hate doing bookkeeping and want the most automated option
  • Freelancers who need project-level profitability tracking
  • Anyone willing to pay $11–$17/month for time savings

QuickBooks Self-Employed: The Tax Machine

QuickBooks Self-Employed is Intuit's attempt at a freelancer-specific product. It's not the same as QuickBooks Online (that's a different, more expensive product). Self-Employed is stripped down and focused on one thing: tracking income and expenses for tax purposes.

What QuickBooks Self-Employed Does Really Well

The best automatic mileage tracking in the business. Intuit's mobile app uses GPS to track every trip automatically. You swipe right for business, left for personal. At tax time, it calculates your deduction based on the IRS standard mileage rate. If you drive a lot for work — real estate agents, delivery drivers, mobile services — this feature alone justifies the cost.

Tax estimates that are actually accurate. QuickBooks Self-Employed calculates your estimated quarterly taxes based on your income and expenses. It's not perfect (it doesn't account for every deduction), but it gives you a solid ballpark. This feature has saved several freelancers I know from nasty surprises in April.

Seamless integration with TurboTax. If you already use TurboTax, your income and expense data flows directly into your tax return. No manual data entry. That's a real time-saver.

The free trial is generous. You get 3 months free (not just 30 days), which is enough time to actually test it with real invoices.

Where QuickBooks Self-Employed Falls Short

The interface is clunky. QuickBooks Self-Employed feels like it was designed by accountants, for accountants. The navigation is confusing, and simple tasks like editing an expense take more clicks than they should. It's functional, but it's not pleasant.

Limited invoicing. You can create invoices, but the customization options are basic. You can't add custom fields or create the polished invoices you get with FreshBooks or even Wave.

The "free" plan is misleading. QuickBooks Self-Employed offers a free trial, but after 30 days, you're paying. The cheapest plan is $7.50/month (billed annually). That's cheaper than FreshBooks, but it's still not free.

Customer support is hit or miss. You can get phone support, but wait times can be long. And the help articles are often outdated or overly generic.

The app is clunky. The mobile app is great for mileage tracking (it's the best of the three), but everything else feels awkward. Creating an invoice on the app takes twice as many taps as FreshBooks. And the web interface feels dated compared to the other two.

What QuickBooks Self-Employed Does Really Well

The best automatic mileage tracking in the business. QuickBooks uses your phone's GPS to log trips automatically, and it's impressively accurate. You can swipe to classify trips as business or personal. If you drive for work — rideshare, delivery, client visits — this alone might justify the subscription.

Tax estimates that are actually useful. QuickBooks Self-Employed calculates your estimated quarterly taxes based on your income and expenses. That's a huge time-saver if you're bad at setting aside money for taxes. You can also pay estimated taxes directly through the app.

Seamless integration with TurboTax. If you already use TurboTax for your personal taxes, QuickBooks Self-Employed exports your data directly. No manual entry, no "I'll do it later" that turns into a weekend of spreadsheet misery.

Where QuickBooks Self-Employed Falls Short

It's not really free. The free version is a 30-day trial. After that, you're paying $7.50/month (billed annually) or $15/month (month-to-month). There's a cheaper "self-employed" plan than FreshBooks, but it's still not free.

The accounting features are shallow. QuickBooks Self-Employed is really an income/expense tracker, not a full bookkeeping system. You don't get a proper balance sheet or P&L statement. If your accountant wants real financial statements, you'll need to upgrade to QuickBooks Online (which is a completely different product at $30+/month).

The interface is clunky. I don't know how Intuit manages to Make accounting software feel this dated, but they do. The web app is slow, the navigation is confusing, and the mobile app is a mess of menus. It works, but it's not pleasant.

The free trial is aggressively short. You get 30 days, then it's $7.50/month for the Self-Employed plan. That's cheaper than FreshBooks, but you get less: no project tracking, no client portal, and the invoicing is basic.

What QuickBooks Self-Employed Does Really Well

Automatic mileage tracking is the standout feature. The app uses your phone's GPS to track every trip and classifies them automatically. You can review trips and mark them as business or personal. For freelancers who drive a lot, this alone is worth the subscription.

Tax estimates that are actually useful. The tool calculates your estimated quarterly taxes based on your income and expenses. It's not perfect, but it gives you a ballpark that's close enough to set money aside. This is a huge stress reliever if you're bad at saving for taxes.

Integration with TurboTax. If you already use TurboTax, your bookkeeping data flows directly into your tax return. That's a genuine time-saver, and it's the main reason people stick with QuickBooks Self-Employed even when the interface frustrates them.

Where QuickBooks Self-Employed Falls Short

The free plan is a tease. QuickBooks Self-Employed has a "free" tier, but it's basically a 30-day trial that auto-converts to a paid plan. There's no permanent free option. The cheapest plan is $7.50/month (billed annually), which is cheaper than FreshBooks but still not free.

The interface feels dated. QuickBooks Self-Employed has a clunky, confusing layout. The dashboard shows you a lot of numbers but doesn't explain what they mean. I've seen freelancers get genuinely lost trying to find basic features like "create an invoice."

Limited invoicing on the basic plan. The $7.50/month plan only lets you send a limited number of invoices (around 25 per year). For that price, you're really paying for tax estimates and mileage tracking, not invoicing. If you invoice more than twice a month, you'll need the Plus plan at $15/month.

The tax estimate feature is a double-edged sword. QuickBooks calculates your estimated quarterly taxes automatically. That's great — until it's wrong. The estimates are based on your income and expenses, but they don't account for your specific deductions or tax situation. I've seen freelancers underpay because they trusted the estimate blindly. It's a starting point, not a definitive answer.

The interface is clunky. QuickBooks Self-Employed feels like it was designed by accountants, for accountants. It works, but it's not pleasant. The web dashboard is functional but dated, and the mobile app (while good for mileage) is awkward for anything else.

Who Should Use FreshBooks

  • Freelancers who invoice clients regularly and want the process to be painless
  • Service-based businesses (consultants, designers, writers) who bill by project or hour
  • People who want automatic mileage tracking without extra apps
  • Anyone who's willing to pay $11–$17/month to save 2–3 hours per month on admin

QuickBooks Self-Employed: The Mileage King with a Catch

QuickBooks Self-Employed is Intuit's stripped-down version of QuickBooks, aimed at freelancers and gig workers. It's not the full QuickBooks — that's a separate product for small businesses with employees and inventory.
