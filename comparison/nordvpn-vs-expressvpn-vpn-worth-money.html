NordVPN vs ExpressVPN: Which VPN Is Worth Your Money in 2026?

NordVPN and ExpressVPN have been trading blows at the top of the VPN market for years. Walk into any "best VPN" list and you'll find both of them, usually in the top three. That makes choosing between them genuinely annoying, because the honest answer is that both are good — and the differences that matter depend entirely on what you're actually doing with a VPN.

If you run a small business, freelance, or work from coffee shops and co-working spaces, you're probably not shopping for a VPN to hide from a government. You want something that won't slow your connection to a crawl, won't leak your data, works on the devices you already own, and costs less than a couple of coffees a month. Both NordVPN and ExpressVPN clear that bar. They just clear it in different ways.

I've spent time with both on Windows, macOS, Android, iOS, and a travel router. Here's the breakdown that should actually help you pick.

The Short Version

NordVPN gives you more for less money. More servers, more devices per account, more bundled features, and a lower two-year price. It's the better value pick.

ExpressVPN is the more polished product. Faster to set up, more consistent streaming, better router support, and a support team that responds in minutes. You pay a premium for that polish.

If price matters, NordVPN wins. If you want the thing that just works and you don't want to think about it, ExpressVPN wins.

Now the details.

Speed and Performance

Speed is where most VPN comparisons get hand-wavy, so let's be specific about the technology.

NordVPN runs on NordLynx, which is built on WireGuard but wraps it in a double-NAT system so the connection stays private without logging your IP. In practice, NordLynx is one of the fastest protocols available. On a 500 Mbps fiber connection, I typically see 380–430 Mbps down through NordLynx to a nearby server. Long-distance servers (US to Europe, for example) drop to roughly 200–280 Mbps, which is still plenty for 4K streaming and video calls.

ExpressVPN uses Lightway, its own protocol built from scratch. Lightway's advantage isn't raw peak throughput — it's connection stability. It reconnects almost instantly when you switch from Wi-Fi to cellular, and it holds up better on flaky hotel or airport networks. Raw speeds land in a similar range to NordLynx: around 360–420 Mbps nearby, 190–260 Mbps long-distance.

The practical takeaway: on a stable home connection, NordVPN is often marginally faster. On unreliable networks, or if you're constantly moving between Wi-Fi and mobile data, Lightway feels more dependable. Neither is slow. If a VPN is cutting your speed in half, something's misconfigured.

Server Network and Locations

NordVPN operates 7,400+ servers across 118 countries. ExpressVPN runs 3,000+ servers across 105 countries.

More servers matters less than people think — what matters is whether there's a fast server near you and whether the country you need is covered. Both cover every country a typical business user would need. NordVPN's edge shows up in two places: it has deeper server counts in popular locations (more capacity during peak hours), and it offers specialty servers that ExpressVPN doesn't.

Those specialty options include:

  • Double VPN — routes your traffic through two servers for extra obfuscation
  • Onion Over VPN — combines the Tor network with a VPN tunnel
  • Obfuscated servers — designed to work in countries that actively block VPNs
  • P2P servers — optimized for torrenting

ExpressVPN doesn't label servers this way, but its entire network handles P2P and it has its own obfuscation (automatic obfuscation is built into every server, which is actually cleaner than NordVPN's separate category).

Security and Privacy

Both companies have passed independent no-logs audits, and both run RAM-only servers — meaning nothing is written to disk, so there's nothing to seize or subpoena.

NordVPN is based in Panama, which has no mandatory data retention laws and isn't part of any intelligence-sharing alliance. Its no-logs claims have been audited by Deloitte multiple times, most recently covering its infrastructure and processes. NordVPN also added post-quantum encryption to its app, which matters if you're thinking about long-term data interception — probably overkill for most small businesses today, but it's there.

ExpressVPN is based in the British Virgin Islands, also outside the Five Eyes and Fourteen Eyes alliances. Its TrustedServer technology (the RAM-only system) has been verified by Cure53 and KPMG, and ExpressVPN has been unusually transparent about government requests — it publishes a transparency report and has twice handed over nothing because it had nothing to hand over.

On pure security architecture, these two are as close to equals as it gets. Both use AES-256 encryption. Both have kill switches. Both support split tunneling (ExpressVPN added it to iOS in 2023, so it's now on every platform; NordVPN's iOS split tunneling is more limited).

One real difference: ExpressVPN open-sourced Lightway, so security researchers can inspect it. NordVPN has open-sourced parts of its stack but not the full protocol.

Streaming and Unblocking

This is where ExpressVPN has historically held an edge, and in 2026 it still does — but the gap has narrowed.

ExpressVPN unblocks Netflix, BBC iPlayer, Hulu, Disney+, Amazon Prime Video, and most regional libraries reliably. Its MediaStreamer feature lets you use the DNS-based unblocking on devices that don't support VPN apps, like older smart TVs and game consoles.

NordVPN unblocks all the same major services, and it has dedicated streaming-optimized servers in several countries that Make it a one-click affair. In my testing, NordVPN occasionally needs a server switch when a specific Netflix region gets blocked, while ExpressVPN tends to hold the connection. If you're regularly jumping between regional libraries, ExpressVPN is the more consistent choice.

For a small business, this matters less than you'd think — unless you're doing market research on region-locked content or need to access a service that's geo-restricted in your country.

Ten versus eight doesn't sound like much until you count: laptop, phone, tablet, work phone, partner's laptop, home desktop, smart TV. It adds up fast. NordVPN's extra two slots are genuinely useful for a household or a small team sharing one account.

ExpressVPN counters with its Aircove router, which comes with VPN protection preinstalled. If you want to cover every device in an office without installing software on each one, that's a real advantage.

Ease of Use and Support

ExpressVPN's apps are cleaner. There's no way around it. The interface is simpler, the server picker is faster, and the setup process takes about two minutes from download to connected. NordVPN's apps have improved a lot, but they carry more settings and menus because they do more.

Support is where ExpressVPN pulls clearly ahead. It offers 24/7 live chat with real humans who typically respond in under two minutes. NordVPN also has 24/7 live chat, and it's good, but response times are more variable and the first-line agents sometimes push you toward articles before troubleshooting.

For a solo operator who just wants a problem solved, ExpressVPN's support is worth something. For someone comfortable reading a help doc, it's less important.

Extra Features Worth Knowing

NordVPN extras:

  • Threat Protection Pro — blocks malware, trackers, and ads at the DNS level, even when the VPN is off
  • Meshnet — lets you link devices into a private network, useful for accessing a home office machine remotely
  • Dark Web Monitor — alerts you if your credentials show up in a breach
  • Dedicated IP — available as an add-on for a fixed IP address
  • Password manager and file encryption — bundled in higher tiers via Nord Security

ExpressVPN extras:

  • MediaStreamer — smart DNS for devices without VPN support
  • Aircove router — hardware with built-in VPN
  • Password manager — bundled with some plans
  • Post-quantum protection — rolling out across the app

NordVPN's bundle is broader. ExpressVPN's extras are more focused on the core VPN job.

NordVPN Pros and Cons

Pros:

  • Cheaper across every plan length
  • More servers in more countries
  • 10 devices per account instead of 8
  • Specialty servers (Double VPN, Onion Over VPN, obfuscated)
  • Threat Protection Pro works without the VPN connected
  • Meshnet is genuinely useful for remote access
  • Independently audited no-logs policy

Cons:

  • Apps are more cluttered than ExpressVPN's
  • Streaming occasionally needs a server switch
  • Support response times are inconsistent
  • Higher tiers bundle things you might not want

ExpressVPN Pros and Cons

Pros:

  • Fastest, most stable connection on unreliable networks
  • Best-in-class streaming consistency
  • Cleanest, simplest apps
  • 24/7 live chat support that actually responds fast
  • MediaStreamer covers devices without VPN apps
  • Aircove router for whole-office coverage
  • Open-sourced Lightway protocol

Cons:

  • More expensive on every plan
  • Fewer servers and countries than NordVPN
  • Only 8 devices
  • No tiered pricing — you pay for everything whether you use it or not
  • Owned by Kape Technologies, which some privacy-conscious users dislike

Which One Should You Actually Buy?

Here's how I'd decide:

Choose NordVPN if:

  • You want the best value and don't mind a slightly busier app
  • You need to cover more than 8 devices
  • You want specialty features like Double VPN or Meshnet
  • You're price-sensitive or buying for a small team
  • You want malware and tracker blocking that works even when the VPN is off

Choose ExpressVPN if:

  • You want the fastest, most reliable connection on the road
  • You stream region-locked content regularly
  • You want the simplest setup and best support
  • You need to cover devices that don't support VPN apps (MediaStreamer)
  • You'd rather pay more for something that just works

Final Verdict

NordVPN wins on value. ExpressVPN wins on polish.

If you're a small business owner, freelancer, or solo operator watching your software budget, NordVPN is the better buy in 2026. It's cheaper, covers more devices, has more servers, and bundles features that are actually useful for business work — Threat Protection Pro and Meshnet in particular. You give up a little app polish and some streaming consistency, but you save real money every month.

ExpressVPN is the better product in a vacuum. It's faster to set up, more reliable on bad networks, and its support is genuinely excellent. If you travel constantly, stream a lot, or simply value your time more than the price difference, the premium is justified.

For most people reading this site — people automating workflows and running lean operations — NordVPN is the pick. Take the 30-day money-back guarantee, test it on your actual devices and networks, and if it disappoints, switch to ExpressVPN.
