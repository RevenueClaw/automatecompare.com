QuickBooks Online has been the default choice for small business accounting for years. But default doesn't always mean best — especially in 2026, when competitors like Xero, FreshBooks, and Wave have stepped up their games.

I've used QuickBooks Online across three different businesses over the past six years. I've loved it, hated it, and eventually made peace with it. This review breaks down everything you need to know — the good, the bad, and the "why does this cost more than my Netflix subscription?"

Let's get into it.

What Is QuickBooks Online?

QuickBooks Online (QBO) is cloud-based accounting software from Intuit. It handles invoicing, expense tracking, bank reconciliation, payroll, inventory, and tax preparation. It's designed for small to mid-sized businesses — from solo freelancers to companies with dozens of employees.

Unlike QuickBooks Desktop (which is installed locally and talks to nobody), QBO syncs across devices, connects to your bank accounts, and integrates with hundreds of third-party apps.

Feature Breakdown: What Actually Works (and What Doesn't)

1. Bank Feeds & Reconciliation

The bank feed setup takes about 5 minutes. You connect your checking, credit card, and PayPal accounts, and transactions start flowing in automatically.

Works well:

  • Categorization rules are surprisingly smart after you train them. If you mark "Starbucks" as "Meals & Entertainment" three times, QBO remembers.
  • Reconciliation feels clean — match transactions, click "Reconcile," done.

Doesn't work well:

  • Sometimes duplicates appear when the bank sends transactions twice. You'll need to manually delete these.
  • Disconnecting and reconnecting a bank account can trigger a 48-hour wait (yes, really).

2. Invoicing & Payments

You can create professional invoices, add your logo, set payment terms, and email them directly. Clients can pay via credit card or ACH through QuickBooks Payments.

What I like:

  • Recurring invoices for subscription clients work flawlessly. I set them up once and forget about them.
  • Late payment reminders are automatic and customizable.

The catch:

  • QuickBooks Payments charges 2.9% + $0.25 per transaction for credit cards. That's standard but not cheap.
  • If you stop paying your QBO subscription, invoices become read-only. You can't send new ones until you pay up.

3. Expense Tracking & Receipt Capture

Snap a photo of a receipt with the mobile app, and QBO reads it — usually correctly. The Mileage Tracker feature uses your phone's GPS to log business drives automatically.

Works well:

  • Mileage tracking has saved me hours. It logs trips, calculates deductions, and exports to tax forms.
  • The receipt matching feature connects uploaded receipts to bank transactions automatically.

Doesn't work well:

  • The OCR (optical character recognition) misreads totals about 15% of the time, especially on handwritten or crumpled receipts. Always double-check.
  • You can't batch-delete mistakes easily. If you import 100 receipts and 10 are wrong, you're clicking each one.

4. Payroll (Built-In)

QuickBooks Payroll is an add-on ($45–$125/month). It handles payroll calculations, direct deposit, tax filings, and W-2s.

The good:

  • Syncing is automatic — payroll entries appear in your accounting without manual entry.
  • Same-day direct deposit is available.

The bad:

  • It forces you into a specific payroll schedule. If you pay irregularly (e.g., project-based), it's clunky.
  • Customer support for payroll issues is notoriously slow. Expect 1–2 hour waits on chat.

5. Inventory Management

If you sell physical products, QBO tracks inventory quantities and costs. It updates when you sell or purchase items.

Works well:

  • Simple setup for businesses with fewer than 500 SKUs.
  • Cost of goods sold (COGS) is calculated automatically.

Falls short:

  • No batch tracking or lot numbers. If you need those, you'll need an add-on like Fishbowl.
  • Inventory adjustments require manual journal entries. That's 2026, folks — it should be a button.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Market leader stability — It's unlikely to disappear tomorrow. Intuit has resources.
  • Huge app ecosystem — Anything with "QuickBooks integration" usually works.
  • Good mobile mileage tracking — Saves real hours if you drive for work.
  • Automatic tax calculations — Sales tax rates by location are built in.
  • Strong reporting — Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, and custom reports are reliable.

Cons

  • Price creep — Your bill will increase 10–15% every year. No loyalty discounts.
  • Customer support is mediocre — Phone hold times average 15–25 minutes. Chat is better but still slow.
  • Not great for complex inventory — Fewer than 500 SKUs? Fine. More than that? Painful.
  • Mobile app restrictions — You can't create estimates or run reports from the phone app (why?).
  • Learning curve — Not as beginner-friendly as FreshBooks. Expect a few frustrating afternoons.

Who Should Use QuickBooks Online?

You'll like QBO if:

  • You sell physical products and need inventory tracking.
  • You work with an accountant who uses QuickBooks (this alone is a reason to stay).
  • You need detailed financial reports for loans or investors.
  • You want payroll, accounting, and payments in one place (even if it costs more).

Skip QBO if:

  • You're a freelancer with under 10 clients a month. Try FreshBooks or Wave.
  • You operate outside the US. Xero handles multi-currency better.
  • You hate recurring price increases. Wave is free (but limited).
  • Your main need is simple invoicing. Don't pay $30/mo for that.

Final Verdict

Score: 8.2 / 10

QuickBooks Online is not the best accounting software for everyone — but it is the safest choice for most US-based small businesses that sell products or need solid reporting.

It wins on bank connections, integrations, and accountant compatibility. It loses on price, customer support, and simplicity.

If you're willing to pay for stability and don't mind the occasional clunky interface, it's a fine choice. But if you're a solo operator who just wants to send invoices and track expenses, you can get the same result for less money.

My recommendation: Start with Simple Start or Plus (depending on whether you need inventory) and test it for 30 days. If the interface frustrates you, try Xero or FreshBooks before committing.

FAQ

Can QuickBooks Online handle payroll?

Yes, but it's an add-on starting at $45/month. It handles tax filings and direct deposit automatically.

Is QuickBooks Online safe?

Yes. Intuit uses bank-level encryption (256-bit SSL) and two-factor authentication is available. Your data is backed up on their servers.

Can I switch from QuickBooks Desktop to Online?

Yes. Intuit offers a migration tool, but it's not perfect — some historical data (like PDF invoices) won't transfer. Plan for some cleanup.

How many users can I have?

Simple Start: 1 user. Essentials: Up to 3. Plus: Up to 5. Advanced: Up to 25. Each user costs extra if you go over the limit.

Does QuickBooks Online work internationally?

Yes, but it's optimized for the US. For multi-currency accounting, you need the Plus plan or higher. Xero handles this better.

What happens if I cancel my subscription?

Your data stays accessible (read-only) for 12 months. You can export reports, invoices, and transactions during that time. After that, it's gone unless you download it.

Is there a free trial?

Yes. QuickBooks Online offers a 30-day free trial for all plans. No credit card required.
