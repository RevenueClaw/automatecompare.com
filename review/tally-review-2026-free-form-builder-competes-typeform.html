Tally Review 2026: The Free Form Builder That Competes with Typeform

Most form builders lure you in with a free plan that runs out fast. Ten responses a month. Three forms. Logic rules locked behind a paywall. You build something, hit the ceiling in a week, and either pay up or start over somewhere else.

Tally took the opposite approach. Its free plan has no submission cap, no form limit, and no question limit. You can collect ten thousand responses on a form with forty questions and pay nothing. That's the whole pitch, and it's held up long enough that Tally has become one of the default recommendations in the small-business tool space.

But "free and unlimited" isn't the same as "good." So let's look at what Tally actually does well, where it gets frustrating, and whether it deserves a spot in your stack.

What Tally Is (and Who Built It)

Tally is a web-based form builder built by a small team based in Belgium. It launched in 2020 and grew largely through word of mouth — the founders built it in public, shared the roadmap, and kept the free tier unusually generous while competitors kept shrinking theirs.

The design philosophy is simple: forms should be as easy to write as a document. Instead of dragging widgets onto a canvas, you type. Hit / and a command menu appears. Type "question" for a new field, "page" for a page break, "logic" for a conditional rule, "payment" for a Stripe field. If you've used Notion, the muscle memory transfers immediately.

That's not a cosmetic difference. It changes how fast you can go from idea to published form. A five-question lead capture form takes a couple of minutes to build, not fifteen.

Feature Breakdown

Question Types

Tally covers the standard set and then some:

  • Short text, long text, email, phone, URL, number
  • Multiple choice, checkboxes, dropdown
  • Linear scale, rating, ranking
  • Matrix and multi-select grids
  • Date, time, signature
  • File upload
  • Payment (via Stripe)
  • Hidden fields and calculated fields

The matrix and ranking questions are the ones that usually push people into paid tools elsewhere. Having them on a free plan is unusual.

Logic Without a Paywall

Conditional logic is where most free form tools draw the line. Tally doesn't. You can show or hide questions based on earlier answers, jump respondents to different pages, and build branching paths — all on the free tier.

There's also a calculation block. You can add scores, multiply values, sum selections, and pipe the result into a later question or a thank-you page. That opens the door to quizzes, quote calculators, pricing estimators, and lead-scoring forms that would otherwise require a script or a paid tier.

Answer piping works too: reference a respondent's earlier answer inside a later question. "So, {Q1}, what's your biggest challenge with that?" Small touch, big lift in completion quality.

Partial Submissions

Long forms lose people. Tally records partial submissions, so you can see where respondents dropped off even if they never hit submit. You can also let people save and resume — useful for applications, onboarding questionnaires, and anything over about fifteen questions.

This is the feature that quietly matters most for lead generation. A form with a 40% completion rate and no visibility into the other 60% is a black box. Partial submissions turn it into a funnel you can actually diagnose.

Integrations

Tally ships native integrations with the tools small businesses actually use:

  • Notion, Airtable, Google Sheets
  • Slack, HubSpot, Pipedream
  • Mailchimp, ConvertKit, ActiveCampaign
  • Stripe, Calendly, Google Analytics
  • Webhooks and Zapier/Make for everything else

The native Notion and Airtable integrations are the standout. Responses land in a database with the right field types without an automation layer in between — no Zapier task consumption, no middleware to debug when a field renames itself.

Embedding and Sharing

You get a hosted page, inline embed, popup, slide-in, and full-page embed. There's a WordPress plugin, and the embed code is plain and small enough not to wreck your page speed.
