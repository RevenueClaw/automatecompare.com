Wave Accounting Review 2026: Actually Free with No Hidden Fees?

Every few months I get the same email from a reader: "I signed up for a free accounting tool and got charged $40 a month three weeks later. What did I do wrong?"

Usually? Nothing. They just picked a product that calls itself free when it means "free trial," or "free until you need to send more than five invoices," or "free if you never want to export your own data." The word has been stretched so thin it barely means anything anymore.

So when I say Wave's accounting software is free, I want to be precise about what I mean: the double-entry ledger, the unlimited invoicing, the expense tracking, the bank feeds, the basic financial reports — all of that costs $0 and has for years. No trial clock. No invoice cap. No "upgrade to export your data" hostage situation.

But "free" and "no hidden fees" are two different claims. Wave absolutely makes money off you, just not from the accounting module. Let me walk through where the free plan ends, where the paid stuff begins, and whether the whole arrangement makes sense for your business in 2026.

What Wave Actually Is (and Who Owns It Now)

Wave started in Toronto in 2010 as a straightforward bet: give small businesses real accounting software for nothing, then earn revenue on payment processing and payroll. H&R Block bought the company in 2019. In 2024, H&R Block sold it to Dye & Durham, a Canadian legal-and-business software company.

That ownership churn matters a little. It means Wave has changed hands twice in five years, which historically signals either new investment or quiet neglect. So far it looks more like the former — the product still ships updates, the payroll coverage has expanded, and pricing hasn't been gutted. But it's worth knowing that Wave is a small piece of a bigger parent company's portfolio, not an independent startup burning venture capital.

Who it's built for: freelancers, consultants, solo operators, and very small service businesses in the US and Canada. If you're a retail shop with 400 SKUs or a contractor tracking job costs across 30 active projects, Wave will frustrate you quickly. If you send 10 invoices a month and need to know roughly what you made and what you spent, it does the job.

The Free Plan, Feature by Feature

Invoicing and Estimates

Unlimited invoices. Unlimited customers. Unlimited estimates. That's the headline, and it's real — I've never hit a ceiling on the free plan and I've been poking at Wave on and off since 2018.

You can build an estimate, send it, and convert it to an invoice with one click when the client approves. Recurring invoices are supported, which is the single most useful automation for anyone on retainer. You get automatic payment reminders (with a schedule you control), invoice status tracking (sent, viewed, paid), and a customizable template with your logo and brand color. The customization is limited — you're adjusting colors and logo placement, not redesigning the layout — but it's enough that invoices don't look generic.

One thing I appreciate: you can accept partial payments and deposits against an invoice. For freelancers who take 50% upfront, that's not a nice-to-have, it's essential. QuickBooks handles this too, but plenty of cheaper tools don't.

Payment Processing (This Is Where Money Changes Hands)

Wave Payments lets customers pay invoices by credit card or ACH bank transfer directly from the invoice. The money lands in your Wave account and reconciles itself against the invoice — no manual matching.

The fees are the honest part of this review. As of early 2026, Wave charges roughly 2.9% + $0.60 per credit card transaction and 1% per ACH transfer (with a $1 minimum). Compare that to Stripe at 2.9% + $0.30 and you'll see Wave is more expensive on cards. On a $2,000 invoice, that extra 30 cents is trivial.
