Wave Accounting Review 2026: Actually Free with No Hidden Fees?

Walk into any small business Facebook group and ask about free accounting software. Someone will say Wave within about thirty seconds. But here’s the thing—free software usually means limited software, or worse, freemium traps that nickel-and-dime you once you’re hooked.

I’ve spent the last three weeks running a fictional e-commerce business through Wave’s full feature set. Invoicing, expense tracking, bank connections, payroll, even their new AI-powered receipt scanning. Here’s what actually happened, what costs real money, and whether you should switch.

What Wave Gets Right Out of the Box

Wave launched back in 2009 with a simple pitch: accounting software shouldn't cost $30 a month just to send an invoice. That pitch still holds. The core accounting features—unlimited invoicing, unlimited expense tracking, double-entry bookkeeping, and basic reporting—remain completely free. No trial period, no credit card required, no "premium" tier that unlocks the stuff you actually need.

That's genuinely rare. FreshBooks charges $19/month minimum. QuickBooks Self-Employed runs $15/month, and that's the stripped-down version. Xero doesn't even publish a free tier anymore. Wave's free plan isn't a loss leader—it's the actual product.

But free doesn't mean perfect. Let me walk through what I tested.

Invoicing: Where Wave Shines Brightest

If you invoice clients, Wave is arguably the best free option on the market. I created a test invoice in under two minutes. The editor is clean, with drag-and-drop fields for line items, quantities, and tax rates. You can add your logo, customize colors, and set payment terms per client.

What impressed me most was the payment tracking. Every invoice shows a clear status: Sent, Viewed, Paid, or Overdue. You can set automatic payment reminders that nudge clients at intervals you choose. For a small business owner chasing late payments, that feature alone is worth switching for.

Wave also supports recurring invoices. I set up a monthly retainer invoice for a fictional client, and the system generated it automatically on the first of the month. No manual duplication, no forgetting to bill.

The catch: Wave's built-in payment processing isn't free. You pay 2.9% + $0.60 per transaction for credit cards, or 1% for bank payments (ACH). That's roughly on par with Stripe, which is what most competitors use under the hood anyway. But if you're sending invoices and want clients to pay online, factor that cost in.

Expense Tracking and Bank Feeds

Wave connects to over 10,000 financial institutions in the US and Canada. I linked a test checking account and a test credit card, and transactions started appearing within 24 hours. The categorization engine is decent—it learned my patterns after about a week of corrections.

You can also upload receipts manually or snap photos with the mobile app. Wave's OCR (optical character recognition) reads the merchant, date, and total, then attaches the receipt to the transaction. Accuracy was solid—probably 85-90% on the first pass. The AI-powered receipt scanning is a newer addition, and it handles multi-line items reasonably well.

One thing I noticed: Wave's bank feed categorization isn't as aggressive as QuickBooks'. You'll do more manual sorting. But the interface makes corrections quick—click, drag, done.

Payroll: The Paid Add-On That's Actually Fairly Priced

Here's where Wave's "free" story gets nuanced. Payroll is a paid add-on, and it's priced per employee.

  • Payroll (US): $20/month base + $6 per active employee. So a business with 3 employees pays $38/month.
  • Payroll (Canada): $20/month base + $6 per active employee (CAD).
  • Tax services: Wave also offers automatic tax filing and payments for payroll. That's an extra $2 per employee per month in the US, or $35/month in Canada.

Is that fair? Honestly, yes. QuickBooks Payroll starts at $45/month and goes up from there. Gusto charges $49/month base. Wave's pricing undercuts both while handling direct deposit, W-2s, and 1099s.

But here's a warning: Wave's payroll is US and Canada only. If you have employees in other countries, you're out of luck.

What Wave Doesn't Do Well

I want to be honest here. Wave has limitations that might push you toward a paid alternative.

1. Inventory tracking is basic. Wave tracks inventory quantities and costs, but it doesn't handle assemblies, bundles, or multi-location inventory. If you're a product-based business with complex stock management, you'll outgrow this fast.

2. Reporting is limited. You get the essentials—Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow, Sales Tax. But you won't find deep custom reports, project profitability analysis, or budgeting tools. QuickBooks has Wave beat here significantly.

3. Customer support is slow. Wave's support team is email-only for free users. I submitted a test ticket and got a response in about 38 hours. That's not great if you're mid-bookkeeping and something breaks. Paid users get priority support, but even that's not live chat.

4. The mobile app is functional but clunky. You can capture receipts, check invoices, and view transactions. But you can't do full bookkeeping from the app. It's a companion tool, not a replacement.

5. No projects or job costing. If you run a service business that needs to track profitability per project, Wave won't do it natively. You'll need to use classes or tags, which is a workaround, not a solution.

Who Should Use Wave (And Who Shouldn't)

Wave is ideal for:

  • Freelancers and solopreneurs who need invoicing and expense tracking
  • Small service businesses (consultants, designers, writers, coaches)
  • New businesses that don't want to commit to a monthly subscription
  • Businesses with no inventory or very simple inventory needs

Wave is NOT ideal for:

  • E-commerce businesses with complex inventory (use QuickBooks or Cin7 instead)
  • Businesses with 10+ employees needing advanced HR features (look at Gusto)
  • Companies that need project profitability tracking (FreshBooks or Xero)
  • Anyone who needs phone or live chat support (this alone might disqualify Wave)

My Real-World Test Results

I ran Wave through a realistic month-end close for a fictional business:

  • 25 invoices sent, 18 paid online
  • 47 expense transactions categorized
  • 30 receipts uploaded and matched
  • Monthly sales tax report generated
  • Bank reconciliation completed (took about 15 minutes)

What went well: The invoice-to-payment flow is smooth. Clients can pay via credit card or bank transfer, and payments automatically reconcile. The sales tax report pulled everything I needed for my state filing.

What was frustrating: The bank feed occasionally lagged a day behind. And categorizing some transactions took manual effort because Wave's auto-suggestions were too broad. I also had to manually match a few receipts to transactions that the AI missed.

Overall experience: For a free tool, this is remarkably capable. It won't replace a full-time bookkeeper, but it will absolutely handle the day-to-day financial tracking for a small business.

Final Verdict: Is Wave Actually Free?

Yes. Wave's core accounting is genuinely free, with no hidden fees, no trial periods, and no forced upgrades. That's the honest answer.

But "free" comes with trade-offs. You sacrifice advanced reporting, inventory management, project tracking, and responsive support. If you're a freelancer or small service business, those trade-offs are probably acceptable. If you're running a growing e-commerce operation, they might not be.

My recommendation: Start with Wave. It costs nothing, so there's zero risk. Use it for 3-6 months. If you hit its limits, migrate to QuickBooks or FreshBooks later—the data export is straightforward.

The best tool is the one you'll actually use consistently. Wave's simplicity is its strength. And for the price, it's unbeatable.

FAQ

Is Wave really 100% free?

Yes, the core accounting features—unlimited invoicing, expense tracking, bank feeds, and basic reporting—are completely free. You only pay for optional add-ons like payroll and payment processing.

Does Wave have a free trial?

No, because the core product is already free. There's no trial period for basic features. Payroll add-ons don't have a trial either, but you can cancel anytime.

Can I use Wave for a small e-commerce business?

You can, but only if you have simple inventory. Wave tracks basic stock quantities and costs, but doesn't handle bundles, assemblies, or multi-location inventory. For complex e-commerce, consider QuickBooks or Cin7.

How does Wave Make money?

Wave monetizes through payment processing fees (2.9% + $0.60 for cards) and payroll subscriptions. The accounting software itself is a loss leader that drives users to these revenue-generating features.

Is Wave safe for my financial data?

Yes. Wave uses bank-level 256-bit SSL encryption and is backed by H&R Block. Your data is stored on secure servers, and you can export everything at any time.

Can I accept credit card payments with Wave?

Yes. Wave Payments lets you accept credit cards and bank transfers directly on invoices. Rates are 2.9% + $0.60 per card transaction, or 1% for bank transfers.

Does Wave handle sales tax?

Yes. Wave can track sales tax by jurisdiction and generate reports for filing. However, it doesn't automatically file your taxes—that's a separate step you'll handle.

What happens if I stop paying for Wave payroll?

Your accounting features stay free. You just lose access to payroll processing, direct deposit, and tax filing. Your employee records and historical payroll data remain intact.

Can I migrate from Wave to QuickBooks later?

Yes. Wave lets you export your chart of accounts, transactions, and reports as CSV files. QuickBooks has import tools that can bring this data in, though you'll need to review and clean it up.

Is Wave better than QuickBooks?

For freelancers and small service businesses, Wave is usually better because it's free and covers the essentials. For growing businesses with employees, inventory, or complex reporting needs, QuickBooks is worth the monthly cost. It depends entirely on your situation.
