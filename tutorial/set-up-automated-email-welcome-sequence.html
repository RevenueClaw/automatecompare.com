How to Set Up an Automated Email Welcome Sequence (Step by Step)

Your email list is an asset. But if you’re not sending a welcome sequence, you’re leaving money on the table. A well-crafted automated email welcome sequence can boost open rates, nurture new subscribers, and turn casual signups into loyal customers. In this tutorial, I’ll walk you through the entire process — from choosing the right tool to writing the emails and setting up the automation.

By the end, you’ll have a repeatable system that runs on autopilot. We’ll also compare three popular email marketing platforms so you can pick the one that fits your business.

Why You Need an Automated Welcome Sequence

When someone subscribes to your list, they’re giving you permission to show up in their inbox. But attention spans are short. A welcome sequence capitalizes on that initial interest by delivering value immediately. Studies show that welcome emails generate 4x more opens and 5x more clicks than standard campaigns.

A good sequence does three things:

  • Introduces your brand and sets expectations
  • Delivers a promised lead magnet (or freebie)
  • Guides the subscriber toward a next step (like a purchase or booking)

Without automation, you’d have to manually send each email — which defeats the purpose. Automation ensures every new subscriber gets the same experience, every time.

Step 1: Choose Your Email Marketing Tool

Before you can build a sequence, you need a platform that supports automation. Here are three solid options for small businesses:

Mailchimp

Mailchimp is the most well-known name in email marketing. Its free plan supports up to 500 contacts and includes basic automation (single welcome email). For multi-step sequences, you’ll need the Standard plan at $13/month.

Best for: Beginners who want a familiar interface and don’t need advanced segmentation.

Step 2: Define Your Welcome Sequence Goals

Not all welcome sequences are created equal. Ask yourself:

  • What do I want subscribers to do after reading these emails?
  • What freebie did they sign up for? (e.g., ebook, checklist, discount code)
  • How many emails should the sequence include? (3–5 is typical)

A common structure:

  • Email 1: Thank you + deliver the freebie
  • Email 2: Share your story or mission
  • Email 3: Highlight a popular product or service
  • Email 4: Social proof (testimonials, case studies)
  • Email 5: Offer a limited-time discount or call to action

Adjust based on your business. A service-based business might send a booking link in email 3. An e-commerce store might send a product carousel.

Step 3: Write the Emails

Keep each email focused on one goal. Use short paragraphs, bullet points, and a clear call to action. Avoid jargon. Write like you’re talking to one person.

Email 1: The Delivery

Subject line: “Your free [lead magnet] is ready!”

Hi [Name],

Thanks for signing up. Here’s the [lead magnet] you requested.

[Download link]

Over the next few days, I’ll share tips and resources to help you [solve a problem]. If you ever want to unsubscribe, just click the link at the bottom.

Talk soon,

[Your name]

Email 2: The Story

Subject line: “How I started [business name]”

Hi [Name],

I started [business] because [reason]. I wanted to help people [benefit]. That’s why every email I send is designed to give you value.

Here’s a quick look at what we do:

  • [Product/service 1]
  • [Product/service 2]
  • [Free resource]

Reply to this email if you have any questions. I read every response.

Best,

[Your name]

Email 3: The Offer

Subject line: “A tool I use every day”

Hi [Name],

One thing that’s helped me [achieve result] is [product/service]. It’s perfect for people who want to [benefit].

Check it out here: [link]

No pressure — just wanted to share something I believe in.

Talk soon,

[Your name]

Email 4: Social Proof

Subject line: “See what others are saying”

Hi [Name],

Don’t take my word for it. Here’s what a few customers have said:

“[Testimonial]” – [Name]

“[Testimonial]” – [Name]

Ready to get started? [Link to product/service]

[Your name]

Email 5: The Final Push

Subject line: “Last chance for 20% off”

Hi [Name],

I’m wrapping up this welcome series. As a thank-you, here’s a one-time discount: [code].

Use it at [link]. Expires in 7 days.

Thanks for reading,

[Your name]

Step 4: Build the Automation in Your Tool

I’ll use ActiveCampaign as an example because it’s the most visual, but the logic applies to any platform.

  • Create a new automation and name it “Welcome Sequence”
  • Set the trigger: “Subscriber added to list” or “Tag applied”
  • Add a wait step: 0 minutes (send immediately)
  • Add an action: Send email 1
  • Add a wait step: 2 days
  • Add an action: Send email 2
  • Repeat for each email, spacing them 2–3 days apart.
  • Add an optional condition: If subscriber clicks a link in email 3, skip remaining emails and move them to a “hot lead” tag.

In Mailchimp, you’d use the “Automation” tab and select “Welcome new subscribers” template. ConvertKit uses “Visual Automations” where you drag triggers and emails onto a canvas.

Step 5: Test and Launch

Before turning it on, send a test email to yourself. Check:

  • Links work
  • Personalization (first name) shows correctly
  • Images load
  • Mobile formatting looks fine

Then add a real subscriber (a separate email) and go through the entire sequence to catch any delays or errors.

Pros and Cons

Mailchimp

Pros:

  • Very easy to get started
  • Free plan includes basic automation
  • Large library of templates
  • Strong analytics

Cons:

  • Multi-step sequences require paid plan
  • Automation can feel limited compared to competitors
  • Pricing jumps quickly as list grows

ActiveCampaign

Pros:

  • Powerful visual automation builder
  • Built-in CRM for tracking leads
  • Conditional logic (if/then branches)
  • Excellent deliverability

Cons:

  • Learning curve, especially for beginners
  • Free plan is only a trial
  • More expensive at higher tiers

ConvertKit

Pros:

  • Clean, writer-friendly interface
  • Good for creators selling courses or memberships
  • Free plan supports up to 1,000 subscribers
  • Simple tagging and automation

Cons:

  • No built-in CRM
  • Templates are plain (you need to design emails)
  • Less advanced segmentation than ActiveCampaign

Verdict: Which Tool Should You Choose?

If you’re just starting out and want a free welcome email, Mailchimp is fine. But if you plan to scale your email marketing and need serious automation, ActiveCampaign is the winner. Its visual builder and conditional logic let you create sequences that adapt to subscriber behavior — something Mailchimp’s basic automation can’t match. For creators who prioritize simplicity and subscribers, ConvertKit is a solid middle ground, but it lacks the CRM power of ActiveCampaign.

My pick: ActiveCampaign for most small businesses. The extra effort to learn it pays off in better conversions and more efficient workflows.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How many emails should a welcome sequence have?

A: 3 to 5 emails is standard. Too few and you miss opportunities; too many and you risk annoying subscribers. Test what works for your audience.

Q: How far apart should I space the emails?

A: 2 to 3 days is a good starting point. You want to stay top-of-mind without overwhelming their inbox.

Q: Can I include a discount in the welcome sequence?

A: Yes. Many businesses offer a 10–20% discount on the final email to encourage a purchase. Just Make sure you’ve delivered value first.

Q: Do I need a separate tool for landing pages?

A: Not necessarily. Most email platforms include basic landing page builders. ActiveCampaign and Mailchimp have them; ConvertKit does too, but they’re simpler.

Q: What if someone already bought something? Should they still get the welcome sequence?

A: It depends. If they bought before subscribing, you might want to send a different sequence (e.g., a “thank you for your purchase” series) instead of the standard welcome. Use tags or segments to handle this.

Q: How do I measure success?

A: Track open rates (aim for 40–60%), click-through rates (10–20%), and conversion rate (purchases or bookings). Also Monitor unsubscribe rates — if they spike after email 2, you’re probably pushing too hard.

Q: Can I use this sequence for a free trial signup?

A: Absolutely. Just adjust the content to onboard trial users: day 1 welcome, day 2 feature highlight, day 3 case study, day 4 upgrade reminder.

Setting up an automated email welcome sequence doesn’t have to be complicated. Pick a tool, write your emails, and let the automation do the heavy lifting. Your future self — and your bank account — will thank you.
