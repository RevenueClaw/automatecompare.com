You’ve got a new subscriber. They’re excited, curious, maybe a little skeptical. That first email you send can make or break the relationship. A well-crafted automated email welcome sequence doesn’t just say “thanks for signing up” – it builds trust, sets expectations, and nudges people toward that first purchase or engagement.

I’ve set up dozens of these sequences for small businesses, freelancers, and SaaS products. Some flopped (too pushy, too long, too boring). Others turned casual signups into loyal customers. The difference? A clear strategy, the right tools, and a sequence that feels human, not robotic.

In this tutorial, I’ll walk you through building a welcome sequence from scratch – no fluff, no fake hype. You’ll learn the exact steps, the tools I actually recommend (and why), and how to avoid the common pitfalls that kill engagement.

Prerequisites

Before you start writing emails or clicking around in automation tools, get these three things in order:

  • An email marketing platform – You need software that supports automation workflows. Free plans exist but often limit you to basic sequences. I’ll compare a few options below.
  • A lead magnet or value offer – What are you giving subscribers in exchange for their email? A PDF checklist, a discount code, a free mini-course? Without this, your welcome sequence will feel hollow.
  • A clear goal – Do you want them to make a purchase, book a call, download a resource, or just stick around? Your sequence should guide them toward one primary action.

Step 1: Map Out Your Sequence Flow

Don’t open your email tool yet. Grab a piece of paper or a Google Doc and sketch the journey you want your subscriber to take.

A standard welcome sequence usually has 3–5 emails sent over 5–10 days. Here’s a common structure that works for most businesses:

  • Email 1 (Day 0): Immediate welcome + deliver the lead magnet
  • Email 2 (Day 1–2): Introduce yourself and your story (build connection)
  • Email 3 (Day 3–4): Share a valuable tip or resource (provide value)
  • Email 4 (Day 5–7): Soft pitch for your product/service (with social proof)
  • Email 5 (Day 7–10): Urgency or scarcity (if applicable) + final call to action

Pro tip: Don’t try to sell in the first email. You haven’t earned that right yet. Give them the freebie, say thanks, and wait.

Real example: When I set up a sequence for a freelance copywriter, we used:

  • Email 1: Free “Email Copy Cheat Sheet” + “I’ll send you more tips this week”
  • Email 2: My client’s story – how she left a corporate job to freelance
  • Email 3: “The biggest mistake most freelancers make” (with a case study)
  • Email 4: “Here’s how I can help you avoid that mistake” (link to services)
  • Email 5: “Last chance – limited spots open this month”

It converted at 12% – not bad for a cold list.

Step 2: Set Up Your Email Marketing Tool

Now let’s get technical. I’ll use ConvertKit as the example because it’s my go-to, but the steps are similar in most tools.

  • Create a new sequence – In ConvertKit, go to Automations > Sequences > New Sequence. Name it something like “Welcome Sequence – Main.”
  • Add a trigger – Usually “subscribes to a form” or “tag added.” If you’re using a lead magnet, create a separate form for that opt-in and set the trigger to that form.
  • Set the delay – Most sequences start with Email 1 sent immediately. Then you add a delay of 1–3 days before the next email. ConvertKit lets you set delays in hours, days, or weeks.

Alternative tool: In Moosend, you use the “Automation” tab and choose “Welcome” template. It’s drag-and-drop, so you can visually arrange emails and delays.

Common mistake: Sending all emails too fast. Space them out. Give people time to read and act.

Step 3: Write the Emails (With Templates)

You don’t need to be a copywriter. You need to be clear, helpful, and a little human. Here are templates you can adapt.

Email 1: The Welcome & Delivery

Subject: Welcome! Here’s your [lead magnet name]

Body:

Hi [First Name],

Thanks for signing up! I’m [Your Name], and I help [audience] achieve [result].

As promised, here’s your [lead magnet name]: [link]

[Optional: 1-2 sentences about what they’ll learn]

Over the next week, I’ll send you a few more tips to help you [get a specific outcome].

Talk soon,

[Your Name]

Email 2: Your Story

Subject: How I got started (and why you should care)

Body:

Hi [First Name],

I wasn’t always [doing what you do]. A few years ago, I was [struggling with problem]. Then I discovered [solution], and everything changed.

[Short story – 3-4 sentences]

That’s why I created [your business/offer]. I want to help you skip the trial and error.

Next email, I’ll share a tactic that saved me [X hours/dollars].

Talk soon,

[Your Name]

Email 3: Valuable Tip

Subject: The one thing most people get wrong about [topic]

Body:

Hi [First Name],

Most people think [common misconception]. But the truth is [correct insight].

Here’s what I recommend instead: [actionable tip with step-by-step]

Try it this week and let me know how it goes (just reply to this email).

[Your Name]

Email 4: Soft Pitch with Social Proof

Subject: How [client/customer] got [result]

Body:

Hi [First Name],

I want to share a quick success story.

[Client name] came to me with [problem]. After [X weeks/months] working together, they [specific result – e.g., doubled revenue, saved 10 hours/week].

If you’re ready to get similar results, I’d love to help. Here’s what I offer: [link to services/product]

No pressure – just know the door is open.

[Your Name]

Email 5: Final Call to Action

Subject: Last chance to [benefit]

Body:

Hi [First Name],

This is the last email in this series. I hope you’ve found the tips useful.

If you’re still on the fence about [your offer], here’s what you’ll get:

  • Benefit 1
  • Benefit 2
  • Benefit 3

[Link to offer]

If now isn’t the right time, no worries – you can always check out my blog or reply to this email with questions.

Stay awesome,

[Your Name]

Pro tip: Use merge tags like [First Name] and [lead_magnet_link] to personalize. Most tools support custom fields.

Step 4: Connect Your Email Tool to Your Signup Source

Your email sequence is useless if people never enter it. You need to connect your signup forms (on your website, landing page, or social media) to your email tool.

Option A: Native forms (easiest)

ConvertKit and Moosend have built-in form builders. Create an inline form or popup, embed it on your site, and the tool handles the rest. This is the simplest method.

Option B: Zapier (if you use a different form tool)

If you use Typeform, Google Forms, or Gravity Forms, you can connect them to your email tool via Zapier. For example:

  • Trigger: New submission in Typeform
  • Action: Add subscriber to ConvertKit sequence

Check Zapier -> – they have a free plan with 100 tasks/month, which is enough for most small lists.

Option C: Direct API (for developers)

Not necessary for 99% of small businesses. Stick with native forms or Zapier.

Testing: After connecting, sign up yourself with a test email. Make sure the first email arrives immediately, and the delay works for the second email. Check spam folder too.

Step 5: Optimize and Track Performance

You’ve launched. Now what? Don’t set it and forget it. Monitor these metrics:

  • Open rate: Aim for 30–50% for welcome sequences. Lower? Test your subject lines.
  • Click-through rate (CTR): 5–15% is good. Lower? Your call-to-action might be weak or buried.
  • Unsubscribe rate: Should be under 1% per email. Higher? You’re sending too often or not delivering value.
  • Conversion rate: How many people took your primary action (purchase, booking, etc.)? Track with UTM links or a dedicated landing page.

A/B testing: Most tools let you test subject lines or even entire email variants. I recommend testing:

  • Subject line: “Welcome to [brand]” vs. “Here’s your free guide”
  • Send time: Morning vs. afternoon
  • Offer: Discount vs. free resource

Iterate: After 30 days, look at the numbers. If Email 3 has a 2% CTR but Email 4 has 15%, maybe move the pitch earlier. If unsubscribes spike after Email 5, consider removing it or softening the ask.

Troubleshooting Common Issues

Even with careful planning, things go wrong. Here are the most common problems and how to fix them.

Problem: Emails going to spam

  • Cause: Low sender reputation, missing SPF/DKIM records, or spammy content.
  • Fix: Authenticate your domain (most email tools have guides). Avoid all-caps subject lines or too many exclamation marks. Ask subscribers to whitelist your address.

Problem: Sequence not triggering

  • Cause: Wrong trigger condition, form not connected, or subscriber already exists.
  • Fix: Double-check the automation setup. Test with a fresh email address. If using Zapier, check the task history for errors.

Problem: Low open rates after first email

  • Cause: Subject lines are boring, or you’re sending too frequently.
  • Fix: Test more curiosity-driven subject lines (e.g., “Did you see this?” instead of “Welcome to our newsletter”). Increase delay between emails.

Problem: Subscribers not clicking

  • Cause: Weak call-to-action, no clear benefit, or too many links.
  • Fix: Use one primary CTA per email. Make the button text action-oriented (“Get Your Free Template” vs. “Click Here”). Ensure the link works and leads to a relevant page.

Problem: People unsubscribe during the sequence

  • Cause: You’re selling too hard, or the content isn’t relevant.
  • Fix: Review your lead magnet – is it aligned with your offer? Add more value before the pitch. Consider a preference center so subscribers can choose topics.

Conclusion

Setting up an automated email welcome sequence isn’t rocket science, but it does require thought and testing. The steps I’ve outlined work for most small businesses, whether you’re selling courses, services, or physical products.

Quick recap:

  • Map your sequence flow – 3 to 5 emails over a week
  • Choose an email tool (I recommend ConvertKit for its balance of power and simplicity)
  • Write emails that deliver value first, pitch second
  • Connect your signup form – native or via Zapier
  • Track metrics and optimize over time

My verdict: If you’re just starting out, go with ConvertKit’s free plan – it handles up to 1,000 subscribers and includes sequences. If you’re on a tight budget, Moosend is a fantastic alternative with a generous free tier. For businesses that already use HubSpot’s CRM, stick with their workflows – but don’t buy HubSpot just for email automation unless you need the full sales suite.

The best welcome sequence is the one that gets sent. Don’t overthink it. Write, test, improve. Your subscribers will thank you.
