How to Automate Social Media Scheduling Across All Platforms

If you're running a small business, you already know the math problem. There are five or six platforms worth posting to, each wants a different format, and none of them care that you also have invoices to send and customers to call.

Most owners I talk to handle it one of two ways. Either they post daily, badly, in whatever 90 seconds they can spare — or they post in bursts, go quiet for two weeks, and wonder why the algorithm stopped showing their stuff to anyone.

There's a third option. You batch a month of content in one sitting, load it into a scheduler, and let the tool handle the timing, the cross-posting, and the recycling. That's what this guide covers. I'll walk through the actual workflow, then break down the tools that do it well, what they cost, and where each one falls apart.

What "automating social media" actually means

The word gets used loosely, so let's separate three different things:

Scheduling. You write a post now, it publishes later. Every tool in this article does this. It's table stakes.

Recycling. You write a post once and the tool republishes it on a rotating schedule — monthly, quarterly, whenever. This is how people with 40 pieces of content keep a feed alive for a year.

Routing. A trigger fires somewhere else in your business and a post goes out. New YouTube video published? Blog post goes live? New product added to Shopify? The post drafts itself or publishes directly.

Most small businesses need all three, but in that order. Scheduling first, because it's the biggest time save. Recycling second, because it's free leverage. Routing third, because it's the most fiddly and the least valuable until the first two are working.

Step 1: Build a content calendar before you touch a tool

This is the step everyone skips, and it's why their scheduling setup collapses in three weeks.

Pick three to five content pillars — recurring topics you can talk about forever. If you run a bookkeeping firm, that might be: tax deadlines, software recommendations, client mistakes, behind-the-scenes. If you sell handmade candles, it's: scent profiles, production process, customer photos, seasonal drops.

Then decide your cadence per platform. Not "post every day everywhere." That's how you burn out. A realistic starting point for a solo operator:

  • LinkedIn: 3x per week
  • Instagram: 4x per week (feed + Reels mixed)
  • Facebook: repurpose the LinkedIn posts, 3x per week
  • X: 5x per week, shorter versions
  • TikTok: 2x per week if you're doing video at all
  • Pinterest: 5x per week, mostly pins pointing at your site

That's roughly 22 posts a week, which sounds like a lot until you realize that 18 of them are repurposed from six original pieces. That's the whole trick.

Step 2: Pick a scheduler that matches your workflow

Here's where it gets opinionated. I've used all of the tools below for real client work. None of them are perfect, and the "best" one depends entirely on which platforms you actually care about.

Buffer

Check Buffer ->

Buffer's pitch is simplicity, and it delivers. You connect channels, drop posts into a queue, and it publishes them at set times. The queue model — where you define posting slots like "9am, 1pm, 5pm on weekdays" and posts fill those slots in order — is the fastest way to schedule a batch of content I've found.

Pricing: Free plan covers 3 channels with 10 queued posts per channel. Essentials runs $6 per channel per month billed monthly, or $5 per channel annually. Team is $12 per channel per month.

Pros: Cheap at small scale. The queue system genuinely saves time. Solid Instagram and LinkedIn support, including carousels and Reels. AI assistant for rewriting and repurposing posts.

Cons: Pricing scales per channel, so 8 channels on Team runs about $96/month — more than some flat-rate competitors. Analytics are shallow on the lower tiers. No content recycling, which is a real gap if you want evergreen rotation.

Later

Check Later ->

Later built its reputation on Instagram, and it still has the best visual planning experience for photo-heavy feeds. The drag-and-drop grid preview shows you exactly how your feed will look before anything goes live.

Pricing: Starter $25/month, Growth $45/month, Advanced $80/month. Pricing is per social set rather than per channel, which gets confusing when you're mixing platforms.

Pros: Best-in-class Instagram and TikTok scheduling, including Reels and carousel previews. Visual planner is worth the money if your feed aesthetic matters. Link in bio tool included. Good for creators and product brands.

Cons: LinkedIn and X support feel like an afterthought. The "social set" pricing model is awkward for anyone posting to six different networks. Video scheduling to TikTok requires a business account and approval.

SocialBee

Check Later ->

SocialBee is the recycling specialist. You organize content into categories — say, "promotional," "educational," "personal" — assign each category a percentage of your weekly slots, and SocialBee keeps the queue full automatically. Evergreen posts can be set to recycle indefinitely.

Pricing: Bootstrap $29/month (5 profiles, 1 user), Accelerate $49/month, Pro $99/month.

Pros: Content categories and evergreen recycling are the best implementation I've used. Workspaces let you manage multiple brands. Handles the "I have 60 old blog posts and no new ideas" problem better than anything else here.

Cons: Interface is busier than Buffer's and takes a real afternoon to learn. Some networks have posting quirks — X threads in particular. The recycling feature can bite you if you don't set expiry dates on time-sensitive posts.

Metricool

Check Metricool ->

Metricool is the analytics-first option that happens to schedule well. It's also the best value on this list.

Pricing: Free plan is genuinely usable (one brand, limited scheduling). Starter is around $18/month billed annually, Advanced around $45/month. Prices vary by region.

Pros: Analytics depth that costs three times as much elsewhere — competitor tracking, historical data, ad performance in the same dashboard. Free tier isn't crippled. Handles Google Business Profile, which most competitors ignore.

Cons: Scheduling interface is functional but not enjoyable. Instagram Reels support has occasionally lagged behind Later's. Support response times can be slow on the lower tiers.

Publer

Check Publer ->

Publer is the budget pick that punches above its price. It supports more networks than anything else at this price point and lets you bulk-upload via CSV, which is a niche feature that saves hours if you're managing content in a spreadsheet.

Pricing: Free plan covers 3 accounts. Professional starts around $12/month for 3 accounts, with additional accounts costing a few dollars each. Business tier around $24/month for 10 accounts.

Pros: Cheapest per-network cost on this list. Bulk CSV scheduling. Supports smaller networks like Mastodon, Bluesky, and Google Business Profile. Recycling and auto-posting included at low tiers.

Cons: Interface is cluttered and the learning curve is steeper than the price suggests. Analytics are basic. Occasional API hiccups with Instagram.

Hootsuite

Check Hootsuite ->

Hootsuite is the incumbent, and it shows — both in capability and in price.

Pricing: Professional $99/month (1 user, 10 social accounts), Team $249/month, Enterprise custom.

Pros: The most complete platform coverage. Inbox and social listening tools are mature. Team approval workflows are solid if you have staff. Integrates with a huge range of other business software.

Cons: $99/month for a single user is hard to justify for a solo operator when Metricool does 80% of it for $18. The interface has accumulated a lot of cruft over the years. Overage charges for extra accounts add up fast.

Sprout Social

Check Sprout Social ->

Sprout is enterprise territory. Standard starts at $249 per seat per month, Professional $399, Advanced $499.

Pros: Best reporting and social listening in the category. Excellent team collaboration and approval chains. Strong compliance and audit features.

Cons: Per-seat pricing means a three-person team pays $747/month minimum. Overkill for anyone reading this article, honestly. I'm including it so you know what the ceiling looks like.

Step 3: Connect accounts and set your posting slots

Once you've picked a tool, the setup is roughly the same everywhere:

  • Connect each network. Instagram and Facebook need a Business or Creator account linked to a Facebook Page — personal accounts won't work with the API. TikTok requires a business account and, for direct publishing, approval. LinkedIn works with a personal profile or a company page.
  • Define posting slots. Don't guess. Check your existing analytics for when your audience is actually online, then set three to five slots per weekday. If you have no data yet, start with 8am, 12pm, and 5pm in your audience's timezone and adjust after a month.
  • Set your timezone correctly. This sounds obvious. It's the single most common setup mistake I see, and it results in posts going out at 3am.
  • Turn on recycling for evergreen posts if your tool supports it. Set an expiry date on anything time-sensitive — promotions, event announcements
