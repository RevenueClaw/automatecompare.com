How to Automate Your Invoicing and Payment Reminders for Free

Here's the part most "free invoicing software" articles skip: the software is free, but the money movement isn't. Every tool below charges you nothing to create an invoice, and roughly 3% to actually get paid by card. That's not a trick — it's just how payment processing works, and no amount of comparison shopping changes it.

What you can do is get to zero on the subscription line. No $30/month billing app. No $16/month "reminders add-on." Just a free tool, a couple of automation rules, and a system that emails your client on day 3, day 10, and day 21 without you remembering to.

I've set this up for my own consulting work and for three small businesses since. Below is what actually works, what the free tiers really limit you to, and where each option falls apart.

The three costs of invoicing (only one of them is optional)

Before picking a tool, separate the costs:

Cost layerWhat it isCan you get it to $0?
SoftwareThe app that creates and tracks invoicesYes — several genuinely free options
Payment processingCard/ACH fees when a client paysNo — 1% to 3.5% is the floor
Your timeChasing, reconciling, re-sendingYes, mostly — this is what automation fixes

Most small businesses spend their energy optimizing the first row and ignore the third. The third is the expensive one. If you're spending four hours a month sending reminders and matching payments to invoices, that's the line item worth killing.

The five steps you're automating

Any invoicing workflow, however fancy, breaks down into the same five steps. Automation just means each step fires without you.

  • Create the invoice — from a template, a time entry, or a project milestone.
  • Deliver it — email with a payment link, ideally the moment the work is done.
  • Remind — the follow-ups, sent on a schedule rather than on vibes.
  • Collect — card, bank transfer, or ACH, landing in your account.
  • Reconcile — matching the payment to the invoice so your books aren't a mess in April.

Step 3 is where free tools differ most, and where most people give up and pay for software. Good news: the free tiers handle it better than you'd expect.

The tools worth your time

Wave — best free option if you also need bookkeeping

Check Wave ->

Wave's pitch is simple: unlimited invoices, unlimited customers, unlimited estimates, plus real double-entry accounting, at no cost. It makes money on payment processing (2.9% + $0.60 for cards, 1% for bank payments) and on Wave Pro, a $16/month upgrade that adds things like custom reports and multi-currency.

For automation, you get recurring invoices you can schedule weekly, monthly, or on custom intervals, and automatic payment reminders that you can toggle on per invoice. You set the cadence — a nudge before the due date, one on the due date, one a week after — and Wave sends them under your name.

Where it stumbles: support on the free plan is email and help-center only, so if something breaks mid-month you're waiting. Bank payment connections are strongest in the US and Canada, and the 1% bank fee applies to those transfers. Also worth noting — Wave is owned by H&R Block, and the product has drifted toward tax-prep integrations over the years. Still the best free combination of invoicing plus books.

Pros: Free accounting included, clean reminder setup, unlimited invoices, mobile app

Cons: Limited free support, 1% fee on bank payments, US/Canada-centric for payouts

Zoho Invoice — the most automation per dollar (which is zero)

Check Zoho Invoice ->

If reminders are your priority, start here. Zoho Invoice is free forever — unlimited invoices, up to 500 customers, one user — and the reminder engine is the most configurable thing in this price bracket. You can build multiple reminder schedules, escalate tone across them, set different rules per customer, and have invoices auto-charge a saved card when the due date hits.

It also does recurring invoices, retainer billing, time tracking that converts to invoices, a client portal where customers can view and pay their own bills, and multi-currency. Payment gateway fees are whatever your gateway charges (Stripe, PayPal, etc.) — Zoho doesn't take a cut.

The catch is the Zoho ecosystem. Every screen has a nudge toward Zoho Books, Zoho CRM, or Zoho One, and the settings are dense enough that your first hour is a scavenger hunt. There's no built-in accounting either — Zoho Invoice tracks invoices, not your P&L. If you need books, that's Zoho Books (paid) or a separate tool.

Pros: Free forever, deepest reminder automation at $0, client portal, multi-currency, time-to-invoice

Cons: Upsell-heavy, dense settings, no accounting, single user on free plan
