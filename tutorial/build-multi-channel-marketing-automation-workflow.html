How to Build a Multi-Channel Marketing Automation Workflow

Most small businesses don't have a multi-channel marketing problem. They have a "three tools that don't talk to each other" problem.

You've got a form on your site. An email platform. Maybe a text message tool someone set up last spring. And a spreadsheet where you try to keep track of who's where. Every channel works fine on its own. Together, they're a mess — and the person who gets caught in the middle is your customer, who gets a welcome email on Tuesday, a text on Thursday asking if they got the welcome email, and a retargeting ad on Friday for a product they already bought.

This tutorial walks through building one connected workflow: a single trigger that fans out to email, SMS, ad audiences, and your internal team, with one source of truth underneath. I'll use a running example — a 4-person coffee subscription business called Ridgeline Roasters — but the structure works for a course creator, a consultant, or a local service business.

I'll also be honest about where this gets expensive and where it gets fragile. Multi-channel automation is not free, and it's not set-and-forget. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

First, Decide What "Multi-Channel" Actually Means for You

The temptation is to turn on everything at once. Don't. Pick two channels to start, get them working, then add the third.

Here's the order I'd recommend for most small businesses:

  • Email — always first. It's the cheapest per message, the easiest to measure, and the least regulated.
  • Ad audience sync — second. It's passive. You're not sending anything; you're just telling Meta or Google "here's my list, go find people like them."
  • SMS — third, and only for high-intent moments. Cart abandonment, appointment reminders, a shipping delay. Not a weekly newsletter.

The reason SMS goes last is consent. Texting people who didn't explicitly opt in for texts is a legal problem in the US (TCPA) and a bigger one in the EU. If your signup form says "email me," that is not permission to text. You need a separate checkbox, and you need to keep a record of when and how they checked it. Build that into your form from day one, because retrofitting consent is a nightmare.

The Stack I'd Build (and What It Costs)

There are two ways to do this. You can buy one all-in-one platform that handles email, SMS, and ads, or you can buy a specialized email tool and glue it to everything else with an automation platform.

For Ridgeline, I'd go with the glue approach — but only because they need the ad sync. If you just want email and SMS, an all-in-one is cheaper and less work.

Here's the stack:

  • Hub: ActiveCampaign (email + SMS + CRM in one place)
  • Glue: Make (formerly Integromat)
  • Forms: Tally (free tier is generous)
  • Ads: Meta Custom Audiences via Make
  • Internal alerts: Slack + a Google Sheet

Step-by-Step: Building the Workflow

Step 1: Pick Your Hub Before You Pick Your Tools

The hub is the system that owns contact records. Every other tool should read from it or write to it — never both, or you'll get duplicate contacts and conflicting tags.

For most small businesses, the hub is either an email platform with a CRM attached (ActiveCampaign, Klaviyo, Brevo) or a standalone CRM (HubSpot's free tier, Pipedrive). Pick one and commit. The single biggest cause of broken marketing workflows is two systems both claiming to be the source of truth.

Ridgeline picks ActiveCampaign. It handles email, has SMS built in (so no separate Twilio account), and the contact record holds tags, custom fields, and a lead score.

Step 2: Build the Data Layer

Before you touch automation, decide what data you need on every contact:

  • Source — which form, which page, which ad
  • Consent flags — email_opt_in (true/false), sms_opt_in (true/false), plus timestamps
  • Segment tags — lead, customer, wholesale, workshop-attendee
  • Engagement signal — did they open the first email? Click the second?
