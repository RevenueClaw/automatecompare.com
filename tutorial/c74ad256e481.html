You’ve got leads piling up in your CRM. You’re sending emails manually. Your follow-up is inconsistent, and leads go cold faster than a cup of coffee at a 9 AM meeting.

I’ve been there. Running a small business means juggling a dozen tools, and the gap between your CRM and email marketing is one of the biggest leaks in your sales pipeline. Connecting them isn’t just a nice-to-have—it’s how you turn strangers into customers without burning out.

In this tutorial, I’ll walk you through five ways to connect your CRM to your email marketing platform. We’ll cover the most common setups (native integrations, Zapier, custom API), the tools I actually recommend (with honest pros and cons), and the exact steps to get lead nurturing running on autopilot.

By the end, you’ll have a working system that sends the right email at the right time based on what your leads actually do. No fluff, no magic—just real workflows that work.

Prerequisites

Before we start plugging things together, Make sure you have:

  • A CRM that stores your contacts and their activity. I’ll use Hubspot (free tier works) and Pipedrive as examples.
  • An email marketing platform that can receive data and trigger automations. I’ll use Convertkit and Moosend (both affordable for small businesses).
  • A Zapier account (free plan gives 100 tasks/month) if you need a bridge between tools that don’t natively integrate.
  • Basic understanding of your lead stages (e.g., new lead, contacted, qualified, converted). You don’t need a full sales script, but know what “nurturing” means for your business.

If you don’t have a CRM yet, start with HubSpot’s free CRM. It’s generous, and you can upgrade later. For email, ConvertKit is my go-to for creators, but Moosend is cheaper for high-volume lists.

Step 1: Map Your Lead Nurturing Workflow

You can’t automate what you haven’t defined. Sit down and sketch out the journey a lead takes from first touch to sale.

Here’s a typical small-business workflow:

  • Lead enters CRM (via web form, manual entry, or imported list).
  • Tag assigned based on source or interest (e.g., “ebook download”, “free trial”).
  • First email sent within 24 hours (welcome series).
  • Behavior triggers: lead opens email → send case study. Lead clicks link → send demo invite. Lead doesn’t open → send re-engagement email after 5 days.
  • Lead reaches a score threshold → notify sales team.

Write this down. It doesn’t have to be fancy. A piece of paper or a Google Doc works. The key is knowing which events in your CRM should trigger which emails.

Pro tip: Don’t try to automate everything at once. Start with one nurture sequence—like a 3-email welcome series—and expand once that’s stable.

  • HubSpot CRM + HubSpot Marketing Hub (native) – simplest, but can get pricey.

Step 3: Native Integration – HubSpot CRM to HubSpot Email

If you’re already in the HubSpot ecosystem, this is the easiest path. HubSpot’s free CRM can trigger emails from the paid Marketing Hub (starts at $50/month). But you can also use the free email tool with limitations.

What you need:

  • HubSpot CRM (free)
  • HubSpot Marketing Hub (free or paid)

How to set it up:

  • Create a contact property to track lead stage. In HubSpot, go to Contacts → Properties → Create property. Call it “Lead Stage” with options like New, Nurturing, Qualified, Customer.
  • Build a list of contacts at a specific stage. Go to Contacts → Lists → Create list. Use filters like “Lead Stage is New” and “Last activity date is within 7 days.”
  • Create an email campaign in Marketing Hub. Go to Marketing → Email → Create email. Choose a template or start from scratch.
  • Set up an automation (workflow). Go to Automation → Workflows → Create workflow. Choose “Contact-based” → “List membership” → select your list. Then add an action: “Send email” and choose your campaign.
  • Add conditional branches (if you’re on paid plan). For example: If contact opens email → move to “Nurturing” stage and send a follow-up. If they don’t open → wait 3 days, then send a different email.

Pros:

  • Zero third-party tools needed.
  • Real-time sync: when a lead’s stage changes in CRM, they automatically move in/out of email sequences.
  • HubSpot’s reporting is excellent.

Cons:

  • Paid tiers get expensive fast. Marketing Hub Professional is $800/month.
  • Limited to HubSpot’s email builder (good, but not as flexible as ConvertKit’s tagging).

Verdict on native: Best if you plan to use HubSpot for everything and have budget. For most small businesses with under 1,000 contacts, the free tier (with basic one-off emails) is too limiting for true nurturing.

Step 4: Zapier Integration – Pipedrive to ConvertKit

This is the setup I use for my own business. Pipedrive handles deals and pipeline, ConvertKit handles email sequences. Zapier bridges them.

What you need:

  • Pipedrive account (free trial, then $12.50/user/month)
  • ConvertKit account (free up to 300 subscribers, then $9/month)
  • Zapier account (free plan gives 100 tasks/month)

How to set it up:

Step 4.1: Create a lead stage in Pipedrive

  • Go to Pipelines → Edit stages. Add stages like “New Lead”, “Nurturing”, “Won”, “Lost”.

Step 4.2: Create a nurture sequence in ConvertKit

  • In ConvertKit, go to Automations → New Automation.
  • Choose “Start from scratch” or use a template. I’ll build a simple 3-email sequence:

- Email 1: Welcome + value (sent immediately)

- Email 2: Case study (sent 2 days later)

- Email 3: Soft pitch for a call (sent 5 days later)

  • Tag the subscriber when they join the sequence (e.g., “Nurture Sequence Active”).

Step 4.3: Set up the Zapier connection

  • Log into Zapier and click “Create Zap”.
  • Trigger app: Pipedrive. Event: “Updated Deal” (or “New Deal” if you want to start when a deal is created).
  • Test trigger: Choose a deal from your Pipedrive to pull sample data.
  • Filter step (optional but recommended): Add a Filter by Zapier step. Condition: “Deal Stage” exactly matches “New Lead”. This ensures only deals in that stage trigger the email sequence.
  • Action app: ConvertKit. Event: “Subscribe to Form” or “Add Tag”. I prefer “Add Tag” because it’s more flexible.
  • Map fields: Map the contact’s email from Pipedrive to ConvertKit. You can also pass their name and any custom fields.
  • Choose tag: Select the “Nurture Sequence Active” tag you created earlier.
  • Test & publish.

Now, whenever you move a deal to “New Lead” in Pipedrive, Zapier automatically adds that person to your ConvertKit nurture sequence.

Step 4.4: Add a second Zap for behavior tracking

To make nurturing smarter, create a second Zap:

  • Trigger: ConvertKit “Subscriber Tags Added” (e.g., when they click a link in email, you can add a tag via ConvertKit’s link triggers).
  • Action: Pipedrive “Update Deal” – change stage to “Nurturing” or add a note.

This closes the loop: CRM → email → CRM.

Pros:

  • Very affordable. You can run this for under $30/month total.
  • Flexible. You can connect any CRM to any email tool.
  • ConvertKit’s tagging system is perfect for lead scoring.

Cons:

  • Zapier tasks add up. 100 tasks/month is enough for 3-4 deals per day. More volume needs a paid plan ($20/month for 750 tasks).
  • Slight delay (a few seconds) between trigger and action – fine for most cases.

Step 5: Advanced – Webhook-Based Integration (No-Code, but Powerful)

If you want real-time sync without Zapier’s monthly task limits, use webhooks. Most CRMs (like HubSpot) can send webhook notifications when a deal changes. Most email tools (like Moosend) can receive webhooks to trigger automations.

I’ll show you a simplified version using Make.com (formerly Integromat) – it’s cheaper than Zapier for high volume.

  • Create a scenario in Make.
  • Trigger: Pipedrive “Watch Deals” module.
  • Filter: only deals moved to “Nurturing” stage.
  • Action: Moosend “Add New Subscriber” to a specific mailing list.
  • Add a second action: Update Pipedrive deal with a custom field like “Email Sequence Started: Yes”.

Make.com’s free plan gives 1,000 operations/month. That’s 10x Zapier’s free tier.

Pros: Cheaper for volume, faster.

Cons: Steeper learning curve. You need to understand JSON mapping.

Troubleshooting Common Issues

Even with the best setup, things break. Here’s what I’ve run into and how to fix them.

1. Leads not being added to email sequence

  • Check Zapier task history. Did the task fail? Common cause: the contact’s email is missing or invalid in CRM.
  • Ensure your filter step matches exactly. “New Lead” vs “New lead” (case-sensitive) can break things.

2. Duplicate emails

  • In ConvertKit, enable “Prevent duplicate subscriptions” in your automation settings.
  • In HubSpot, use suppression lists for contacts who already received the sequence.

3. Delay in sync

  • Zapier polls every 15 minutes on free plan. Upgrade to paid for 2-minute checks.
  • Webhooks (Step 5) are instant.

4. Lead moved to wrong stage

  • Double-check your automation triggers. If you have multiple workflows, they can conflict. I once had a Zap that moved a lead back to “New” every time they opened an email. Chaos.

5. Email deliverability drops

  • When you sync lots of old leads from CRM to email, you might trigger spam filters. Warm up your sending domain first. Send to active subscribers only.

Conclusion: Which Setup Should You Choose?

Let’s cut through the options.

If you’re a solopreneur or freelancer with a small list (under 1,000) and want the simplest path: Use HubSpot CRM + HubSpot’s free email tool for basic one-off sends, but upgrade to ConvertKit for sequences. Connect them with Zapier (Step 4). It’s $9/month for ConvertKit + $20/month for Zapier = $29/month. That’s a steal.

If you’re a growing team with a larger sales pipeline: Pipedrive + ConvertKit + Zapier is the most flexible. You can scale to thousands of leads without breaking the bank. Skip HubSpot’s paid marketing hub unless you need its advanced reporting.

If you’re technical and want to avoid monthly fees: Use Make.com with webhooks (Step 5). You get 1,000 free operations/month, which can handle 30-50 leads per day.

My personal verdict: For 90% of small businesses, Pipedrive + ConvertKit + Zapier is the sweet spot. It’s affordable, easy to set up, and gives you full control over your nurture sequences. ConvertKit’s tagging is superior for behavioral automation, and Pipedrive’s pipeline management keeps your sales team happy.

Start with the free trials. Connect one workflow. See how it feels. Once you taste the power of automated lead nurturing—where leads get the right message without you lifting a finger—you’ll never go back.
